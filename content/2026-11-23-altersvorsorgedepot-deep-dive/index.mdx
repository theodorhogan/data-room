## Bottom line: better, not bigger

Germany has replaced a pension product that failed savers with one that should serve them well. It has not created a large new pool of capital. Measured against a world in which the Riester pension simply kept shrinking, the Altersvorsorgedepot moves money that already existed into a cheaper, equity-heavy wrapper, at a higher cost to the budget than the government has planned for and at the expense of providers' margins.

- **What changes.** From 1 January 2027 new subsidised private pension contracts no longer need a capital guarantee. The state adds 50 cents to each euro saved up to EUR 360 a year and 25 cents up to EUR 1,800, at most EUR 540, plus up to EUR 300 per child; the self-employed become eligible and the default product may cost at most 1.0% a year <Cite id="bundestag-hib-kurzmeldung-reform-2026" /> <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4996" />.
- **Take-up: about six million savers.** In our Base scenario 5.9m people pay into an Altersvorsorgedepot in 2030E (Low 2.2m, High 10.1m). Riester had 9.4m subsidised savers in 2023 and 11.1m at its peak in 2015 <Cite id="bmf-statistische-auswertungen-riester-2026" />.
- **Half the inflow is re-labelled.** Depots take in EUR 9.1bn in 2030E, which is EUR 7.4bn more than subsidised products would have received without the reform. 50% of the inflow is money that would have gone into Riester contracts or unsubsidised fund savings plans anyway. New household saving is EUR 0.9bn, or 0.03 percentage points of the saving rate; it is EUR 0.3bn to EUR 1.5bn across our range for the share of contributions that is new.
- **Published forecasts ask too much.** Estimates of EUR 26bn to EUR 56bn a year would need 11m to 24m people each paying in the full subsidised EUR 1,800, or, with our Base number of savers, EUR 4,400 to EUR 9,500 per saver a year against a legal limit of EUR 6,840 per contract <Cite id="ipe-providers-position-eur" /> <Cite id="bmf-faq-reform" />. The government's planning points the other way. Its draft fiscal table assumed that the reform merely stops the decline of Riester subsidies; the table as amended by Parliament, which we have from a single search extract, shows EUR 0.88bn of extra cost in 2030 <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4088" /> <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4996" />. Our Base scenario costs EUR 3.3bn more than no reform.
- **Savers are the winners.** The same EUR 100 a month for 35 years builds about EUR 162,000 in a low-cost depot against EUR 69,000 in a Riester contract, 2.3 times as much on our return assumptions. The largest part of the gain, 46% to 63%, comes from holding more equities, which is risk taken and not cost saved; lower costs supply 25% to 42% and the higher allowance the rest.
- **Providers get volume without margin.** Standard products have been announced at 0% to about 0.5% a year, against 1.46% for the average Riester offer on sale; one large fund house has not published its price <Cite id="traderepublic-altersvorsorge-press-release-2026" /> <Cite id="warentest-trade-republic-scalable" /> <Cite id="finanzwende-riester-rueruprenten-leider-2026" />. In our Base scenario provider revenue is about EUR 0.2bn below the no-reform path in 2030E and recovers only in the late 2030s. The sign of that result depends on what Riester capital really earns its providers.
- **The macro effect is small.** The gross inflow is 0.2% of gross domestic product (GDP), and about 2 cents of each euro invested reach German companies. Depot assets reach EUR 134bn by 2035E, but subsidised pension assets are only EUR 93bn higher than without the reform, 2.0% of GDP.
- **The larger pool lies elsewhere.** The pension commission's proposed Kapitalrente, a mandatory funded contribution of 2% of wages inside the statutory pension, would move about EUR 35bn a year, 3.6 times our Base depot inflow <Cite id="bundesregierung-empfehlungen-alterssicherun-beri" /> <Cite id="ifo-gesetzliche-kapitalrente-potenzial-2026" />. It is a proposal, not law.

## A match replaces the guarantee

The Altersvorsorgereformgesetz keeps the principle of Riester, a state allowance on top of private retirement saving, and removes the feature that made Riester expensive: the obligation to guarantee every euro paid in. In its place the law offers a proportional match, a wider circle of eligible people and a cost-capped default product.

### Five months from draft to law

The cabinet adopted the draft on 17 December 2025, the Bundestag passed it on 27 March 2026 with the votes of the governing CDU/CSU and SPD, the Bundesrat consented on 8 May, and the law was promulgated on 29 May 2026 <Cite id="bmf-private-altersvorsorge-alle-2025" /> <Cite id="bundestag-beschliesst-altersvorsorge-textarchiv" /> <Cite id="bundestag-br-drs-206-2026" /> <Cite id="bgbl-2026-156" />. Products may be sold from 1 January 2027; new Riester contracts end on 31 December 2026 <Cite id="drv-reform-privaten-altersvorsorge-2026" /> <Cite id="bmf-faq-reform" />.

Parliament changed the bill more than is usual. The government draft had offered 30 cents per euro up to EUR 1,200 and 20 cents above that, a maximum of EUR 480, and a cost cap of 1.5% <Cite id="bmf-private-altersvorsorge-alle-2025" /> <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4996" />. The Finance Committee raised the allowance, admitted the self-employed, cut the cost cap to 1.0%, kept the product information office and added an authorisation for a state-run standard product <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4996" /> <Cite id="bundestag-beschliesst-altersvorsorge-textarchiv" />.

### What changes against Riester

Riester contracts had to guarantee contributions and allowances at retirement. With interest rates low, providers met that promise by holding bonds, which capped the share of equities and the return <Cite id="ivfp-renditechancen-fondsgebundene-riester-2018" />. A working paper for the German Council of Economic Experts puts the cost of the guarantee for a 40-year contract at about EUR 20,000 for a 2001 start and EUR 140,000 for a 2015 start <Cite id="svr-15-jahre-riester-2016" />.

The new regime has three product types. The Altersvorsorgedepot has no guarantee and may hold funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) up to risk class 5, but no single shares <Cite id="bmf-faq-reform" /> <Cite id="lrfinanzkonzepte-welche-anlagen-sind" />. The Standarddepot is a simplified version with two funds chosen by the provider, default de-risking before retirement and the 1.0% cap on effective costs; every provider must offer one <Cite id="bundestag-antwort-bundesregierun-kleine-2026" /> <Cite id="bmf-faq-reform" />. Guarantee products remain as an option, at 80% or 100% of contributions according to one guide <Cite id="allianz-altersvorsorge-aktualisiert-28-2026" />.

Savers may pay in up to EUR 6,840 a year. Only the first EUR 1,800 earn an allowance or a tax deduction; returns on the rest are untaxed during the saving phase <Cite id="bmf-faq-reform" />. At most two new contracts per person count as certified <Cite id="deutscherentenve-82-estg-altersvorsorge-2027" />.

Existing Riester savers have three choices. They can do nothing and keep the old allowance, opt into the new allowance within the old contract by a declaration to the provider, or move the capital into a new contract without repaying past subsidies <Cite id="netztrends-altersvorsorge-statt-riester" /> <Cite id="bmf-faq-reform" />. A move costs at most EUR 150 at the old provider and is free after 5 years; the guarantee on the transferred capital lapses <Cite id="avdepot-altersvorsorge-anbieter-wechseln" /> <Cite id="vorsorgedepotlot-was-kostet-wechsel" /> <Cite id="debeka-altersvorsorge-riester-kunden" />.

### The match is steep at the bottom

The allowance is now a function of what the saver pays in. A saver without children receives EUR 180 on EUR 360 of contributions and EUR 540 on EUR 1,800, a match rate that falls from 50% to 30%. A parent of two receives EUR 780 on the same EUR 360, because each child adds 100% of contributions up to EUR 300 <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4996" /> <Cite id="vr-kinderzulage-altersvorsorge-sollten-2026" />. Savers under 25 receive a one-off EUR 200, and the minimum own contribution is EUR 120 a year <Cite id="bmf-faq-reform" />.

Riester worked the other way round: a fixed allowance for anyone who saved 4% of the previous year's income, allowances included, with a floor of EUR 60 a year <Cite id="deutscherentenve-staatliche-foerderung-sie-2026" /> <Cite id="gesetzeiminterne-einkommensteue-estg-86-2026" />. That favoured low earners with children, who could collect the full allowance for a small contribution. One consumer portal calculates that a single parent can receive up to EUR 475 a year less under the new rules <Cite id="biallo-altersvorsorge-reform-2027-2026" />. No official source gives the figure, but our own arithmetic reproduces it for a parent of two who pays only the minimum contribution: EUR 775 under Riester against EUR 300 under the new schedule. For most other savers the new schedule pays more: the average Riester saver's contribution of EUR 888 would earn a basic allowance of EUR 312, against an average of EUR 131 actually received in 2022 <Cite id="bmf-statistische-auswertungen-riester-2026" />.

The Altersvorsorgedepot is a third-pillar reform. It leaves the pay-as-you-go statutory pension untouched; that system is being changed on separate tracks, one of which matters more for capital markets than the depot itself.

## Baseline: Riester was running off

To judge the reform we need the world without it. In that world Riester stays open, and its numbers keep falling as they have since 2017. This baseline is the reference for every increment in this report.

### Contracts peaked in 2017

Riester contracts peaked at 16.6m at the end of 2017 and stood at 14.66m at the end of 2025, a fall of 1.94m or 11.7% <Cite id="bmas-entwicklung-riester-vertraege-2026" /> <Cite id="bmas-entwicklung-riester-vertraege-2026-2" />. Insurance policies make up 9.75m of the total, fund plans 3.00m, home-ownership contracts 1.44m and bank savings plans 0.49m <Cite id="bmas-entwicklung-riester-vertraege-2026-2" />. The Labour Ministry estimates that between a fifth and a quarter of contracts are dormant <Cite id="bmas-statistik-privaten-altersvorsorge-2026" />.

### Costly products, thin returns

Riester's weakness was cost. An advocacy group's survey of 18 current offers found average effective costs of 1.46% a year, and Stiftung Warentest measured 1.42% for the largest fund product in 2017 <Cite id="finanzwende-riester-rueruprenten-leider-2026" /> <Cite id="warentest-riester-rente-ueberblick-2017" />. No official figure exists for realised returns or for total Riester capital <Cite id="bundestag-antwort-bundesregierun-kleine-2024" />. For returns we apply the market returns we assume for the depot to Riester's lower equity share and higher cost, which gives 2.0% a year. A study for a fund house projects contract returns of 1.8% to 2.5%; we could not re-verify those figures or their definition and use them only as a cross-check <Cite id="unioninvestment-rendite-rentenhoehe-riester-2024" />. For capital, four third-party estimates, each from a single secondary source, range from EUR 220bn to EUR 255bn; we use their median, EUR 232bn <Cite id="fundresearch-wie-altersvorsorge-eltif-2026" /> <Cite id="versicherungsbote-riester-rente-lebensversiche-2" /> <Cite id="boersenzeitung-scalable-capital-lockt-2026" /> <Cite id="pfefferminzia-versicherern-droht-milliardenabfl" />. The fund industry alone reports EUR 50bn in Riester fund contracts <Cite id="bvi-jahrbuch-2026-daten-2026" />.

Providers had been leaving before the reform. Life insurers wrote only 30.4 thousand new Riester policies in 2024, before a last rush to about 0.1m in 2025, and DWS closed its Riester products to new business in 2021, according to a press report <Cite id="gdv-deutsche-lebensversiche-zahlen-2025" /> <Cite id="gdv-zahl-neu-abgeschlossene" /> <Cite id="bca-faq-riesterausstie-berater-2021" />.

## Take-up: six million, not twenty

How many people open a depot decides everything else. Our Base scenario has 5.9m depot savers in 2030E and 6.7m in 2035E. That is well below what the industry's inflow forecasts imply, and it rests on what voluntary schemes have achieved elsewhere and on what Germans say they will do.

### Voluntary schemes stall early

Schemes that enrol people automatically reach most of them. In the United Kingdom 55% of eligible employees were in a workplace pension in 2012, when automatic enrolment began, and 89% in 2024; 10% of the newly enrolled opted out in August 2022 <Cite id="govuk-workplace-pension-participation" /> <Cite id="govuk-workplace-pension-participation-3" /> <Cite id="govuk-ten-years-automatic" />. Poland's employee capital plans, also opt-out, reached about 62% participation in July 2026 according to one press report <Cite id="wbj-participation-ppk-increases" />.

Schemes that wait for people to sign up reach far fewer. 17% of US households paid into an individual retirement account (IRA) in tax year 2024 <Cite id="ici-role-iras-us-2026" />. In a US field experiment a 50% match raised take-up from 3% to 14% <Cite id="nber-saving-incentives-low-2005" />. Riester itself, after two decades of commission-driven selling, had 26% of employees paying in during 2023 <Cite id="bmas-verbreitung-altersvorsorge-2023-2025" />. The EU's pan-European pension product, also voluntary and with a 1% cost cap, had attracted a single provider by March 2024, by one press count <Cite id="euronews-hype-over-hit-2024" /> <Cite id="eiopa-staff-paper-future-2024" />.

The Altersvorsorgedepot is opt-in. The Council of Economic Experts had proposed the opposite, automatic enrolment into a state-managed default fund, on the grounds that opt-in schemes reach low-income households poorly <Cite id="svr-jahresgutachte-2025-26-2025" />. Japan shows that a deposit-heavy country can move quickly when an account is simple: its tax-free investment account drew purchases of 17.4 trillion yen in 2024, its first year in the new form <Cite id="diamond-nisa-2024-17" />. But that account has no lock-in until retirement, so it is a weak guide to a pension product.

### Three groups, three behaviours

We split the roughly 40m eligible people by what they did before the reform. The government publishes no count of the eligible; ours is the sum of employees subject to social insurance, civil servants, soldiers and the self-employed <Cite id="bundestag-antwort-bundesregierun-kleine-2026" /> <Cite id="ba-monatsbericht-arbeits-ausbildungsmar-2026" /> <Cite id="destatis-beschaeftigte-oeffentlichen-dienstes-20" /> <Cite id="destatis-eckzahlen-arbeitsmarkt-deutschland-2026" />. One fund house works with about 45 million <Cite id="dasinvestment-avd-wie-sich" />. The difference would fall into our third group and add about 0.3m savers to the Base scenario in 2030E.

- **Active Riester savers, 8.4m.** They already save and already receive an allowance. We assume 30% redirect their contributions into a depot over time (Low 15%, High 50%). In a September 2026 survey 20% of Riester holders said they intended to switch <Cite id="pfefferminzia-jeder-fuenfte-riester-2026" />. Our Base is above that stated intent: the survey predates most price announcements, our figure is a long-run share, and one broker pays the old provider's switching fee <Cite id="scalable-viele-riester-sparer" />.
- **Investors without Riester, 7.2m.** They hold funds or shares and are the brokers' target. We assume 35% take-up and EUR 1,500 a year. That is less than the EUR 2,170 of the average German ETF savings plan and the EUR 1,704 that intending savers state in a survey, because only the first EUR 1,800 is subsidised, the money is locked until 65 and stated amounts usually exceed actual ones <Cite id="extraetf-etf-retail-marktreport-2026" /> <Cite id="cashonline-altersvorsorge-depot-studie-2026" />. With the ETF-plan average instead, the Base inflow in 2030E would be EUR 10.8bn, not EUR 9.1bn.
- **Everyone else, 24.7m.** They neither hold securities nor pay into Riester. We assume 7% take-up and EUR 600 a year. In the 2026 savings banks survey 21% of respondents said they do not or cannot save <Cite id="sparkasse-vermoegensbaro-2026-2026" />.

### Surveys point the same way

For investors and non-savers, stated intentions are higher than our assumptions, as they usually are. In a bank-commissioned poll 38% of adults aged 18 to 55 called it likely that they would open a depot; 16% said very likely <Cite id="finanznachrichte-trotz-staatlicher-zulagen-2026" />. In another, 52% of eligible people could imagine it, but only 8% felt well informed <Cite id="procontraonline-181-euro-monat-2026" />. Intent is fading as the launch nears: in a market researcher's survey, as reported by one trade publication, the share of those aware of the product who call a contract certain or likely fell from 34% in April to 29% in September 2026 <Cite id="cashonline-altersvorsorge-2027-prognose-2026" />.

The same researchers publish the one survey-based forecast we found. Its conservative case is 2.9m contracts in 2027, cut from 4.5m four months earlier; one report of the study gives a range up to 6.3m <Cite id="cashonline-altersvorsorge-2027-prognose-2026" /> <Cite id="dasinvestment-altersvorsorge-depot-sirius-2026" />. At a stated EUR 142 a month per contract the conservative case comes to EUR 6bn of contributions including allowances <Cite id="cashonline-altersvorsorge-depot-studie-2026" />. Our Base scenario has 2.7m savers and EUR 4.2bn in 2027E: about the same number of contracts and 29% less money, because we assume that savers pay in EUR 1,080 a year on average, not the EUR 1,704 they state. The agreement on contracts is not independent support: we set the first-year share of our take-up path with this forecast in view. A market-research institute warns of large deadweight effects because the product appeals mainly to higher earners and to people who save anyway <Cite id="nim-reform-privaten-altersvorsorge-2026" />.

## Half the inflow is re-labelled

Providers will report gross inflows. The economy cares about what is new. In our Base scenario EUR 9.1bn flows into depots in 2030E, but only EUR 0.9bn of it is saving that would not otherwise have happened.

### Where each euro comes from

We trace each euro of inflow to its origin. Of the EUR 9.1bn in 2030E:

- EUR 2.2bn is Riester money: contributions and allowances that switching savers would have paid into their old contracts.
- EUR 2.4bn is securities saving that investors redirect from unsubsidised fund and ETF plans.
- EUR 1.4bn comes out of bank deposits. It is new to capital markets but not new saving.
- EUR 2.3bn is additional allowances paid into depots by the state.
- EUR 0.9bn is new household saving, 14% of savers' own contributions.

One modelling choice sits inside the Riester line. Former Riester savers are assumed to keep paying once they have switched, while Riester savers in the no-reform world keep leaving at 3.6% a year. Of the EUR 0.5bn that this group pays in beyond what it would have paid into Riester, EUR 0.3bn is such avoided lapses and EUR 0.2bn is higher contributions per head. The Low scenario removes the effect.

### Four ways to count new money

Which number is right depends on the question. An asset manager counting client money sees EUR 9.1bn. Compared with a world without the reform, subsidised products receive EUR 7.4bn more. Net new money in subsidised products is EUR 4.9bn: new saving, money out of deposits and all additional allowances, including EUR 0.4bn for Riester savers who stay in their contracts and opt into the new schedule. Extra demand for equities from the annual flows is EUR 4.7bn, because depots hold far more equity than the Riester contracts they replace; Riester capital that is moved into depots adds a further EUR 2.3bn in 2030E, a one-off shift worth EUR 15bn over 2027E to 2031E. Our Base new-saving shares sit inside the range of the international literature; our Low values follow the more sceptical German evidence.

Two findings shape our assumptions. First, the people who respond to subsidies are wealthier and mostly shift existing assets; in Denmark each unit of subsidy raised total saving by 1 cent <Cite id="nber-active-vs-passive-2012" />. We therefore give existing investors a new-saving share of 15%. Second, a review by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) puts the share of saving in tax-favoured plans that is new at 25% to 33%; we give the other two groups 30% <Cite id="oecd-financial-incentives-retirement-2018" />. German studies of Riester found that its effect on household saving was small at best and absent for low earners, which argues for less; our Low scenario therefore uses 10% <Cite id="dunckerhumblot-riester-scheme-private-2009" /> <Cite id="fuberlin-erhoeht-riester-foerderung" />. With only the new-saving shares moved between their Low and High values, new household saving in 2030E is EUR 0.3bn to EUR 1.5bn. The OECD's 2026 pensions outlook, as reported in the trade press, repeats the caution that subsidised contributions are not necessarily additional saving <Cite id="versicherungsbote-oecd-studie-zeigt-2026" />.

The S&P, Morgan Stanley and Deutsche Bank figures are as reported by the trade press. The figure of EUR 90bn that also circulates is Apollo's total for all three pension pillars, including the Kapitalrente that is not yet law <Cite id="apollo-germany-pension-reform-2026" />.

No voluntary scheme in our comparison has done that, and the one survey-based forecast points to less than a quarter of S&P's low end in the first year.

### The budget assumes far less

The government's own numbers sit at the other extreme. The fiscal table of the government draft showed tax shortfalls rising to EUR 0.47bn in 2030, measured against a scenario without reform <Cite id="br-drs-768-25" />. Its explanatory memorandum states the assumption behind that table: the volume of Riester subsidies fell by about EUR 175m a year from 2020 to 2025, and the reform is assumed to stop that fall because lapsing contracts are replaced by new ones <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4088" />. In other words, the draft budgeted for a product that does no more than hold the subsidy volume steady. Parliament then admitted the self-employed, costed at EUR 350m a year, and raised the allowance, costed at EUR 15m for each annual cohort of new contracts <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4997" />. The amended table, which we have from a single search extract, shows EUR 0.24bn in 2027 rising to EUR 0.88bn in 2030; the two additions explain the difference to the draft <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4996" />.

In our Base scenario the reform costs EUR 3.3bn more than no reform in 2030E, 3.7 times the amended official figure and 7.1 times the draft. Two things drive the gap. Savers who move to a depot receive more per head than under Riester, and Riester savers who stay can opt into the higher allowance with a single declaration; that opt-in, for which we have no data and assume 50%, accounts for EUR 0.4bn. If the industry's inflow forecasts came true at the subsidised maximum, allowances alone would run to between EUR 6bn and EUR 13bn a year. The official table and the industry forecasts cannot both be right; we think both are wrong, in opposite directions.

## Savers gain on returns, not subsidy

For the individual saver the reform raises the expected outcome. The gain comes from holding more equities, which the guarantee used to prevent, and from lower costs; the higher allowance matters least. More equity also means more risk: the numbers below are expected values on fixed return assumptions, not promises.

### Same money in, twice the capital

Take a saver who puts in EUR 100 a month for 35 years. In a Riester contract earning 2.0% a year after costs, our estimate for a 25% equity share at Riester's cost, with the full old allowance, the capital at retirement is about EUR 69,000. In a depot at a neobroker, invested 80% in equities at an annual cost of 0.12%, it is about EUR 162,000. The same depot at the statutory cap of 1.0% yields EUR 135,000.

Their financial assets stood at EUR 9,831bn in mid-2026 <Cite id="bundesbank-geldvermoegens-aussenfinanzie-deutsch" />. Against these magnitudes the depot is small.

We estimate provider revenue as product cost on average assets, by channel. In the Base scenario 47% of depot assets sit with neobrokers and direct banks, 32% in branch-bank standard depots and 21% in advised, active or guarantee products. The average cost is 0.40% a year, against 1.46% on Riester capital.

Whatever the yield, part of the difference goes to savers: on the capital that would otherwise sit in Riester contracts they pay EUR 0.4bn less in 2035E. **Brokers and direct banks** treat the depot as customer acquisition. Trade Republic had more than 10m customers at the end of 2025, according to one press report, and a net profit of EUR 233m for the year to September 2025; ING Germany runs 3.2m securities accounts and flatexDEGIRO 3.72m <Cite id="financeloop-trade-republic-bank-2026" /> <Cite id="handelsblatt-fintechs-trade-republic-2026" /> <Cite id="ing-deutschland-waechst-erweitert-2026" /> <Cite id="tokenpost-flatexdegiro-q3-2026-2026" />. Refunding fund costs is cheap at this stage: in our Base scenario all platforms together refund EUR 36m in 2035E. The prize is a customer with a locked-in account who may also trade, hold cash and buy other products. The risk is that the German exemption from the EU ban on payment for order flow ended on 30 June 2026, which removed one way of earning on such customers <Cite id="brokervergleich-pfof-verbot-neobroker-2026" />. Online providers already took a good half of net new securities accounts in the first half of 2026 <Cite id="itfinanzmagazin-hausbanken-legen-depotzuwachs-20" />.

**Fund houses** face a trade between new flows and old margins. Union Investment manages more than EUR 34bn for just under 1.8m Riester customers, 68% of all Riester fund assets (our calculation), and DWS about EUR 8bn according to a press report <Cite id="union-riester-rente-abschliessen" /> <Cite id="bvi-jahrbuch-2026-daten-2026" /> <Cite id="boersenzeitung-beim-neuen-riester" />. Both now offer standard depots at a fraction of the old cost through their banking networks. DWS earned a management fee margin of 24.5 basis points in the third quarter of 2026 on EUR 1,221bn of assets; a standard depot at 0.28% is close to that average, but far below what Riester products paid <Cite id="dws-q3-2026-quarterly-2026" />. Deka, with 9.1m savings plans in the savings banks, chose to defend the relationship at 0.1% <Cite id="deka-anleger-investieren-deutlich-2026" />.

**Life insurers** have the most to lose. They hold about two-thirds of Riester contracts: 9.75m at the end of 2025 in the Labour Ministry's count, 9.5m at the members of the insurers' association <Cite id="ipe-banks-asset-managers-2026" /> <Cite id="bmas-entwicklung-riester-vertraege-2026-2" /> <Cite id="gdv-zahl-riester-renten-2026" />. A survey-based extrapolation, reported in the trade press, puts the Riester capital that could move to depots at EUR 61bn, just under a quarter of the stock in that survey; we use 12% of the stock, EUR 28bn, in the Base scenario and the survey's share, 24%, only in the High scenario <Cite id="procontraonline-riester-wechsel-61-2026" /> <Cite id="pfefferminzia-versicherern-droht-milliardenabfl" />. Insurers are responding: 29 of 38 surveyed life insurers plan a product for January 2027, 17 of them with an 80% guarantee <Cite id="cashonline-marktueberblic-altersvorsorge-29-2026" /> <Cite id="versicherungswir-franke-bornberg-29-2026" /> <Cite id="pfefferminzia-altersvorsorge-volle-garantie-2026" />. Their advantage is the payout phase, where a lifelong annuity needs an insurer, and advice-led selling to customers who want a guarantee. S&P does not expect them to keep their two-thirds share, according to a press report of its study <Cite id="ipe-banks-asset-managers-2026" />.

**ETF issuers** collect the fees that remain. By one data provider's count, as reported in the trade press, iShares holds 38.8% of European ETF assets and the five largest issuers 74.1% <Cite id="etfworld-european-etfs-record-2026" />. Standard depots are being built on their funds, sometimes under exclusive arrangements: according to press reports, Scalable's on an Xtrackers fund and quirion's on two Vanguard funds <Cite id="brokerexperte-altervorsorged-avd-vergleich-2026" /> <Cite id="quirion-riester-nachfolger-vanguard" />. At 0.12% a year, EUR 134bn of depot assets would be worth little to any one of them. The reform matters to issuers as a reason for savers to open a first account, not as a revenue line.

## The bigger pool is the Kapitalrente

If the question is how much capital German pension reform will send to markets, the Altersvorsorgedepot is the wrong place to look.

### A mandatory 2% dwarfs the depot

The pension commission reported on 24 June 2026 with 33 recommendations. One is a mandatory funded component inside the statutory pension, the Kapitalrente: individual capital accounts financed by an additional contribution of 2% of gross wages, phased in from 2028 in steps of half a point <Cite id="bundesregierung-empfehlungen-alterssicherun-beri" /> <Cite id="bmas-rentenkommissi-2026-33-2026" />. The ifo Institute puts the resulting flow at about EUR 35bn a year in today's values, and the Bundestag's research service at EUR 30bn to EUR 35bn <Cite id="ifo-gesetzliche-kapitalrente-potenzial-2026" /> <Cite id="bundestag-fragen-gesetzlichen-kapitalrente-2026" />.

A mandatory contribution also differs in kind. Compulsory saving is hard to offset: in Australia each dollar of compulsory superannuation added an estimated 70 to 90 cents to household wealth <Cite id="rba-rdp-2007-08-2007" />. That evidence is from another country and from 1992 to 2007, so it carries over only loosely. It suggests that a large part of the Kapitalrente would be new saving, where the depot's new saving is 10% of its inflow.

The proposal is not law. As of 23 November 2026 the cabinet had not adopted a bill; press reports expect a decision on the pension package in December, with the Kapitalrente fixed only as key points and its own bill not before the first quarter of 2027 <Cite id="cashonline-rentenreform-2026-kapitalrente-2026" /> <Cite id="cashonline-rentenreform-fahrplan-tabelle-2026" />. Trade unions and employers both oppose it, for different reasons <Cite id="frankenpost-ringen-um-reformen-2026" /> <Cite id="cashonline-rentenkommissi-gewerkschaften-arbeitg" />. Who would manage the money is open: according to press reports the Bundesbank has signalled interest, and the commission also named the public nuclear-waste fund <Cite id="dasinvestment-bundesbank-will-kapitalrente-2026" /> <Cite id="fundresearch-milliarden-wettlauf-um-2026" />. A design with a state default fund and a choice among certified private funds would make the Kapitalrente, not the depot, the mandate that asset managers compete for <Cite id="versicherungsbote-kapitalrente-bundesbank-koennt" />.

### Opt-out would cost the budget

The second lever is enrolment. If the depot were opt-out and 80% of eligible people stayed in at EUR 600 a year, the gross inflow would be EUR 31bn, 3.2 times our Base scenario. Under the enacted allowance schedule the allowances alone would cost EUR 12bn a year. The Council of Economic Experts' own opt-out design, with a participation rate of about 80%, was costed at about EUR 5bn of subsidy a year <Cite id="svr-arbeitspapier-04-2025-2025" />. An opt-out depot would therefore need a leaner allowance. On our counterfactual, the generous match in the enacted law is affordable only at low take-up; that is our inference, not a statement by the government.

### The public product is a shell

The law authorises the government to create a state-run Standarddepot by ordinance <Cite id="bundestag-antwort-bundesregierun-kleine-2026" />. No ordinance has been issued. The Bundesrat asked for the product to be on sale by 1 January 2027 and for the Bundesbank to be considered as manager; in November 2026 a Finance Ministry spokesperson said it would probably come only after the start of the reform, and government circles spoke of summer 2027 at the earliest <Cite id="bundesrat-br-drs-206-2026" /> <Cite id="dasinvestment-altersvorsorge-bundesregierun-verp" /> <Cite id="versicherungswir-staatliches-standarddepot-kommt" />. The insurers' and fund managers' associations oppose a state provider, according to press reports <Cite id="dasinvestment-staatliches-standarddepot-sagen" />.

Where a public default exists, it sets the price. Savers in Sweden's premium pension paid 0.12% on average in 2023 after rebates, and members of UK default funds 0.48% in 2020 against a cap of 0.75% <Cite id="realtid-premiepensione-fylls-pa" /> <Cite id="govuk-pension-charges-survey" /> <Cite id="govuk-government-response-review" />. In Germany competition has done that work before the state arrived: the cheapest private standard depots already cost less than the Swedish average. A public product would now matter mainly in the branch channel and for people who distrust private providers.

A first state-managed fund will exist all the same. Under the Frühstart-Rente the state pays EUR 10 a month for each child aged 6 to 17, starting with those born in 2020, into a Standarddepot opened by the parents; where there is no contract, the money goes from 1 January 2028 into a federal special fund managed by the Bundesbank <Cite id="bt-drs-21-7864" /> <Cite id="bmf-fragen-antworten-fruehstartrent" /> <Cite id="bundestag-bt-drs-21" />. The budget for it rises from EUR 198m in 2027 to EUR 411m in 2030 <Cite id="bt-drs-21-7864" />. The sums are small, but every cohort that turns 18 will arrive with an account already open.

## Sources and method

**Data used.** About 2,200 data points from 755 sources, gathered on 23 November 2026: the law and parliamentary papers, the Finance and Labour Ministries, the Deutsche Rentenversicherung, the Bundesbank, the Federal Statistical Office, the Council of Economic Experts, industry associations (BVI, GDV, Deutsches Aktieninstitut), company reports and press releases, academic papers, the OECD, foreign pension authorities, and the financial and trade press. All of them, with URL, access date and the extract used, are in the workbook.

**How sources were read.** The working environment could not open source documents. Every figure was taken from search-engine extracts of the cited page and graded: A for a verbatim extract attributed to the primary source's own domain, A- where the search tool attributed the figure to that domain without a verbatim quote, B where two or more independent secondary sources agree, C for a single secondary source. Grades are shown for each data point in the workbook. Where a sentence or exhibit relies on grade C material, the text says so or names the type of source. After drafting, an independent reviewer traced a sample of figures to the workbook and re-searched 59 of the sourced facts; its findings led to the corrections listed in the workbook's log.

**Cut-off.** 23 November 2026. Riester subsidy statistics are the Finance Ministry's edition of 16 November 2026; contribution year 2023 is the last near-complete year. Provider prices are announcements made by the cut-off.

**The model.** The workbook compares two worlds from 2026 to 2040. In the baseline Riester stays open, subsidised savers fall at the 2020-23 rate, and contributions and public support per saver stay at their 2023 values. In the reform scenarios three groups of eligible people take up the depot at assumed rates along a common ramp; allowances follow the statutory formula; each euro of contribution is assigned an origin (Riester money, redirected securities saving, deposits or new saving); assets accumulate at an assumed return after costs; provider revenue is product cost on assets by channel, less the revenue lost on Riester capital. The return on Riester capital uses the same market returns at Riester's equity share and cost. Low, Base and High are run side by side, and a closed-form recalculation checks the Base results for inflow, new saving, public cost and assets.

**Our calculations.** Shares, ratios, per-saver averages, the count of eligible people, the reconstruction of Deutsche Bank's estimate, the savers implied by each third-party estimate and all scenario results are ours. They are marked as model results, as "our calculation" or by a year followed by E.

**Conflicts resolved.** Promulgation of the law: 29 May 2026 per the gazette header, not 1 June as in one law-firm note. Riester statistics: the latest ministry edition, which revises earlier years. Household financial assets: the Bundesbank press-release series for households. Riester capital: no official total exists; four third-party figures range from EUR 220bn to EUR 255bn and differ in scope, and we use their median. Riester insurance contracts: 9.75m in the Labour Ministry's statistics, 9.5m at the members of the insurers' association. Eligible people: our sum of official group sizes (40.3m) rather than the "over 30 million" cited in a committee debate for the pre-reform group or the roughly 45 million used by one fund house <Cite id="bundestag-hib-kurzmeldung-reform-2026" /> <Cite id="dasinvestment-avd-wie-sich" />. The EUR 90bn in circulation is a three-pillar total, not a depot estimate; the EUR 40bn that Apollo cites for the private pillar is Oliver Wyman's.

**Not verified.** The original S&P, Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank and Vanguard publications, whose figures are as reported by the press. The amended official fiscal table: its values come from one search extract of the committee report and three further searches did not return the table; they reconcile with the draft table plus the two additions quantified in the budget report (EUR 875m against EUR 880m in the last year, our check), the year labels 2027 to 2030 are inferred, and we assume that it covers allowances and tax relief together <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4996" /> <Cite id="bt-drs-21-4088" />. Union Investment's price for its standard depot, which comes from comparison portals; the company has published none, and other figures circulate. The upper end of the survey-based contract forecast, returned by one search only. The projected Riester returns in a study for a fund house, and that study's date and definitions. Consorsbank's ETF cost, our price for the direct channel, which a second search did not return. The enacted wording of several product rules (permitted investments, switching costs, the effect of a transfer on the Riester guarantee), which rest on the Finance Ministry's FAQ and on guides. Any official headcount of eligible people. Realised returns and portfolio-wide costs of Riester products. Scalable Capital's latest accounts and Trade Republic's results after September 2025. The claim that fund managers rather than Trade Republic bear its waived fund costs, which rests on one press report. Whether the Frühstart-Rente law had been promulgated at the cut-off.

**Limitations.** No study measures new saving for this product; the new-saving shares are judgement within the range of the literature, and the German evidence on Riester points to the low end. Several inputs are judgement without direct data: the mix of sales channels, the split of non-new money between deposits and securities, the share of remaining Riester savers who opt into the new allowance, the overlap between investors and Riester savers and the timing of transfers. Depot savers do not lapse in the Base scenario, while Riester savers in the baseline do. Allowances are computed at each group's average contribution, which overstates them somewhat where contributions spread around the kinks of the schedule. Returns are fixed assumptions: the saver comparison shows expected values, not the range of outcomes of an equity portfolio. The model has one take-up ramp for all groups, no contribution growth, a flat payout rate and no tax on payouts, and it ignores second-round effects. Provider revenue rests on a Riester cost taken from offers on sale and excludes brokerage, interest and lending income. Announced prices may change before certification.
