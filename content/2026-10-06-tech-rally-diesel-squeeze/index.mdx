## Services growth eased, but price pressure increased

The Institute for Supply Management (ISM) services Purchasing Managers' Index slipped from 55.4 in August to 54.9 in September, still above the 50-point threshold that separates expansion from contraction <Cite id="ism-services" />. Business activity slowed more sharply, from 61.7 to 56.5, while employment returned to marginal expansion at 50.1 after two months below 50 <Cite id="ism-services" />.

Prices moved the other way. The prices index rose from 72.6 to 74.0, its highest reading since July 2022 <Cite id="ism-services" />. These are diffusion-index readings, not percentage increases in prices: 74 does not mean that service prices rose 74% <Cite id="ism-services" />.

Fuel costs appeared twice as often as any other individual issue in respondents' comments; tariffs were another prominent supply-chain concern <Cite id="ism-services" />. One agricultural respondent described diesel costs raising freight expenses and squeezing farmers during harvest <Cite id="ism-services" />. That provides a reported channel from energy costs into services, though the survey cannot isolate how much of the prices-index rise came from fuel.

By Tuesday morning, the ten-year Treasury yield was 5.281% and the thirty-year was 5.637%, both roughly three basis points below their previous levels <Cite id="treasury-yields" />. CNBC reported a 78% market-implied probability of unchanged rates at the next Federal Reserve meeting; the September meeting minutes were due on Wednesday, 7 October <Cite id="treasury-yields" />. That probability was a futures-market snapshot, not a Fed commitment.

## Crude supply is recovering faster than refined fuel

The relevant distinction for transport costs is between crude arriving at refineries and finished fuel reaching buyers. Vortexa figures reported by Oilprice, citing Reuters, put Gulf crude flows at 91% of pre-war levels in September, versus only 60% for refined fuel exports <Cite id="gulf-flows" />.

The two ratios should not be added together or read as export-market shares. Nor should this crude-only comparison be mixed with the broader 81% oil-export recovery figure reported in the same article, which discusses different product groupings and data providers <Cite id="gulf-flows" />. Vortexa also warned that daily flows remained more volatile than before the war, leaving the durability of September's recovery uncertain <Cite id="gulf-flows" />.

China's reported export pause adds another constraint. CNA, citing a Reuters report from 1 October, said Chinese refiners had suspended fuel exports for October; Beijing had not made an official announcement <Cite id="china-fuel" />. Analysts interviewed by CNA said additional production in India and South Korea could cushion Asia's shortfall, but replacement cargoes could also be diverted away from Europe <Cite id="china-fuel" />. That is a possible transmission mechanism, not a measured forecast of how many barrels will move.

## US diesel relief changes tax treatment, not refining capacity

The US national average diesel price exceeded $6 a gallon in September for the first time, according to CNBC <Cite id="diesel-prices" />. The executive order signed on 5 October responds through temporary tax and penalty relief rather than new fuel production <Cite id="diesel-order" />.

The order contains two distinct instructions. Within five days, Treasury must direct the Internal Revenue Service to announce penalty relief for dyed diesel sold for highway use or used on highways between 5 October and 31 December <Cite id="diesel-order" />. Dyed diesel is normally used off-road, including in farming and construction, and is exempt from the highway fuel tax <Cite id="diesel-prices" />.

Within the same five-day period, Treasury must determine whether statutory conditions allow certain diesel excise-tax payments to be deferred; if they do, it must defer qualifying payments to the extent permitted by law <Cite id="diesel-order" />. Implementation guidance must specify eligibility, conditions and repayment deadlines <Cite id="diesel-order" />.

<Callout type="important" title="Deferral is not tax forgiveness">

The order separately directs Treasury to explore ways, including legislation, to eliminate deferred liabilities. It does not itself cancel those liabilities <Cite id="diesel-order" />. The distinction matters when estimating how much relief persists beyond the payment delay.

</Callout>

## Germany's orders slump overstates the monthly deterioration

Germany's real manufacturing orders fell 10.6% in August from July, after seasonal and calendar adjustment, according to provisional Destatis data <Cite id="german-orders" />. Most of the fall came from other transport equipment, including aircraft, ships, trains and military vehicles: orders in that category fell 61.5% after unusually large contracts had more than doubled July's volume <Cite id="german-orders" />.

Excluding large orders, the August decline was just 0.1% <Cite id="german-orders" />. The three-month comparison still showed underlying weakness: June–August orders were 1.3% above the preceding three months in total, but 2.6% lower excluding large contracts <Cite id="german-orders" />. Removing the monthly distortion therefore does not establish a broad recovery.

In an interview conducted on 1 October and published on 6 October, the European Central Bank's chief economist, Philip Lane, said energy prices were above the bank's baseline assumptions, while very strong second-round effects had not yet appeared <Cite id="ecb-lane" />. He also identified debt-financed US artificial-intelligence investment as one factor behind higher long-term yields <Cite id="ecb-lane" />. That is Lane's assessment, not a decomposition showing how much of the yield increase AI investment caused.

## The next tests are fuel availability and policy implementation

For the inflation outlook, three developments would be more informative than another isolated day of equity gains:

- Whether refined-fuel exports recover alongside crude, and whether that improvement persists despite the daily volatility reported by Vortexa <Cite id="gulf-flows" />.
- The scope and repayment terms of Treasury's diesel-tax guidance, rather than assuming the headline announcement provides permanent relief <Cite id="diesel-order" />.
- What the Fed's September minutes reveal about policymakers' assessment of price pressure when they are released on 7 October <Cite id="treasury-yields" />.

These are monitoring questions, not trading recommendations or forecasts. The available evidence shows a strong technology market alongside an unresolved fuel shortage; it does not establish which will dominate the next market move.
