# Oracle, Blue Owl and the Jupiter notice

## Bottom line: a hedge, not an exit, but the risk has moved from demand to delivery

Oracle's notice is a hedge, not an exit. What it reveals is that the AI build-out's binding constraint is no longer customer demand but power, permits and the price of money.

Oracle cannot walk away from Jupiter, according to a Reuters source <Cite id="reuters-oracle-force-majeure-power-2026" />. The notice seeks to push back when full rent starts if gas for the campus's on-site fuel cells arrives late. Because securing power is reportedly Oracle's own obligation, the claim is contestable; we expect a negotiated change to the rent schedule, not a lawsuit (our judgment).

Markets have priced the risk before the lawyers have. Jupiter's $18bn loan is quoted around 90 cents, Oracle's credit default swaps are at a record and S&P rates it one notch above junk <Cite id="reuters-ft-jupiter-loans-2026" /> <Cite id="bloomberg-oracle-force-majeure-2026" />. Meanwhile the 10-year Treasury is at its highest since 2007 and the Fed has started hiking again <Cite id="tradingeconomics-us-10y-2026" />.

Our base case, at roughly 55%, is a 6–12 month slip, a later rent start and no default. The risks worth watching lie beyond this campus: a second rating cut at Oracle, more stalled syndications across Stargate, and New Mexico politics after the Nov. 3 midterms.

## Mechanics: a fight over when rent starts, framed as force majeure

The notice is best read as Oracle's attempt to recast a power delay it owns as a government-caused event. If that framing holds, full rent starts later; if it fails, Oracle pays on the original schedule.

The money flows one way, from customer to lenders, but the risk sits in the power chain: gas, fuel cells and two state decisions that Oracle, not its landlord, has to get through.

### What force majeure does in these contracts

- **Leases.** Force majeure clauses usually extend the time to perform; many leave the duty to pay money untouched. A Delaware court in *Simon Property v. Regal* (2022) held that a government shutdown did not excuse a tenant's rent <Cite id="quinn-emanuel-force-majeure-2026" />.
- **Power and gas contracts.** Relief typically suspends delivery, but clauses often exclude loss of supply or markets. Price spikes do not count: *J.P. Morgan Ventures v. Miami Wind* rejected a Winter Storm Uri claim <Cite id="quinn-emanuel-force-majeure-2026" />.
- **Governing law.** New York excuses only events a clause specifically names (*Kel Kim*, 1987); Delaware enforces broad catch-all wording between sophisticated parties <Cite id="quinn-emanuel-force-majeure-2026" />.

### The likely trigger: government action on power

A Reuters source says Oracle invoked force majeure because of delays in securing power <Cite id="reuters-ai-financing-ripples-2026" />. The candidate events are all official acts: two land-office denials of the pipeline's route (March 20, July 14), the pipeline's slip to Feb. 1, 2027, and the Supreme Court's Aug. 23–Sept. 17 pause of the air-permit case <Cite id="searchlight-jupiter-timeline-2026" /> <Cite id="bloomberg-oracle-force-majeure-2026" />. Which of these the notice cites is not public (our reading).

### Why the claim is contestable (our judgment)

- **Assumed risk.** Power is reportedly Oracle's obligation under the lease <Cite id="reuters-oracle-force-majeure-power-2026" />. A party that took on a risk generally cannot call it force majeure when it arrives.
- **Foreseeability.** Oracle signed in January 2026, after lawsuits, a contested county vote and an incomplete air-permit filing. Quinn Emanuel warns that community opposition and permitting delays now look foreseeable <Cite id="quinn-emanuel-force-majeure-2026" />.
- **Causation.** The April switch from turbines to fuel cells restarted the air-permit process. A landlord could argue part of the delay was Oracle's own design choice.
- **Scope.** Even a valid claim tends to buy time, not forgiveness. The reported three-year deferral applies only if both sides agree an event occurred, and full-term rent is still owed <Cite id="bloomberg-oracle-force-majeure-2026" />.

### What the notice preserves

- **Timeliness.** Clauses often demand notice within days; late notice can forfeit relief (*Kaiser Aluminum*: 10 days; *Magna Entertainment*) <Cite id="quinn-emanuel-force-majeure-2026" />. Sending early protects the claim even if no delay materialises, consistent with Oracle's line that such notices are routine.
- **The rent clock.** If accepted, the notice could defer full rent while Oracle keeps paying the lower development-stage rent that covers debt costs (Reuters source <Cite id="reuters-oracle-force-majeure-power-2026" />).
- **Alignment downstream.** It lets Oracle hold a consistent position with OpenAI, Bloom and the pipeline, so a delay under one contract does not leave it exposed under another (our inference).

### How the other side can respond

| Party | Options | Likely move (our judgment) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stack and Blue Owl | Dispute the claim with a reservation of rights; demand adequate assurance (*Norcon Power Partners*, N.Y. 1998); trade a later rent start for a longer term, higher rent or a guarantee | Negotiate an amendment; Blue Owl says commitments are unchanged |
| About 20 banks | Confirm no default, which one Bloomberg source already says; lean on extension options and interest reserves; seek tighter terms | Hold the loan rather than sell near 90 cents |
| OpenAI | Seek substitute Oracle capacity; reset delivery dates | Quiet renegotiation, not a public fight |
| Oracle | Document mitigation, such as its 2GW renewables tender; keep paying development rent; avoid overclaiming | Keep the notice as insurance |

### What past disputes suggest

- **Litigated claims usually fail** when the event is economic, foreseeable or within the invoker's control: *Macalloy* (costly environmental rules), *Zero Carbon Holdings* (2024, regulatory delays) <Cite id="quinn-emanuel-force-majeure-2026" />.
- **They succeed** when the clause names the event and causation is clean, as in *LNG Americas v. Chevron* (2023, winter storm) <Cite id="quinn-emanuel-force-majeure-2026" />.
- **Overreach can backfire.** An overbroad notice can hand the counterparty an exit (*Blue Bus Tours*, 2025) <Cite id="quinn-emanuel-force-majeure-2026" />. That matters only if Blue Owl wanted out, which it denies.
- **Most such fights settle.** Parties who need each other rewrite schedules rather than litigate (our judgment). The exception is when a court voids the backstop itself, as Washington's did with WPPSS in 1983; see the parallels below.

## Implications: Oracle pays first, private credit next

Jupiter changes little in this year's numbers but tests the premise behind all of them: that contracted backlog turns into cash on schedule. The first bill lands on Oracle's balance sheet, the second on the private-credit machinery funding the build-out.

### Blue Owl and private credit

- **Blue Owl's exposure is equity, not debt.** It has about $3bn in Jupiter, earning about 9% during construction and about 11% after; a delay postpones the step-up rather than erasing it (Reuters source <Cite id="reuters-oracle-force-majeure-power-2026" />).
- **Funding is the pressure point.** Two Blue Owl credit funds capped withdrawals this spring after requests of 22% and 41% of shares† <Cite id="bloomberg-blue-owl-redemptions-2026" />. It also struggled to syndicate a $4bn CoreWeave-anchored project in Lancaster, Pa. <Cite id="reuters-ai-financing-ripples-2026" />.
- **The asset class is fraying at the edges.** Private-credit defaults hit a record 6.3% in the year to August (CNBC†) <Cite id="wallst247-blue-owl-2026" />. Blue Owl's shares now trade as a proxy for that stress (our judgment).

### OpenAI and Stargate

- **The customer burns cash by design.** OpenAI projects $278bn of negative free cash flow over 2026–30 and may run through its March raise by 2028, the FT reported <Cite id="runtimewire-openai-cash-burn-2026" />. It raised $122bn at an $852bn valuation in March and is discussing a round at $1.2trn or more.
- **A slip cuts both ways.** Later capacity slows OpenAI's compute ramp but also defers payments it would otherwise have to fund (our judgment).
- **Stargate financing feels it first.** The notice is reverberating through talks for SB Energy's Ohio campus for OpenAI, and SB Energy has delayed its IPO <Cite id="reuters-ai-financing-ripples-2026" />.

### Power and equipment suppliers

- **Bloom Energy** fell about 6% on the news† <Cite id="aiweekly-oracle-force-majeure-2026" />, though Evercore argues its Oracle agreement spans many sites† <Cite id="seekingalpha-ai-infrastructure-risks-2026" />. Fuel cells for Jupiter cannot be installed until the air permit is issued.
- **Energy Transfer** says it is still working through permits <Cite id="dailycaller-jupiter-air-permit-2026" />. For a 17-mile line the risk is delay costs, not a stranded asset (our judgment).
- **The wider squeeze is political.** Data Center Watch counted 45 projects worth $68bn facing local opposition in the second quarter, after 75 worth about $130bn were disrupted in the first <Cite id="reuters-ai-financing-ripples-2026" />.

### Data-center financing

- **A strong tenant no longer suffices.** Reuters reports that the presence of highly rated firms such as Oracle is not enough to reassure lenders when schedules slip <Cite id="reuters-ai-financing-ripples-2026" />.
- **Contracts are being rewritten.** Quinn Emanuel's Rajat Rana told Reuters that data-center contracts signed today differ sharply from those of January <Cite id="reuters-ai-financing-ripples-2026" />.
- **What changes next (our judgment):** completion guarantees, explicit allocation of permit and power risk, wider spreads, and more loans left on bank balance sheets. The stakes are rising: Moody's expects AI capex by the six biggest US tech firms to reach about $1trn in 2027 <Cite id="reuters-ai-financing-ripples-2026" />.

### The AI trade and the macro backdrop

- **Money is dearer.** The 10-year Treasury yield was 5.17% on Sept. 25, near its highest since 2007. The Fed raised rates to 3.75–4% on Sept. 16, its first hike since 2023, and markets see roughly two-in-three odds of another in October <Cite id="tradingeconomics-us-10y-2026" />.
- **Oil is the accelerant.** Brent traded near $104 amid the US–Iran conflict, and consumer inflation ran at 3.4% in August <Cite id="tradingeconomics-us-10y-2026" />.
- **Borrowing is at records.** Five hyperscalers raised nearly $302bn of debt and equity by July 22, against $108bn in all of 2025. Goldman counted $489bn of AI-related bond and loan supply over the same span <Cite id="axios-hyperscaler-debt-2026" />.
- **What it means (our judgment).** The 2025 question was whether AI demand was real; the 2026 question is whether permits and the price of money let the build-out keep its schedule. That favours cash-rich hyperscalers over leveraged builders such as Oracle and CoreWeave, and over the lenders behind them.

### What the parallels say about Jupiter (our judgment)

- **Warning signs already visible:** repriced project loans, stalled syndications, record CDS, organised local opposition, and a customer planning to burn cash for years. Falling unit prices echo telecom too: OpenAI cut one model's price by about 80% in July <Cite id="runtimewire-openai-cash-burn-2026" />.
- **Signs still missing:** widespread defaults, accounting scandal and, above all, a demand shortfall.
- **The WPPSS lesson** is the one lenders should heed: a contract backstop is only as strong as the courts, regulators and ratings behind it. At Jupiter the weak links are state permits and Oracle's rating, not the lease itself.
- **The usual ending:** assets survive and change hands. Leveraged builders and their lenders absorb the losses, and buyers near the bottom inherit the infrastructure cheaply.

## Scenarios and signposts: a slip is likely, a default is not

Our central case is a 6–12 month slip absorbed by a change to the rent schedule. The tail risks sit in New Mexico politics and Oracle's rating, not in demand for computing. The odds below are our judgment.

### Signposts (thresholds are our judgment)

- **Permit.** A hearing officer and schedule named before Nov. 23 is constructive; a missed deadline or a denial is not.
- **Pipeline.** Any regulatory filing that pushes the Feb. 1, 2027 in-service date later points to the bear case.
- **Politics.** The Nov. 3 results for governor and land commissioner, and whether a moratorium bill moves in the January session.
- **Contract.** A Blue Owl dispute letter or an 8-K quantifying its exposure would be bearish; an announced amendment, bullish.
- **Credit.** Jupiter loan quotes below 85, Oracle CDS sustained above 250bp or a Moody's downgrade would tilt us bearish.
- **Customer.** OpenAI closing a round at $1.2trn or more supports the base case; a stalled round does not.

What would change our base case: evidence that demand, not permits, lies behind the delay, such as OpenAI moving capacity away from Oracle.
