## Business Description

### Overview

**Alibaba Group Holding Limited**, headquartered in Hangzhou, China, is a **multinational technology company** operating a diversified digital commerce and cloud computing ecosystem (Exhibit 4). The group primarily **generates revenue through online marketplaces, merchant services, logistics-enabled commerce, and cloud infrastructure**, with a core focus on the **Chinese consumer and small business economy**. As of today, Alibaba has a market capitalization of approximately RMB 2.6 trillion (USD 370 billion).

**The company operates digital platforms** that connect consumers, merchants, brands, and service providers, enabling transactions, marketing activities, fulfilment, and enterprise digitalization across its ecosystem. This interconnected ecosystem **empowers small business owners** and **creates synergy benefits** non-comparable to competitors.

### Competitive Positioning

#### Cloud Computing and AI

**Chinese market dominance.** As of the first half of 2025, Alibaba Cloud continues to hold the largest share of the Chinese cloud infrastructure market, maintaining a significant advantage over its closest rivals: Huawei Cloud, Tencent Cloud, and Baidu AI Cloud. In Q2 of 2025, Alibaba Cloud held a 34% market share, followed by Huawei Cloud at 17%, Tencent Cloud at 10%, and Baidu AI Cloud at 6% (Exhibit 22).<Cite id="omdia-china-cloud-q2-2025" />

**Vertical integration cost advantages.** Alibaba Cloud's competitive positioning in AI is characterized by a vertically integrated, full-stack architecture spanning the entire value chain, from proprietary semiconductors (T-Head) to cloud infrastructure and foundation models (Qwen). This integration generates three key advantages: (1) the elimination of intermediaries margins, which greatly reduce the total cost structure compared to competitors assembling third-party components; (2) great product efficiency, as co-optimizing in-house chips with proprietary models reduces power consumption and lowers unit compute costs; and (3) broad infrastructure cost amortization across Alibaba's e-commerce and logistics operations, helping achieve unmatched economies of scale. This "AI + Cloud" value proposition has resulted in triple-digit revenue growth for AI-related products for nine consecutive quarters as of September 2025 (Q2 FY26E).

### Profitability

**Margin compression in the immediate future**. Alibaba's profitability is being greatly affected by its transformation from a mature e-commerce platform to an AI-driven cloud provider. Gross margin declined from 41.3% in FY21A to 36.8% in FY22A due to the COVID pandemic, although it had almost completely recovered by FY25A to 40.0%, supported by higher-margin customer management revenue within China e-commerce and improved product mix in Cloud Intelligence as AI-related services scale. However, we expect EBIT margins to decline from 14.1% in FY25A to 6.6% in FY26E, reflecting elevated sales and marketing expenditures to **defend market share against PDD Holdings and Douyin**, alongside accelerated investment in AI product development. This near-term compression is expected to be transitory, though, as **Cloud Intelligence shifts toward higher-margin PaaS** offerings and e-commerce monetization stabilizes following competitive repositioning, EBIT margin is forecasted to recover to 10.3% by FY27E and expand steadily to 12.2% by FY35E. The **margin trajectory** is supported by **three drivers**: (1) an **increasing contribution from Cloud Intelligence**, where AI-related workloads carry higher margins than previous IaaS contracts; (2) the **operating leverage across TTG** given that fixed costs dilute with GMV growth and higher take rates; and (3) a **gradual improvement of international e-commerce profitability** as Lazada and AliExpress move past breakeven. Return on invested capital (ROIC) is expected to improve from 9.9% in FY25A to 12.6% by FY35E, as cloud infrastructure reaches the desired scale and AI monetization matures.

Since Alibaba Group Holding Limited comprises many businesses, we decided to include an SOTP analysis as an additional measure of its value.

#### Implied WACC of 7.03%

We estimated the present cost of debt, assuming a synthetic rating spread of 240 bps based on the 5-year and current spreads of A+-rated companies, and adjusting for an additional country risk premium. With a risk-free rate of 1.89% from CYN 10Y government bonds, the pre-tax cost of debt equals 4.29%, implying a post-tax cost of debt of 3.22% assuming a 25% tax rate.

For the cost of equity, we first estimated Alibaba’s beta using a 25/25/50 weighting of its historical beta, weighted-average peer-group betas, and median peer-group betas, with all betas are Blume-weighted to assume reversion to the market average. This yielded an unlevered beta of 0.75x and a levered beta of 0.81x. The ERP was calculated using a revenue-weighted regional exposure approach and the respective implied ERPs for those regions, resulting in an ERP of 4.33%. The same approach was applied to CRP, yielding 2.05%. The implied cost of equity is 7.46%. After estimating the market value of interest-bearing debt, the resulting capital structure implied an equity weight of 89.87% and a debt weight of 10.13%. We assume a constant WACC of 7.03% across the forecast horizon.

In a stress case with a WACC of 7.78% and a terminal CAGR of 1.25%, the intrinsic equity value falls to RMB 123, implying a 4% downside from the current price, which remains limited relative to the upside potential (Exhibit 43). ### Financial Risks

#### Probability of default (F1)

We employed the KMV-Merton methodology with 4 iterations to estimate the 10-year probability of default (PD), yielding a PD of 8.77%. Assuming a constant annual default intensity (flat hazard rate), this translates to a 0.91% 1-year PD. While there is no indication of short-term stress, the long-term risk is non-trivial. Nonetheless, the main driver of the risk is structural and macro exposure, rather than solvency stress. Alibaba is exposed to US–China macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainties, causing volatility in equity value, and therefore artificially increasing PD. Altman Z’’-score (for non-manufacturing firms in emerging markets) also consistently places Alibaba within the Safe zone. This implies a smaller concern regarding balance sheet stress.

### Operational Risks

#### Conglomerate decentralization (O1)

In 2023, Alibaba undertook one of the most significant structural changes in its history by adopting a “1+6+N” organizational model. This broke the business into six major semi-independent groups, including Cloud Intelligence, Taobao & Tmall Commerce, Local Life Services, and others, each with its own CEO and board under the Alibaba Group umbrella.<Cite id="alibaba-seller-six-units" /> This restructuring was aimed at reigniting entrepreneurial agility and enabling each unit to pursue tailored growth or even independent financing and listings, effectively shifting away from a tightly centralized conglomerate toward a federated ecosystem of businesses. While this change can help accelerate innovation within individual units, it also introduces fragmentation risk: divergent strategies, incentive misalignment, and increased coordination overhead can weaken synergy, slow cross-unit initiatives, and create internal competition for resources, talent, and strategic priorities.

Alibaba’s commitment to achieve carbon neutrality across direct (Scope 1 & 2) emissions by 2030 is ambitious, alongside a target to reduce Scope 3 carbon intensity by 50% by 2030 from a 2020 baseline and ecosystem-wide emissions reductions of 1.5 gigatons by 2035 (Scope 3+). ### Social | Strong Employee Support Tampered by Gig Economy and Regulatory Risks

Alibaba’s social performance is a result of its **continued efforts in building stability in the workforce and taking care of its employees**. Despite this, the percentage of women in Alibaba’s workforce and in senior management has been declining since FY22A from 49.5% and 50% to 35.4% and 37.5% in FY25A, respectively (Exhibit 67). Employee support activities have been extended outside the workplace, with more than 103,000 physical examinations arranged for employees’ parents and 68,000 employees taking family care leave in the year. These, combined with learning and development activities such as the “Learn AI, Use AI” employee training program, which attracted over 16,000 employees in FY25A, continue to position Alibaba as a talent developer in the long run.<Cite id="company-filings" /> This is further supported by external recognition, with Alibaba ranked among the top five Chinese companies on Forbes’ World’s Best Employers 2024 list. Workplace safety and satisfaction have been improving, with main campuses obtaining ISO 45001 and ISO 14001 certifications and an office environment satisfaction rate of 93.1%

Despite these strengths, there are considerable social risks associated with Alibaba. First, there are concerns regarding **labor practices** in the delivery and logistics segment of Alibaba. Ele.me focuses on a **gig workforce and contractual labor**, where labor laws are governed by platform rules rather than employment laws. This industry is particularly associated with China’s “996” work culture, which was officially ruled illegal by China’s Supreme People’s Court. This industry is consistently subjected to investigations by Chinese authorities regarding labor laws and regulations regarding labor hours. Even though Alibaba has introduced insurance plans and entered into collective labor agreements involving approximately 3 million logistics workers, there are interventions from Chinese authorities, indicating that these measures are not sufficient to mitigate labor risks. Second, there are risks associated with Alibaba’s size and algorithmic business, particularly regarding regulations on data protection. Any gaps in data protection can lead to regulatory risks due to increasing regulations on data protection in China.

### Governance | Concentrated Control and State Influence as Structural Risk

**Governance represents Alibaba’s most material ESG risk.** Alibaba operates within a system in which control is held by insiders and the state. There is little room for minority shareholders to influence corporate decisions. Alibaba’s corporate governance framework is based on the Alibaba Partnership, who reserve the right to appoint the majority of directors to the company’s board despite holding a minority stake.<Cite id="company-filings" />

**Ownership concentration further reinforces this imbalance.** There are no shareholders who own a majority of outstanding shares either as an outsider or an insider. The largest shareholders own less than 5% of outstanding shares (Exhibit 68 and Exhibit 69).<Cite id="factset" />

**Board composition reflects this concentration of control.** The company’s Board consists of 11 directors of whom 55% are classified as independent directors. This points to a lack of diversity in the company’s corporate governance. These concerns were compounded by the reversal in its corporate strategy in which the company’s management shifted from Daniel Zhang to Eddie Wu alongside Joe Tsai’s expanded executive role, which highlighted limited depth beyond the founding leadership.

**Executive remuneration.** Executive remuneration consists of salaries, bonuses, and share-based compensation, while non-executive directors receive fees and share-based compensation. Compensation is overseen by a fully independent Compensation Committee, though board nomination rights sit with the Alibaba Partnership rather than public shareholders. In FY25A, total remuneration for Directors and the chief executive amounted to RMB 518 million, with share-based compensation representing 75% of total remuneration (Exhibit 70). **Governance risk is further elevated by state involvement.** Chinese state-affiliated entities have board representation and veto power through Special Management Shares.<Cite id="bloomberg-state-ownership-2024" /> This is a risk to shareholders, as their interests are not protected when regulatory and political interests conflict with shareholders’ returns. **This structural exposure is reinforced by geopolitical and data-sovereignty concerns.** Alibaba is affected by legislation that requires cooperation with state authorities. This is an area where Alibaba conflicts with the US and European data sovereignty. This risk has been highlighted by a recent US political investigation, which includes reports suggesting that Alibaba may have access to data from Chinese state entities. However, Alibaba denies this. Despite this, there has been no evidence of wrongdoing. This is a risk to shareholders since Alibaba is not able to refuse state requests. This risk is increased by Alibaba Cloud’s position as a major infrastructure provider, prompting U.S. policymakers to call for restrictions on Chinese technology firms.

### Appendix 3: SWOT Analysis

#### Strengths

**Dominant e-commerce ecosystem** with 1.31 billion annual active consumers across Taobao, Tmall, and Freshippo as of March 31, 2025, generating RMB 996 billion in FY25A revenue and enabling unmatched data scale for AI training.

**Cloud leadership** in China with accelerating growth, ranking #1 domestically (34% market share per Omdia Q2 2025)<Cite id="omdia-china-cloud-q2-2025" /> and delivering 34% YoY revenue growth in Q2 FY26A driven by AI-as-a-service adoption.

**Strong balance sheet** with RMB 407 billion ($54 billion) in cash & short-term investments as of September 30, 2025, funding USD 24 billion in buybacks since 2023 without compromising AI/cloud investments.

#### Weaknesses

**International commerce remains low-margin**, with AIDC achieving only RMB 162 million in adjusted EBITA profit in Q2 FY26A after years of losses – profitability still fragile versus Temu/Shein's scale.

**Cloud segment lags global peers on margins**, operating at 3.5% adjusted EBITA margin in FY25A versus AWS's 18%, reflecting pricing pressure in China's competitive IaaS market.

**Domestic consumption headwinds persist**, with China's retail sales growth moderating to 3.7% YoY in 2025 (NBS data), pressuring Taobao/Tmall GMV growth to single digits despite AI enhancements.

#### Opportunities

**AI monetization via Qwen integration**, with Taobao's AI shopping assistant driving 30% higher conversion during 11.11 2025 festival.

**Regulatory normalization under "New Quality Productive Forces"** policy, with China's 2025 AI governance framework explicitly prioritizing domestic cloud/AI providers for government contracts.

**Capital return acceleration**, with USD 5.8 billion in buybacks executed in Q1 FY26A alone.

#### Threats

**Intensifying competition across all segments**: Pinduoduo's Temu capturing cross-border share (32% U.S. market penetration per Bloomberg Intelligence), while Tencent Cloud gains enterprise contracts with Hunyuan AI.

**Geopolitical friction on AI infrastructure**, with NVIDIA recording $0 H20 sales to China-based customers in Q2 FY26A after $4.6B in Q1 FY26A, constraining cloud GPU supply.

**Execution risk on AI monetization**, as Qwen 3.0's Tier-1 registry approval (deadline March 31, 2026) remains uncertain, only \~46% of 2025 applicants received approval per CAC data.
