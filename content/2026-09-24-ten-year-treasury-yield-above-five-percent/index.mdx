## Bottom line

- The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.11% on Sept. 23, 2026, its highest level since July 2007<Cite id="te-10y-2026-09-24" />. It had averaged 4.13% in February, before the war with Iran<Cite id="fred-gs10" />.
- The rise is mostly a repricing of real interest rates, not an inflation scare. From mid-July to Sept. 21 the 10-year real yield rose about 0.3 point while breakeven inflation rose about 0.1<Cite id="ycharts-10y" /><Cite id="ycharts-10y-real" />.
- TD Economics attributes about 50 basis points of the rise from late February to early September to a higher expected Fed path, about 20 to a larger term premium and only about 10 to inflation expectations<Cite id="td-bond-yields-2026-09" />.
- Three forces did most of the work: an oil shock from the Iran war, a Fed that has started hiking again, and record borrowing by Washington and AI companies.
- The politics rhyme with the 1970s, but market pricing so far looks more like 1987 and 1994. After eight of ten sell-off peaks since 1966, the 10-year was lower a year later; the two exceptions, 1966 and 1974, came during the Great Inflation<Cite id="fred-gs10" />.
- The next tests are the Oct. 28 Fed decision, six days before the Nov. 3 midterms, and the path of oil<Cite id="vantage-2026-09-24" /><Cite id="spectrum-dividend-2026" />.

## Oil, a hawkish Fed and heavy borrowing drove the rise

TD Economics puts most estimates of the term premium, the extra yield investors demand for holding long-term debt, at 80 to 100 basis points<Cite id="td-bond-yields-2026-09" />.

### The war pushed Brent up 63% by late April

Uncertainty over US-Iran talks kept oil elevated this week<Cite id="te-10y-2026-09-24" />.

### The Fed cut 1.75 points, yet the 10-year rose

The Fed began cutting in September 2024<Cite id="sbs-warsh-2026-06" /> and held its target at 3.5% to 3.75% from December 2025 until September<Cite id="euronews-fed-2026-09" />. Over that easing cycle the 10-year's monthly average rose from 3.72% to 4.14%<Cite id="fred-gs10" />, a sign that investors wanted more compensation to hold long-term debt.

On Sept. 16 the Fed voted unanimously to raise its target range to 3.75% to 4%, its first hike since 2023, and 16 of 18 officials see at least one more increase this year<Cite id="te-fed-2026-09-16" />. Chair Kevin Warsh again declined to submit projections<Cite id="te-fed-2026-09-16" />. Payrolls rose 162,000 in August against expectations of 55,000, and unemployment held at 4.1%<Cite id="firsttrust-payrolls-2026-08" />.

### Washington and AI companies are competing for the same savings

The federal deficit reached \$2.0 trillion in the first 11 months of the fiscal year<Cite id="cbo-mbr-2026-08" />, and gross federal debt has passed \$40 trillion<Cite id="crfb-deficit-2026-09" />. The Supreme Court's 6-3 ruling against the IEEPA tariffs in February exposed more than \$175 billion of collections to potential refunds<Cite id="reuters-tariffs-2026" /><Cite id="pwbm-tariffs-2026" />. Trump's promise of a \$5,000 dividend to every adult if Republicans keep Congress would cost more than \$1.2 trillion, by one estimate<Cite id="foxbiz-dividend-2026" /><Cite id="spectrum-dividend-2026" />.

AI companies have issued more than \$1.5 trillion of debt this year<Cite id="te-10y-2026-09-23" />. Foreign official holders now own 13% of publicly traded Treasuries, down from 15% three years ago, while investment funds take 67% of auction allotments<Cite id="td-bond-yields-2026-09" />. The Treasury sold two-year notes at 4.787% on Sept. 22, the highest in two years<Cite id="wolf-2026-09-23" />.

## The sell-off is global, and the US is leading it

Between the pre-war low and early September, 2-year yields rose about a full point in the US, Japan and the UK, and 10-year yields about 80 basis points<Cite id="td-bond-yields-2026-09" />.

Japan matters most for Treasuries. Its 10-year yield hit 3% on Sept. 1, the first time since 1996<Cite id="bnn-jgb-2026-09-01" />, and rose to about 3.08% after the Bank of Japan raised rates<Cite id="te-jgb-2026-09" />. ## Mortgages, the budget and stock valuations are feeling it

### Mortgage rates are up three-quarters of a point since February

Freddie Mac's 30-year rate bottomed at 5.98% in late February and reached 6.71% on Sept. 3<Cite id="freddie-2026-03-05" /><Cite id="freddie-2026-09-03" />. On a \$400,000 loan, that move adds about \$190 to the monthly payment. The weekly survey does not yet capture the mid-September jump in yields.

### A one-point miss on rates could add about \$3.8 trillion to deficits

<Chart src="./chart-12.csv" type="bar" title="Even before this rise in rates, CBO saw deficits reaching $3.1 trillion by 2036" x="fiscal_year" y="deficit_usd_tn" yLabel="Deficit ($ trillion)" caption="CBO February 2026 baseline by fiscal year; 2025 is actual. CBO's February baseline assumed a 4.1% 10-year yield this year<Cite id="crfb-outlook-2026" />. By CBO's rule of thumb, interest rates 0.1 point higher across the board would add \$379 billion to deficits over 2027 to 2036<Cite id="cbo-sensitivity-2026" />. Scaled linearly, a one-point miss sustained across all maturities implies roughly \$3.8 trillion of extra borrowing; that is an estimate, not a CBO projection.

Interest reached 18.5% of federal revenue by the end of 2025, above the previous record set in 1991, and annual interest costs have passed \$1 trillion<Cite id="pgpf-interest" />. CBO projects net interest rising from 3.3% of GDP this year to 4.6% in 2036, with debt held by the public reaching 120% of GDP, above the 1946 record of 106%<Cite id="cbo-outlook-2026" />. Nominal GDP grew at a 7.9% annualized rate in the second quarter<Cite id="bea-gdp-2026q2-advance" />, faster than the government's borrowing rate, a cushion that depends partly on inflation.

### Profits, not valuations, are holding stocks up

<Chart src="./chart-13.csv" type="line" title="Booming profits pulled the forward P/E from 22.5 to 19.1" x="date" y="forward_pe" yLabel="Forward 12-month P/E (times earnings)" caption="Forward price-to-earnings ratio of the S and P 500 from FactSet reports. The S&P 500 fell 0.8% to 7,706 on Sept. 23 and the small-cap Russell 2000 lost 1.8%, while the Nasdaq slipped 1.1% from a record<Cite id="ap-stocks-2026-09-23" />. Analysts expect S&P 500 earnings to grow 31.6% this year, which has pulled the forward P/E down to 19.1<Cite id="factset-ei-2026-09-11" />. But 19.1 times earnings is a 5.2% earnings yield, about the same as the 10-year, so the extra reward for owning stocks has nearly vanished.

JPMorgan argues that AI, health care and services make the economy less sensitive to rates, putting stocks' pain threshold nearer 5.5% to 6%<Cite id="reuters-5pct-2026-09" />. History is less comforting: world stocks halved after the 10-year last broke 5%, in 2007, and after a near-6.8% spike helped burst the dot-com bubble<Cite id="reuters-5pct-2026-09" />.

### The Treasury's attempts to cap yields have not worked

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent expanded buybacks of long-dated bonds in late August, funded with more bills, and floated using the Treasury's cash account; the relief was modest and brief<Cite id="td-bond-yields-2026-09" />. A buyback announced on Sept. 23 fell flat<Cite id="wolf-2026-09-23" />.

## The Fed's independence is being tested before the midterms

Trump said in February he would not have chosen Warsh had he wanted rate hikes<Cite id="sa-trump-warsh-2026-02" />. Warsh's first meeting as chair, in June, dropped the statement's easing bias and pointed to a hike as the next move<Cite id="sbs-warsh-2026-06" />. Three regional Fed presidents then dissented in favor of a hike in July<Cite id="euronews-fed-2026-09" />.

The September vote was 12-0<Cite id="schwab-fomc-2026-09" />. Trump responded that rates should be 1% or lower but stopped short of criticizing Warsh by name<Cite id="bloomberg-fed-2026-09" />. The next decision, on Oct. 28, comes six days before the Nov. 3 midterms<Cite id="vantage-2026-09-24" /><Cite id="spectrum-dividend-2026" />.

One guardrail held this summer. In Trump v. Cook, the Supreme Court ruled 5-4 that Fed governors can be removed only for cause, after notice and a hearing<Cite id="crs-cook-2026" />.

## History says the reason behind 5% matters more than the level

<Chart src="./chart-14.csv" type="line" title="At about 5%, today's yield is low by the standards of past peaks" x="month" y="yield_pct" yLabel="10-year Treasury yield (%)" caption="Monthly averages, sampled each quarter plus peak months. September 2026 runs through Sept. 23 and is computed from daily par yields." sources="fred-gs10 ycharts-10y" />

In nominal terms, 5% is unremarkable. The monthly average sat above 5% for most of 1968 to 2000 and peaked at 15.32% in September 1981<Cite id="fred-gs10" />. From mid-1967 it did not fall below 5% again until September 1998<Cite id="fred-gs10" />.

<Chart src="./chart-15.csv" type="bar" title="Yields fell in the year after 8 of 10 past peaks" x="peak" y="change_pts" yLabel="Change after 12 months (percentage points)" caption="Change in the monthly-average 10-year yield from each sell-off peak month to the same month a year later." sources="fred-gs10" />

Yields usually fell after a peak because the Fed stopped tightening or something broke. In 1966 and 1974 they kept rising, and they did not peak until 1981<Cite id="fred-gs10" />.

### The 1970s: the politics rhyme

Nixon pressed Fed Chair Arthur Burns toward highly expansionary policy before the 1972 election, the White House tapes show<Cite id="abrams-2006" />. After the 1973 oil shock the Fed raised rates from about 5% to 13%, stocks fell nearly 50% and a deep recession followed; the Fed then eased, and inflation stayed above 5% until 1978<Cite id="cme-1970s" />. In June, economist Brad DeLong accused Warsh of "cosplaying Arthur Burns"<Cite id="delong-2026-06" />; September's hike was a rebuttal. The key difference so far is that today's 10-year TIPS yield is the highest since 2008<Cite id="tipswatch-2026-09-17" />.

### 1987: a new chair's first test

Alan Greenspan took office as Fed chair on Aug. 11, 1987, and the Fed raised the discount rate on Sept. 4; the Dow fell 22% on Oct. 19, only 69 days into his tenure<Cite id="npr-greenspan" />. The 10-year peaked at 10.23% on Oct. 15 and fell to 8.72% by Oct. 26 as money fled to safety<Cite id="bs-wood-1987" />. Warsh is also a new chair proving himself, with stocks near records and a thin equity premium. Today's market, though, rests on profits growing about 30% a year<Cite id="factset-ei-2026-09-11" />.

### 1990: the war-and-oil template

Iraq's invasion of Kuwait on Aug. 2, 1990, sent oil prices sharply higher, and a recession followed that ended in March 1991<Cite id="npr-greenspan" />. The NBER dates that recession from July 1990, two months before the 10-year's monthly average peaked in September<Cite id="nber-cycles" /><Cite id="fred-gs10" />. The Fed looked through the price spike and cut, and the 10-year fell from about 9% to 6.6% by late 1992<Cite id="sc-gulf-war" />.

The same sequence held in 1973-74 and 1979-80: recessions began in November 1973 and January 1980, while the 10-year peaked in August 1974 and March 1980<Cite id="nber-cycles" /><Cite id="fred-gs10" />. In oil shocks, rising yields have not proved the economy was fine.

### 1984 and 1994: sell-offs that ended well

In 1984, Volcker later wrote, Treasury Secretary James Baker told him with President Reagan present: "The president is ordering you not to raise interest rates before the election"<Cite id="volcker-quotes" />. The Fed held its ground, and the 10-year's monthly average fell from 13.56% in June 1984 to 10.16% a year later<Cite id="fred-gs10" />.

In February 1994 the Fed began publicly announcing its rate changes with a hike<Cite id="npr-greenspan" />. The 10-year's monthly average rose from 5.33% in October 1993 to 7.96% in November 1994, then fell 2 points over the next year without a recession<Cite id="fred-gs10" /><Cite id="nber-cycles" />.

### 2007 and 2023: the last two visits to 5%

The 2007 breach was followed by the global financial crisis and a halving of world stocks<Cite id="reuters-5pct-2026-09" />; the NBER dates the recession from December 2007<Cite id="nber-cycles" />. In 2023 the monthly average peaked at 4.80% in October and fell to 4.02% by December<Cite id="fred-gs10" />. This time the Treasury has already used its buyback and bill-issuance tools, and the main driver is the expected Fed path<Cite id="td-bond-yields-2026-09" />.

## For now this looks more like 1994 than 1974

Real yields are rising, long-run inflation expectations are anchored and growth is strong. But almost every 1970s risk factor is present: war, an oil shock, a president demanding cheaper money before an election, big deficits and a promise of cash handouts.

Forecasters are split. TD expects cooling core inflation and oil to cap yields, though heavy borrowing keeps them elevated<Cite id="td-bond-yields-2026-09" />. Adam Posen expects inflation to hold at 3.5% to 4.5% until rate hikes bite<Cite id="posen-247-2026-09" />.

The paths below are judgment calls drawn from the episodes above, not sourced forecasts.

### Signposts

- **Breakeven inflation.** Near 2.3% now<Cite id="ycharts-10y" /><Cite id="ycharts-10y-real" />. A sustained climb toward 3% would mean markets doubt the Fed.
- **The dollar and gold.** On Sept. 23 gold slipped and the dollar index firmed to about 101.2 as yields rose<Cite id="riotimes-2026-09-24" />, the signature of a real-rate repricing. A credibility scare looks different: rising yields with a falling dollar and rising gold.
- **Auctions.** Watch tails, bid-to-cover ratios and Japanese demand as the Bank of Japan hikes.
- **Stocks.** With the earnings yield level with the 10-year, equities may become the release valve, as in 1987.
- **Oil.** A durable reopening of Hormuz would do much of the Fed's work for it.
- **Politics.** An October pause that looks political, or a dividend bill after Nov. 3.
