## Bottom line: global firms in francs

The Swiss equity market is a small group of global, mostly defensive multinationals that are domiciled, listed and priced in Switzerland. It tells an investor more about world demand for medicines, food, wealth management and electrification, and about the franc, than about the Swiss economy.

- **Three companies set the index.** Roche, Novartis and Nestlé were 47.9% of the SMI on 23 October 2026, using iShares SMI ETF holdings as a proxy, and 40.9% of MSCI Switzerland on 30 September; the ten largest stocks were 82.5% and 74.5% <Cite id="ishares-smi" /> <Cite id="msci-ch-usd" />. Among large national benchmarks only the FTSE 100 comes close, with a top 10 of 49.5% <Cite id="ftse100" />.
- **It is a health-care and defensive market.** Health care is 38.6% of MSCI Switzerland against 9.2% of MSCI World; with consumer staples, defensive sectors make up 52% against 14%. Information technology is 0.8% and energy is absent <Cite id="msci-ch-chf" /> <Cite id="msci-world" />.
- **The index is not the economy.** Pharma produces 6.6% of gross value added (GVA) and 1.2% of jobs, but 41% of goods exports and, with medtech, 39% of the index <Cite id="bfs-gva" /> <Cite id="bfs-statent" /> <Cite id="focbs-2025" />. Commodity trading (2.3% of GDP), the cantonal banks (40% of mortgages), the retail cooperatives and the private watch brands are largely absent from SIX <Cite id="srf-commodities" /> <Cite id="sba-barometer" />.
- **Revenue is earned abroad and reported in francs.** SMI companies earn about 90% of their sales outside Switzerland, according to a press estimate <Cite id="cash-10facts" />. The franc gained 47% against the euro between 2010 and 2025 on annual averages, so currency moves shape reported growth and foreign investors' returns: the SPI returned 17.8% in francs and 34.7% in dollars in 2025 <Cite id="snb-fx-annual" /> <Cite id="pictet-2026-web" />.
- **The economy is rich, open and steady.** GDP was CHF 881.6bn in 2025 (about USD 1.06trn, or USD 117,000 per head), real growth 1.6%, registered unemployment about 3% and gross public debt about 40% of GDP <Cite id="fso-ngdp25" /> <Cite id="imf-gdp-pc" /> <Cite id="seco-unemp" /> <Cite id="ffa-debt" />. The Swiss National Bank (SNB) policy rate has been 0% since June 2025, and CPI inflation was 1.0% in September 2026 <Cite id="snb-rates" /> <Cite id="fso-cpi-sep26" />.
- **High payout, middling price.** On 30 September 2026 MSCI Switzerland yielded 2.78% (MSCI World 1.52%) and traded at 17.0 times forward earnings, a 20% premium to Europe (14.2x) and a 5% discount to the World index (17.9x) <Cite id="msci-ch-eur" /> <Cite id="msci-europe" /> <Cite id="msci-world" />. Swiss companies paid a record USD 49.0bn of dividends in 2024 <Cite id="jhgdi-45" />.
- **Long-run returns are median, falls are shallower.** Swiss equities returned 6.8% a year nominal and 4.6% real from 1900 to 2025, about 2.9 points a year more than Swiss government bonds <Cite id="pictet-2026-pdf" />. In 2007–09 MSCI Switzerland fell 52% in dollars, against 58% for MSCI World <Cite id="msci-ch-usd" />.
- **Control is concentrated.** A family pool holds 64.97% of Roche's voting shares, the Rupert family 50.6% of Richemont's votes, two families 69.5% of Schindler's votes and the Confederation 51% of Swisscom <Cite id="roche-ar-2025" /> <Cite id="richemont-capital" /> <Cite id="schindler-ar24" /> <Cite id="vontobel-swisscom" />.
- **The main risks are decided in Washington and Bern.** They are US tariffs (up to 12.5% on most goods since 24 July 2026) and US drug pricing; UBS's extra capital requirement, about USD 16bn after the Council of States' vote of 23 September 2026; and a referendum on the Bilaterals III package with the EU, likely in 2027–28 <Cite id="seco-us-trade" /> <Cite id="swissinfo-ubs-sep26" /> <Cite id="lenews-eu" />.

## Pharma exports drive a rich economy

Switzerland is a small, rich and very open economy. A few multinational sectors, above all pharmaceuticals, dominate its output growth, its exports and the volatility of its statistics, while domestic demand grows steadily. The franc and the SNB set the conditions for every listed company.

### Rich, small and steady

Nominal gross domestic product (GDP) was CHF 881.6bn in 2025, up 2.4%, and real GDP grew 1.6% <Cite id="fso-ngdp25" />. In dollars that is about USD 1.06trn, the world's 20th-largest economy by our count from IMF data, and USD 117,000 per head, fourth after Liechtenstein, Luxembourg and Ireland <Cite id="imf-gdp-usd" /> <Cite id="imf-gdp-pc" />. The resident population reached 9.13m at the end of 2025, growing 0.8% a year mainly through immigration; in 2025 people aged 65 and over outnumbered those under 20 for the first time <Cite id="seco-pop" /> <Cite id="fso-pop" />.

Growth is steady rather than fast. Real GDP grew 1.0–1.6% a year in 2023–25, and the IMF series shows only one contraction since 2015, in 2020 <Cite id="fso-ngdp25" /> <Cite id="imf-growth" />. Quarterly data are lumpy: GDP adjusted for sporting events rose 1.5% in Q2 2026, the strongest quarter since Q3 2021, but the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) says nearly half came from the volatile chemical-pharmaceutical industry and that the quarter "does not represent a boom" <Cite id="seco-gdp-q" /> <Cite id="cash-seco" />. International sports federations based in Switzerland add another distortion, because their revenue from the Olympic Games and football World Cups is booked in Swiss GDP <Cite id="seco-gdp-q" /> <Cite id="fso-ngdp25" />.

Forecasters now cluster at 1.5–1.9% growth for 2026, up from 0.8–0.9% in early summer, mainly because of the pharma-driven second quarter and a weaker franc <Cite id="cash-seco" /> <Cite id="kof-sep26" />. Registered unemployment was 3.0% in September 2026 (3.1% seasonally adjusted), and the broader International Labour Organization (ILO) measure was 5.2% in Q2 2026 <Cite id="seco-unemp" /> <Cite id="eurostat-unemp" />. Both are above their 2022–23 lows, while vacancies rose 18% year on year, which in our view points to mismatch rather than weak demand.

Multinationals make Swiss data unusually hard to read. The Federal Statistical Office (FSO) attributes its large GDP revisions to multinational groups' business models, licence and patent income and restructuring, and is setting up a dedicated "Large Case Unit" to handle them <Cite id="fso-ngdp25" />.

### Pharma dominates goods exports

On the customs "business-cycle" basis, which excludes gold, other precious metals, gems, art and antiques, Swiss goods exports reached a record CHF 287.0bn in 2025 (+1.4%), against imports of CHF 232.7bn <Cite id="focbs-2025" />. Chemicals and pharma made up 53% of the total and pharmaceutical products alone 41%; antisera and vaccines were 20% <Cite id="focbs-2025" /> <Cite id="scienceind-2025" />. Gold flows are large enough to distort the headline: including gold and other valuables, exports were CHF 461.2bn <Cite id="focbs-annual" />.

Goods are only part of the external picture. The current-account surplus was CHF 49.6bn, or 5.6% of GDP, in 2025, after 9.3% in 2024 <Cite id="snb-bop" />. Merchanting, the margin earned by Swiss-based traders on goods that never cross the Swiss border, brought in a net CHF 63.6bn, more than the entire customs trade surplus of CHF 54.3bn <Cite id="snb-bop" /> <Cite id="focbs-2025" />. Licence fees (CHF 29.9bn) and financial services (CHF 24.4bn) were the largest services exports, although the services balance as a whole is negative <Cite id="snb-bop" />.

Why does a country of 9m people host so many global groups? We think five things work together. Tax: the Confederation levies a flat 8.5% profit tax and the cantons compete with their own rates (secondary source) <Cite id="wiki-tax" />; since 2024 large groups pay at least the OECD's 15% minimum, which 78% of voters approved in 2023 <Cite id="wiki-ref-2023" /> <Cite id="ey-pillar2" />. Innovation and skills: Switzerland topped WIPO's Global Innovation Index for the 16th consecutive year in 2026, and immigration supplies specialist staff <Cite id="wipo-gii" />. Money: a hard currency, a 10-year government bond yield of about 0.5% and a large wealth-management industry make capital cheap and plentiful <Cite id="te-bond10y" /> <Cite id="sba-barometer" />. History: Basel's 19th-century chemical industry is the root of today's pharma cluster. Institutions: political stability, neutrality and predictable courts are widely cited, though we found no single measure of them. Licence-fee receipts, and head-office, legal and consulting activity worth 4.3% of GVA in 2024, show how much intellectual property and management is booked in Switzerland <Cite id="snb-bop" /> <Cite id="eurostat-a64" />.

### The franc and SNB set conditions

The SNB defines price stability as CPI inflation between 0% and 2% <Cite id="snb-remarks-sep26" />. Its policy rate was negative from 2015 to mid-2022 (−0.75%), rose to 1.75% in 2023 and was cut back to 0% by June 2025 <Cite id="snb-rates" />. It held at 0% on 24 September 2026, while during 2026 the European Central Bank (ECB) raised its deposit rate to 2.50% and the Federal Reserve its target range to 3.75–4.00%; the next SNB assessment is on 10 December 2026 <Cite id="snb-mpa-sep26" /> <Cite id="snb-rates" />. CPI inflation rose to 1.0% in September 2026, a two-year high driven by fuel; core inflation was 0.5%, and the SNB saw no second-round effects <Cite id="snb-cpi" /> <Cite id="fso-cpi-sep26" /> <Cite id="snb-zus" />.

The SNB leans against unwanted appreciation by buying foreign currency. It bought CHF 5.2bn in 2025 and CHF 5.4bn in H1 2026, after selling CHF 132.9bn in 2023 <Cite id="snb-fxtr" />. Its balance sheet was CHF 951bn in August 2026, about 108% of 2025 GDP (our calculation) <Cite id="snb-bs" />. Profits swing with the franc, gold and equity markets: CHF 80.7bn in 2024, CHF 26.1bn in 2025 and CHF 25.2bn in H1 2026 <Cite id="snb-result-2025" /> <Cite id="snb-h1-2026" />. It paid CHF 4bn to the Confederation and cantons for 2025, and a new agreement allows up to CHF 6bn a year for 2026–2030 <Cite id="snb-result-2025" /> <Cite id="ffa-snb" />.

### Tariffs changed basis four times

The US has changed the legal basis of its tariffs on Swiss goods four times since August 2025. A 39% tariff under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) took effect on 7 August 2025, the highest on any developed economy, and covered nearly two-thirds of Swiss goods exports to the US; pharmaceuticals were exempt <Cite id="lombard-odier" />. A non-binding joint statement of 14 November 2025 capped the all-in rate at 15% and committed Switzerland to encourage at least USD 200bn of investment in the US <Cite id="seco-us-trade" /> <Cite id="economiesuisse-us" />. After the Supreme Court ruled on 20 February 2026 that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs, a temporary 10% surcharge under Section 122 applied until 24 July 2026. Since then a Section 301 duty of up to 12.5%, including the normal most-favoured-nation (MFN) duty, applies, with pharmaceuticals and gold exempt <Cite id="orrick-scotus" /> <Cite id="seco-us-trade" /> <Cite id="gt-301" />.

Patented medicines have their own regime. A Section 232 proclamation of 2 April 2026 sets a default tariff of 100%, 15% for Switzerland and 0% until 20 January 2029 for companies with both an MFN pricing agreement and a US onshoring agreement <Cite id="ey-232" />. Roche's US unit Genentech and Novartis signed MFN pricing agreements in December 2025 <Cite id="wh-mfn" />. Exports to the US fell 22% quarter on quarter in Q2 2025, after front-loading, and another 9% in Q3 2025, and rose again in May and June 2026 <Cite id="focbs-2025" /> <Cite id="focbs-jul26" />. In our view the instability of US policy matters more than the level of the rate: no binding agreement existed at the end of October 2026, and a second Section 301 investigation, into industrial overcapacity, remains open <Cite id="seco-us-trade" />.

### Strong finances, low yields

General government gross debt was 40.7% of GDP in 2025, and the general government ran a surplus of about CHF 4bn <Cite id="ffa-debt" />. On IMF data that is about a third of the French, US or Italian ratios (115.5%, 124.4% and 137.1%) and well below Germany's 62.7% <Cite id="imf-debt" />. A constitutional debt brake, approved by voters in 2001 and applied since 2003, requires a cyclically balanced federal budget (secondary source) <Cite id="wiki-debtbrake" />. Pressure comes from spending rules, not from markets: a 13th annual old-age (AHV) pension, approved by voters in March 2024, is first paid in December 2026 <Cite id="bluewin-ahv" />. The 10-year Confederation bond yielded about 0.46% on 27 October 2026 (third-party quote), against a monthly low of −0.98% in August 2019 <Cite id="te-bond10y" /> <Cite id="snb-bond" />. All three major rating agencies rate Switzerland AAA with stable outlooks, according to secondary compilations <Cite id="cnb-ratings" />.

## Much of the economy is not listed

Switzerland's listed market and its economy overlap only in part. About half of GVA comes from industries with little or no listed exposure: wholesale and commodity trading, real estate, public administration, health services, education, construction, retail, transport and hospitality (our estimate from 2024 FSO shares) <Cite id="bfs-gva" />. Conversely, a few export industries with small domestic footprints carry most of the index weight.

The gap is a global-multinational effect, not a measurement error. Health care's index weight is about six times pharma's share of GVA, and consumer staples' weight about six times the share of food manufacturing (our calculation) <Cite id="bfs-gva" /> <Cite id="msci-ch-chf" />. Roche, Novartis and Nestlé are valued on their worldwide earnings, while real estate, 7.3% of GVA including the imputed rents of owner-occupiers, gets 0.6% of the index and wholesale trade, 10.7% of GVA, almost nothing. Listed coverage is best where firms are large: 83% of pharma jobs, 82% of insurance jobs and 80% of telecom jobs are in companies with 250 or more staff, against 12% in construction and 9% in real estate <Cite id="bfs-statent" />.

### Pharma is the growth engine

Pharma's GVA doubled from CHF 27.4bn in 2014 to CHF 55.0bn in 2024, with real growth of 13.9% a year against 2.0% for the whole economy <Cite id="bfs-gva" />. By our calculation from FSO data it supplied about 40% of cumulative real GVA growth over 2014–24; the industry association Interpharma puts its share of GDP growth over ten years at "almost half", using a different period and data vintage <Cite id="bfs-gva" /> <Cite id="interpharma" />. It does this with about 58,000 employees in 343 companies, 1.2% of market-sector jobs <Cite id="bfs-statent" />. Interpharma warns that pharma employment growth stalled in 2020–22, that "major investment projects are taking place abroad", and that three-quarters of pharma exports face uncertainty from US tariffs and drug pricing <Cite id="interpharma" />.

Chemicals outside pharma are small at home, 0.9% of GVA in 2024, but exported about CHF 34bn in 2025 (our calculation) and include global leaders such as Givaudan and Sika <Cite id="bfs-gva" /> <Cite id="scienceind-2025" />. Two large Swiss-based chemical groups have no primary listing on SIX: Syngenta, whose chief executive said in February 2026 that no listing decision had been taken, and dsm-firmenich, whose primary listing is in Amsterdam and which began trading on SIX in May 2026 <Cite id="swissinfo-ipo" />.

Medtech employs just under 72,000 people in about 1,400 companies, 95% of them small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), with revenue of CHF 26bn and exports of about CHF 12bn <Cite id="medtech-2026" />. Its association reports that 43% of companies have no investment plans, the highest share since its survey began <Cite id="medtech-2026" />. The listed leaders, Alcon, Sonova and Straumann, earn almost all their revenue abroad.

### Watches, machinery and SMEs

Swiss watch exports were CHF 25.6bn in 2025, down 1.7% and below the 2023 record of CHF 26.7bn; in January–September 2026 they rose 2.7% <Cite id="fh-markets" />. The industry sells fewer and dearer watches: wristwatch exports fell from 29.7m units in 2000 to 14.6m in 2025, while watches priced above CHF 3,000 rose to 80% of export value on 13% of units <Cite id="fh-price" />. The US is the largest market at 17% of exports, and exports to China fell by more than a third in two years <Cite id="fh-world-2025" />.

The most valuable brands are private. Rolex belongs to a foundation, and Patek Philippe and Audemars Piguet to their founding families, so listed exposure is limited to Richemont, 5.95% of MSCI Switzerland, and Swatch Group <Cite id="msci-ch-chf" />. In September 2026 exports rose 11.7%, helped by an 85.7% jump to the US against a weak, tariff-hit base, while exports to China fell 14.2% <Cite id="fh-sep26" />.

The machinery, electrical and metals (MEM) industry is the largest industrial employer, with 320,000 jobs in Switzerland and 80% of output exported, according to its association Swissmem <Cite id="swissmem" />. Excluding watches and electronics, its share of GVA fell from 5.2% in 2014 to 4.1% in 2024 as nominal value added barely grew, which we attribute mainly to the strong franc <Cite id="bfs-gva" />. In Q3 2025 its capacity utilisation was 80.7% against a long-term average of 86.2%, and machine-tool exports to the US fell 43% under the 39% tariff <Cite id="kmu-mem" />. The SME base is wide and almost entirely private: 99.7% of Switzerland's 631,794 market enterprises have fewer than 250 employees, and they employ 66% of the workforce <Cite id="bfs-statent" />.

### Finance: wealth and reinsurance

Banks in Switzerland held a record CHF 9,729bn of client assets at the end of 2025 and passed CHF 10trn in H1 2026; 45.9% belonged to foreign clients <Cite id="sba-barometer" />. Cross-border private wealth was CHF 2,946bn in 2025, but Boston Consulting Group (BCG) found that Hong Kong overtook Switzerland as the largest cross-border wealth centre for the first time, with about USD 2.9trn <Cite id="sba-barometer" /> <Cite id="bcg-wealth" />. Banks employed 92,002 full-time staff, and banks, insurers and auxiliary services together produced 8.7% of GVA <Cite id="sba-barometer" /> <Cite id="bfs-gva" />.

The listed banking sector captures wealth management well and domestic banking poorly. Since the Credit Suisse takeover the "big banks" category is UBS alone, earning 43.3% of the banking sector's net income, while the cantonal banks, mostly state-owned and largely unlisted, hold 40.4% of domestic mortgages <Cite id="sba-barometer" />.

Insurance produced CHF 26.6bn of GVA in 2025 with 1% of the workforce, about 2.6 times average productivity <Cite id="svv-2026" />. Zurich is one of the world's largest reinsurance centres: 45 reinsurers booked CHF 60bn of global premiums in Switzerland in 2024 and generated 79% of insurance-services exports <Cite id="svv-2026" />. Much of the domestic market is mutual, cooperative, public or foreign-owned, from the health insurers to the accident insurer Suva <Cite id="svv-2026" />.

### What the market leaves out

Commodity trading is the clearest gap. The FSO's first official estimate puts its 2024 value added at CHF 19.2bn, 2.3% of GDP and almost as much as tourism (2.9%), concentrated in Geneva (58%) and Zug (27%) <Cite id="srf-commodities" /> <Cite id="bluenews-commodities" />. The large trading houses are private, and Glencore, which is based in Baar, is listed in London <Cite id="nasdaq-glencore" />. The retail cooperatives Migros and Coop, the federal railways and post office, cantonal utilities and banks, and the Raiffeisen cooperative banks are also outside the market.

We see three structural reasons (our interpretation). First, ownership: foundations, families, partners, cooperatives and governments own these businesses and have no need for public equity. Second, business models: trading houses and private banks prize confidentiality and have long been run as partnerships. Third, scale: the listed market selects large, intellectual-property-rich global groups, and few SMEs reach that size. Several Swiss-domiciled companies are listed abroad, among them Chubb, Garmin, Glencore and On, so SIX also understates the Swiss corporate footprint.

## Index rules cap a top-heavy market

SIX Swiss Exchange lists about 200 domestic companies, worth USD 2.5trn at the end of 2025, about 2.4 times GDP (our calculation) <Cite id="wb-listed" /> <Cite id="wb-mcap" />. Because three companies dominate, its benchmarks are built to contain concentration: the SMI caps any issuer at 18% and the SLI at 9%. Investors choose between capped, uncapped and broad versions, and that choice changes their exposure.

### SIX is owned by its users

SIX Swiss Exchange belongs to SIX Group, an unlisted company owned by about 120 banks and other financial institutions that are also its main users; no shareholder or bank category has a majority <Cite id="six-ar25" />. At the end of 2025 the big banks held 34.5%, commercial and investment banks 18.7%, cantonal banks 14.3% and foreign banks 14.0% <Cite id="six-ar25" />. SIX Group also owns Spain's exchange operator BME, bought in 2020, and since July 2025 the pan-European venue operator Aquis <Cite id="wiki-sixgroup" /> <Cite id="six-ar25" />. It earned net operating income of CHF 1.5bn in 2025 <Cite id="six-kf25" />.

Shares trade in francs. The Geneva, Basel and Zurich exchanges merged in the 1990s, trading became fully electronic in the mid-1990s, and the exchange, the securities depository and the data provider combined into SIX in 2008 <Cite id="wiki-six" />. Trades settle two business days after execution (T+2) at SIX SIS; Switzerland moves to T+1 on 11 October 2027, together with the EU and UK <Cite id="six-ar25" /> <Cite id="thetrade-t1" />. A second exchange, BX Swiss, and an SME segment, Sparks, with four companies at the end of 2025, complete the picture <Cite id="bx-swiss" /> <Cite id="six-ar25" />.

### The SMI caps any stock at 18%

All SIX equity benchmarks start from the Swiss Performance Index (SPI), which holds all primary-listed Swiss equities with at least 20% free float, excludes investment companies and weights stocks by free-float market value <Cite id="six-rulebook" />. Indices with fixed membership are drawn from one quarterly selection list that ranks stocks half on 12-month average free-float capitalisation and half on 12-month order-book turnover, with buffers against churn <Cite id="six-rulebook" />. Membership is reviewed once a year and changes on the third Friday of September <Cite id="six-rulebook" />.

### Liquid but fragmented trading

Equity turnover on SIX Swiss Exchange was CHF 874.8bn in 2025 (+10.5%) and CHF 723.6bn in January–September 2026 (+8.8% on a year earlier); exchange-traded funds turned over a near-record CHF 124.3bn in 2025 <Cite id="six-fy25" /> <Cite id="six-sep26" />. On World Federation of Exchanges data, annual trading fell from 50–65% of market value in 2015–20 to 35–38% in 2021–25, partly because trading moved to other venues <Cite id="wb-traded" /> <Cite id="wb-mcap" />.

Fragmentation follows politics. When the EU let its recognition of Swiss stock-exchange rules ("equivalence") lapse on 30 June 2019, Switzerland barred EU venues from trading most Swiss shares, and SIX's share of trading jumped to almost 100% <Cite id="sif" /> <Cite id="thetrade-six" />. UK venues returned in February 2021, and Switzerland lifted the measure against the EU from 1 May 2025, although the EU still had not recognised Swiss equivalence <Cite id="sif" />. SIX's share of trading in the 30 SLI stocks fell to 64.4% in 2025 from 68.5% <Cite id="six-kf25" />.

The primary market is thin. Listed domestic companies fell from 234 in 2015 to 201 in 2025 <Cite id="wb-listed" />. SMG Swiss Marketplace Group raised about CHF 1bn in September 2025, Europe's second-largest IPO that year, but there were no Swiss IPOs in Q1 2026 and only three small or technical listings in Q2 <Cite id="six-ar25" /> <Cite id="swissinfo-ipo" />.

### Taxes, access and control

Swiss dividends carry a 35% withholding tax; investors from treaty countries such as the US, UK, Germany and France can usually reclaim it down to 15% <Cite id="pwc-wht" />. Repayments of capital contribution reserves (Kapitaleinlagereserven, KER) are free of withholding tax, but since 2020 listed companies must pay at least as much in taxable dividends, or half of the shortfall becomes taxable <Cite id="estv-ker" />. That is why Lonza and Sika paid half of their 2025 dividends from these reserves <Cite id="lonza-fy25" /> <Cite id="sika-fy25" />. Private investors resident in Switzerland pay no capital gains tax <Cite id="pwc-ind" />.

Share structures separate control from economic ownership. Swiss companies can issue registered shares, bearer shares and non-voting participation certificates; index free float is measured on voting lines only, and an issuer's lines are combined for capping <Cite id="six-rulebook" />. Crossing a third of the votes triggers a mandatory takeover offer, but a company's articles can raise the threshold to 49% ("opting up") or remove it ("opting out"); the Takeover Board ruled on opting-out clauses proposed by dormakaba, SHL Telemedicine and WISeKey in 2026 <Cite id="cms-takeover" /> <Cite id="takeover-board" />. The market even lists the SNB, whose private shareholders' votes are capped at 100 shares <Cite id="wiki-snb" />.

## Leaders that earn little at home

The corporate landscape is a set of global category leaders with Swiss head offices. They earn most of their revenue in the US, Europe and Asia, report in francs, dollars or euros, and several are controlled by families or the state. ### Health care: pharma plus niches

**Roche** sells patented medicines (Pharmaceuticals, CHF 47.7bn or 77% of 2025 sales of CHF 61.5bn) and laboratory diagnostics (CHF 13.8bn, 23%) <Cite id="roche-fy25" />. Its largest drugs in 2025 were Ocrevus for multiple sclerosis (CHF 7.0bn), Hemlibra for haemophilia (CHF 4.8bn) and the eye drug Vabysmo (CHF 4.1bn); oncology and haematology were about half of H1 2026 pharma sales <Cite id="roche-fy25" /> <Cite id="roche-hy26" />. The US took about 47% of group sales in H1 2026 and China about 7% (our calculation), and the core operating margin was 39.0%; the stronger franc cut reported sales growth by 8 points <Cite id="roche-hy26" />. Roche is often described as the world's leading supplier of cancer medicines and ranked 45th among the world's companies by market value in H1 2026 <Cite id="wiki-roche" /> <Cite id="swissinfo-ey-top100" />. Its index line is a non-voting participation certificate: in March 2026 the old dividend-right certificates (Genussscheine) were exchanged for 702,562,700 participation certificates, traded as ROP (ISIN CH1499059983 in SIX's index notice) <Cite id="roche-hy26" /> <Cite id="six-review-2026" />. Votes sit with the 106.7m bearer shares, of which the Hoffmann and Oeri family pool held 64.97% at the end of 2025 <Cite id="roche-ar-2025" />. Roche has raised its dividend 39 years in a row and is 17.9% of the SMI <Cite id="roche-fy25" /> <Cite id="ishares-smi" />.

**Novartis** has been a pure innovative-medicines company since spinning off Alcon in April 2019 and Sandoz in October 2023 <Cite id="wiki-novartis" />. Its 2025 net sales were USD 54.5bn, with a core operating margin of 40.1% <Cite id="novartis-fy25" />. The portfolio is changing: Entresto for heart failure (USD 7.7bn in 2025) lost US exclusivity and fell 45% at constant currency in 9M 2026, while Kisqali (breast cancer), Kesimpta (multiple sclerosis) and Cosentyx (immunology) made up about a third of 9M 2026 sales (our calculation) <Cite id="novartis-q3-26" />. In 9M 2026 the US was about 41% of sales, Europe 31% and China 9% (our calculation) <Cite id="novartis-q3-26-ifr" />. On 27 October 2026 it raised its 2026 guidance for core operating income to low-single-digit growth, despite a USD 1.9bn impairment after a failed trial of a drug from the USD 12bn Avidity acquisition <Cite id="novartis-q3-26" />. It has raised its dividend 29 years running, had USD 4.9bn left of a USD 10bn buyback on 30 September 2026, has no controlling shareholder and is 16.0% of the SMI <Cite id="novartis-q3-26" /> <Cite id="ishares-smi" />.

**Lonza** makes biologic and small-molecule drugs under contract for pharma and biotech clients, as a contract development and manufacturing organisation (CDMO) <Cite id="lonza-fy25" />. 2025 sales from continuing operations were CHF 6.5bn (+21.7% at constant exchange rates), with a core EBITDA margin of 31.6%; Integrated Biologics was about 56% of sales, Advanced Synthesis 25% and Specialized Modalities 19% (our calculation) <Cite id="lonza-fy25" />. It calls itself the largest biologics CDMO in the US, helped by the Vacaville plant bought from Roche's Genentech in 2024, and sold 46% in Europe, 41% in the Americas and 13% in Asia-Pacific in 2025 (our calculation) <Cite id="lonza-fy25" /> <Cite id="lonza-sr25" />. Weight: 2.9% of the SMI.

**Galderma** is the largest independent dermatology company <Cite id="wiki-galderma" />. Of its 2025 sales of USD 5.2bn (+17.7% at constant currency), injectable aesthetics such as Dysport and Restylane were 49%, dermatological skincare such as Cetaphil 28%, and prescription dermatology 23% <Cite id="galderma-fy25" />. The eczema drug Nemluvio drives growth: prescription dermatology rose 64% in 9M 2026, and on 22 October 2026 the company raised its 2026 growth guidance to 22.5–23.5% <Cite id="galderma-q3-26" />. The US was 43% of 2025 sales <Cite id="galderma-fy25" />. Sold by Nestlé to an EQT-led consortium in 2019 and re-listed in March 2024, it is 20% owned by L'Oréal and joined the SMI in September 2026, with a 2.4% weight on 23 October <Cite id="wiki-galderma" /> <Cite id="swissinfo-loreal" /> <Cite id="ishares-smi" />.

**Sandoz**, spun off from Novartis in October 2023, sells generic medicines (USD 7.8bn, 70% of 2025 sales of USD 11.1bn) and biosimilars (USD 3.3bn, 30%) <Cite id="sandoz-fy25" />. It is mainly European: 54% of 2025 sales came from Europe, 22% from North America and 25% from the rest of the world (our calculation) <Cite id="sandoz-fy25-regions" />. Generics and biosimilars fall outside the US Section 232 pharma tariff <Cite id="ey-232" />. It joined the SMI in September 2026 <Cite id="six-review-2026" />.

**Alcon**, spun off from Novartis in 2019 and listed on SIX and the New York Stock Exchange, makes eye-surgery equipment and lenses (Surgical, 56% of 2025 sales of USD 10.3bn) and contact lenses and eye drops (Vision Care, 44%) <Cite id="alcon-fy25" />. The US was 45% of sales <Cite id="alcon-fy25" />. It expects 5–7% sales growth at constant currency in 2026 <Cite id="alcon-q2-26" />.

**Sonova** (hearing aids, own audiology shops and cochlear implants; CHF 3.6bn of sales in the year to March 2026) calls itself the global leader in hearing care; it left the SMI in 2025 and is selling its consumer headphone business <Cite id="sonova-fy26" /> <Cite id="wiki-smi" />. **Straumann** (dental implants and clear aligners; CHF 2.6bn of 2025 revenue, 8.9% organic growth, a 25.2% core EBIT margin) sells 42% in Europe, the Middle East and Africa (EMEA) and 26% in North America <Cite id="straumann-fy25" />.

### Consumer: Nestlé and luxury

**Nestlé** is the world's largest listed food company <Cite id="wiki-nestle" />. Its 2025 sales of CHF 89.5bn split into coffee and other beverages (28%), petcare (21%), nutrition and health science (16%), prepared dishes (11%), milk products and ice cream (11%), confectionery (10%) and water (3%) (our calculation) <Cite id="nestle-fy25-mp" />. Organic growth was 3.5%, but volume contributed only 0.8 points, and currency took 5.7 points off reported sales, which fell 2.0% <Cite id="nestle-fy25-rtrs" />. Zone Americas was 39% of sales, Asia, Oceania and Africa 23% and Europe 20% <Cite id="nestle-fy25" />. Its underlying trading operating margin fell to 16.1% from 17.2% <Cite id="nestle-fy25-rtrs" />. Governance has been turbulent: chief executive Laurent Freixe was dismissed in September 2025 and replaced by Philipp Navratil, Pablo Isla became chairman, and the company is cutting 16,000 jobs and targeting CHF 3bn of savings by the end of 2027 <Cite id="wiki-nestle" /> <Cite id="swissinfo-nestle-jobs" />. It is placing waters in a joint venture and selling its remaining ice cream and mainstream vitamins businesses <Cite id="nestle-hy26" />. Nestlé is 14.1% of the SMI, and its 21% share-price fall in 2024 explains much of that year's Swiss underperformance <Cite id="ishares-smi" /> <Cite id="swisslife-smi" />.

**Richemont** sells jewellery and watches through its own boutiques (71% of sales), wholesale (23%) and online (6%) <Cite id="richemont-fy26" />. In the year to March 2026, sales were EUR 22.4bn (+11% at constant currency); the jewellery houses Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels made 74% of sales at a 30.5% operating margin, while the specialist watchmakers (Vacheron Constantin, IWC, Jaeger-LeCoultre and others) made 14% at 3.4% <Cite id="richemont-fy26" /> <Cite id="gjepc-richemont" />. Asia-Pacific was 32% of sales, the Americas 25%, Europe 24%, Japan 10% and the Middle East and Africa 9% <Cite id="richemont-fy26" />. Sales rose 20% at constant currency in April–June 2026 <Cite id="richemont-q1-27" />. Compagnie Financière Rupert, controlled by the chairman's family, holds 10.18% of the capital but 50.60% of the votes <Cite id="richemont-capital" />. Weight: 5.7% of the SMI.

**Swatch Group** makes watches at all price points, from Swatch to Omega and Breguet, and owns most of its production <Cite id="wiki-swatch" />. Its 2025 sales were CHF 6.3bn; 48% went to Asia (Greater China 23%, down from 27% in 2024), 29% to Europe (Switzerland 8%) and 21% to the Americas <Cite id="swatch-ar25" />. Its operating margin fell to 2.1% in 2025 and 1.7% in H1 2026, partly because it kept production staff without short-time work <Cite id="timeandwatches-swatch" /> <Cite id="swatch-hy26" />. It has two listed share classes and left the SMI in 2021 <Cite id="wiki-smi" />. **Lindt & Sprüngli** (premium chocolate; CHF 5.9bn of 2025 sales, half in Europe and 37% in North America) raised prices 19% in 2025 as cocoa costs soared, then cut its 2026 organic growth guidance to 0–2% on 29 September 2026 <Cite id="foodnav-lindt" /> <Cite id="lindt-fy25" /> <Cite id="lindt-guidance" />.

### Financials: wealth and risk

**UBS** is a wealth-management-led universal bank and, since absorbing Credit Suisse in 2023, Switzerland's only big bank <Cite id="sba-barometer" />. Its 2025 net profit was USD 7.8bn, and group invested assets passed USD 7trn <Cite id="ubs-highlights" />. In 2Q26, Global Wealth Management (GWM) produced about 52% of revenue and pre-tax profit, the Investment Bank 27% and 32%, the Swiss Personal and Corporate bank about 14% and 19%, and Asset Management about 6% (our calculation) <Cite id="ubs-2q26" />. Wealth assets are booked mainly in the Americas: at the end of 2025, 48% of GWM invested assets were there, 19% in Switzerland, 17% in Asia-Pacific and 16% in the rest of EMEA (our calculation) <Cite id="ubs-4q25-6k" />. The Credit Suisse integration is nearly complete, with client migration finished in March 2026 and USD 12.6bn of a USD 13.5bn cost-saving target achieved; the CET1 capital ratio was 14.4% on 30 June 2026 <Cite id="ubs-2q26" />. UBS is 7.7% of the SMI, and its capital requirement is the largest open policy question in the market (section 7).

**Zurich Insurance Group** is a global property-and-casualty (P&C) and life insurer. Business operating profit (BOP) reached a record USD 8.9bn in 2025, and net income USD 6.8bn <Cite id="zurich-fy25" /> <Cite id="insbiz-zurich" />. Farmers, a US fee business that manages the Farmers Exchanges without owning them, contributed 27% of BOP <Cite id="zurich-pres-25" /> <Cite id="zurich-9m25" />. North America produced about half of P&C operating profit, and about 57% of group BOP including Farmers (our calculation) <Cite id="zurich-pres-25" />. H1 2026 BOP rose 13% to USD 4.8bn, with a combined ratio of 92.7% and a Swiss Solvency Test ratio of 266% <Cite id="vz-zurich" />. Zurich completed its GBP 8.1bn takeover of the Lloyd's specialty insurer Beazley on 1 October 2026 <Cite id="insbiz-beazley" />. Weight: 6.1% of the SMI.

**Swiss Re** is one of the world's largest reinsurers. Its 2025 net income was USD 4.8bn (+47%) on insurance revenue of USD 43.1bn, with a P&C reinsurance combined ratio of 79.4% <Cite id="swissre-fy25" />. P&C reinsurance was about 43% of revenue, life and health reinsurance 38% and the Corporate Solutions commercial unit 18%; the Americas were 49% of revenue (US 39%), EMEA 34% and Asia-Pacific 17% (our calculations) <Cite id="swissre-fy25" /> <Cite id="swissre-ar25" />. It targets net income of at least USD 4.5bn in 2026, but property-catastrophe prices fell about 16% at the 2026 renewals <Cite id="swissre-hy26" /> <Cite id="artemis-cat" />. Weight: 3.0% of the SMI.

**Partners Group** manages USD 185bn of private-markets assets: private equity 43%, infrastructure 22%, private credit 22% and real estate 13% (30 June 2026) <Cite id="pg-hy26" />. Of its 2025 revenue of CHF 2.6bn, 68% were recurring management fees and 32% volatile performance fees, at an EBITDA margin of 63% <Cite id="pg-fy25" />. Clients are mostly European: 58% of assets come from Europe, including 15% from Switzerland, and 23% from North America <Cite id="pg-ar25" />. It is an SMI member.

**Swiss Life** (life insurance, pensions and asset management; net profit CHF 1.26bn in 2025) earns 46% of its segment results in Switzerland, 21% in asset management, 17% in France and 10% in Germany (our calculation) <Cite id="swisslife-fy25" />. **Julius Baer**, a pure wealth manager, had CHF 547bn of client assets in mid-2026; Swiss-booked entities generate about half of its operating income <Cite id="jb-hy26" /> <Cite id="jb-ar25" />. **Helvetia Baloise**, formed when Helvetia and Baloise merged on 5 December 2025 and based in Basel, has a business volume of about CHF 20bn, 40% of it in Switzerland; the cooperative Patria is its anchor shareholder <Cite id="baloise-completion" /> <Cite id="hb-equity" />.

### Industrials: global capex plays

**ABB** makes electrification products such as switchgear and circuit breakers for buildings, utilities and data centres (Electrification, 51% of 2025 revenue of USD 33.2bn), motors and drives (Motion, 23%) and process automation (Automation, 24%) <Cite id="abb-q4-25-fin" />. Electrification earned about 60% of segment operating profit (our calculation) <Cite id="abb-q4-25-fin" />. ABB is the most geographically balanced of the large caps: the Americas were 37% of revenue (US 29%), Europe 34%, and Asia, the Middle East and Africa 28% (China 11%) (our calculation) <Cite id="abb-q4-25-fin" />. Q3 2026 orders reached a record USD 12.1bn (+40% comparable), with Electrification orders up about 70% on data-centre demand, and an operational EBITA margin of 20.8% <Cite id="abb-q3-26" /> <Cite id="swissinfo-abb-q3" />. It agreed to sell Robotics to SoftBank for USD 5.4bn (closing not confirmed at the cut-off) and to buy the UK flow-control maker Rotork for about GBP 4.1bn <Cite id="softbank-abb" /> <Cite id="vz-rotork" />. It entered the world's top 100 companies by market value in H1 2026 and is 6.9% of the SMI <Cite id="swissinfo-ey-top100" /> <Cite id="ishares-smi" />.

**Schindler** (lifts and escalators: new installations, modernisation and service; CHF 10.9bn of 2025 revenue) earned half its H1 2026 revenue in EMEA, 29% in the Americas and 21% in Asia-Pacific; the Schindler and Bonnard families control 69.5% of the votes <Cite id="schindler-fy25" /> <Cite id="schindler-h126" /> <Cite id="schindler-ar24" />. **Geberit** (concealed cisterns, piping and bathroom products; CHF 3.2bn of sales, an EBITDA margin of 29.4%) is the most European of the large caps, with 29% of sales in Germany and 11% in Switzerland <Cite id="geberit-fy25" /> <Cite id="geberit-ar25" />. **Kühne+Nagel** (sea, air and road freight forwarding and contract logistics; CHF 24.5bn of 2025 net turnover) earns 24% in the US and only 1.5% in Switzerland; it raised its 2026 profit guidance for the third time on 22 October 2026 on an air-freight boom, after founder Klaus-Michael Kühne, whose holding company owns about 53–55%, died on 24 August 2026 <Cite id="kn-ar25-fs" /> <Cite id="bluewin-kn-q3" /> <Cite id="verkehrsrundschau" />. **SGS** (testing, inspection and certification; CHF 6.9bn of 2025 sales, 34% in Asia-Pacific and 33% in Europe) left the SMI in 2022 <Cite id="sgs-fy25" /> <Cite id="sgs-fs25" />.

### Materials: chemicals, cement

**Holcim** sells cement, aggregates and ready-mix concrete (Building Materials) and other building products and systems (Building Solutions). After spinning off its North American business as Amrize on 23 June 2025, its continuing 2025 net sales were CHF 15.7bn, with a recurring EBIT margin of 18.3% <Cite id="holcim-fy25" /> <Cite id="wiki-smi" />. In H1 2026 Europe made 55% of sales, Latin America 22% and Asia, the Middle East and Africa 19% (our calculation); Latin American margins were 30.5% <Cite id="holcim-hy26" />. It keeps buying, including Xella in Europe and Cementos Pacasmayo in Peru in 2026 <Cite id="holcim-hy26" />. **Amrize** (USD 11.8bn of 2025 revenue, almost all in North America) has its primary listing in New York and stays in the SMI <Cite id="amrize-fy25" /> <Cite id="wiki-smi" />.

**Givaudan** creates flavours and fragrances for food, consumer-goods and luxury-perfume companies. Its 2025 sales of CHF 7.5bn split evenly between Fragrance and Beauty (51%) and Taste and Wellbeing (49%), at an EBITDA margin of 23.4% <Cite id="givaudan-fy25" />. Europe, Africa and the Middle East were 42% of sales, Asia-Pacific 24%, North America 23% and Latin America 11% (our calculation) <Cite id="givaudan-fy25" />. Fine fragrance grew 15% in 9M 2026, and the company has raised its dividend for 25 consecutive years <Cite id="icn-givaudan" /> <Cite id="givaudan-fy25" />.

**Sika** makes chemicals for construction (waterproofing, concrete admixtures, sealants, adhesives, flooring and roofing) and for car and industrial manufacturing. Its 2025 sales were CHF 11.2bn, down 4.8% in francs but up 0.6% in local currencies, at an EBITDA margin of 18.4% <Cite id="sika-fy25" />. EMEA was 47% of H1 2026 sales, the Americas 34% and Asia-Pacific 19% (our calculation), and Chinese construction fell by double digits in 2025 <Cite id="sika-hy26" /> <Cite id="sika-fy25" />. Its shares fell 24% in 2025, the worst performance in the SMI <Cite id="cash-2025" />.

### Others: tech and telecoms

**Logitech**, listed on SIX and Nasdaq and reporting in dollars, sells computer and gaming peripherals: gaming was 29% of sales of USD 4.8bn in the year to March 2026, at a non-GAAP operating margin of 18.8% <Cite id="logitech-q4fy26" />. Amazon took 18% of gross sales, and about 35% of production by value is made in its own plant in China <Cite id="logitech-10k" />. It is the SMI's only technology stock. **Swisscom**, 51% owned by the Confederation, bought Vodafone Italia and merged it with Fastweb, so Switzerland is now only about 54% of revenue (our calculation); it left the SMI in September 2026 <Cite id="swisscom-hy26" /> <Cite id="vontobel-swisscom" /> <Cite id="six-review-2026" />.

### Swiss firms listed abroad

Several large companies with Swiss domicile or headquarters are listed only abroad and are absent from Swiss indices. **Chubb**, the Zurich-domiciled P&C insurer listed in New York, earned net income of USD 10.3bn in 2025 and is worth more than Zurich Insurance <Cite id="chubb-q4-25" /> <Cite id="companiesmarketcap" />. **Glencore**, the Baar-based miner and commodity trader listed in London, had 2025 revenue of USD 247.5bn, about five times that of UBS <Cite id="nasdaq-glencore" /> <Cite id="finews-glencore" />. **Garmin** (wearables and navigation devices) dates its releases from Schaffhausen and pays dividends under Swiss company law, and **On** (running shoes, CHF 3.0bn of 2025 sales, 58% in the Americas) is based in Zurich; both are listed in New York <Cite id="garmin-q2-26" /> <Cite id="on-fy25" />. **TE Connectivity** moved its incorporation from Switzerland to Ireland at the start of its 2025 fiscal year <Cite id="te-10q" />. The flavours group **dsm-firmenich**, Swiss-domiciled with its primary listing in Amsterdam, added a SIX listing in May 2026 and joined the SMIM in September <Cite id="swissinfo-ipo" /> <Cite id="six-review-2026" />.

### Owners: families and the state

Anchor shareholders are a defining feature of the Swiss market. ## Concentrated, defensive, high-yield

Owning the Swiss market means owning a few large, defensive, high-payout global companies at a valuation between Europe's and the US's, with returns in foreign currency that depend heavily on the franc. This section compares those characteristics with other major markets.

### Three stocks dominate

Swiss equities are among the most concentrated developed markets. Roche, Novartis and Nestlé make up about 48% of the SMI, 41% of MSCI Switzerland and 38% of the SPI, and the top 10 stocks 82%, 75% and 65% <Cite id="ishares-smi" /> <Cite id="msci-ch-usd" /> <Cite id="ishares-spi" />. The US market is concentrated by its own historical standard, but MSCI USA's top 10 is 38% <Cite id="msci-usa" />.

Concentration has eased slightly: the three giants were more than half of the SMI at the end of 2024, before Nestlé's de-rating and the rise of UBS and ABB <Cite id="swisslife-smi" />. Even so, three company-specific stories, two of them in pharma, decide much of the index return (our view).

### Defensive sectors, little tech

Health care and consumer staples make up 52% of MSCI Switzerland, against 14% of MSCI World and USA and 21% of MSCI Europe; information technology is 0.8%, against 32% of the World index and 40% of the US <Cite id="msci-ch-chf" /> <Cite id="msci-world" /> <Cite id="msci-usa" /> <Cite id="msci-europe" />. In that sense Switzerland is close to a mirror image of the US market (our view). Financials, at 19.9%, are above their World weight but below their European weight <Cite id="msci-ch-chf" />. Including small caps changes little: in the SPI, technology, real estate and materials weigh slightly more, but health care is still about 38% <Cite id="ishares-spi" />.

### Revenue abroad, costs at home

Because most revenue is foreign while part of the cost base, head offices, research and some production, is in francs, a strong franc depresses reported earnings and margins (our interpretation). Company results show the size of the effect: currency cut 5.7 points from Nestlé's 2025 sales growth, 8 points from Roche's H1 2026 sales growth and 6.5 points from Swatch's H1 2026 sales growth <Cite id="nestle-fy25-rtrs" /> <Cite id="roche-hy26" /> <Cite id="swatch-hy26" />. That is why Swiss companies guide and report at constant currency, and why several of the largest, including Novartis, UBS, ABB and Zurich, report in dollars. We found no published estimate of the index's earnings sensitivity to the franc.

### High payouts, slow growth

MSCI Switzerland yielded 2.78% on 30 September 2026, against 1.52% for MSCI World and 1.11% for MSCI USA; MSCI Europe yielded 2.87% <Cite id="msci-ch-eur" /> <Cite id="msci-world" /> <Cite id="msci-usa" /> <Cite id="msci-europe" />. The implied payout ratio, yield times trailing price-earnings ratio, is about 56% in Switzerland, 48% in Europe, 34% for the World and 28% in the US (our calculation). From 2000 to 2024 the SMI price index rose 1.7% a year and the total-return index 4.5% a year, so dividends supplied about two-thirds of the return <Cite id="swisslife-smi" />.

Swiss dividends grow slowly but rarely fall. Payouts rose 0.8% on an underlying basis in 2024 to a record USD 49.0bn, 3.2% of the global top 1,200's dividends, and 85% of Swiss companies raised or held their dividend <Cite id="jhgdi-45" />. Long records are common: Roche has raised its dividend 39 years in a row, Novartis 29 and Givaudan 25, and Nestlé has maintained or raised it for 66 years <Cite id="roche-fy25" /> <Cite id="novartis-fy25" /> <Cite id="givaudan-fy25" /> <Cite id="nestle-fy25-mp" />. Buybacks are lumpy: Swiss companies repurchased USD 31–38bn a year in 2018–22, except USD 65bn in 2021 <Cite id="jhgdi-38" />.

### Priced above Europe, below US

On 30 September 2026 MSCI Switzerland traded at 20.3 times trailing and 17.0 times forward earnings and 4.2 times book value <Cite id="msci-ch-eur" />. On forward earnings that is a 20% premium to MSCI Europe, a 5% discount to MSCI World and a 12% discount to MSCI USA (our calculation). The cyclically adjusted price-earnings ratio (CAPE), which divides price by ten years of inflation-adjusted earnings, was 23.2 for Switzerland, against 35.9 for the US, 35.4 for Japan, 22.9 for Germany, 19.6 for the UK and 19.5 for France <Cite id="siblis-cape" />.

We think the premium to Europe mostly reflects the sector mix: Switzerland has no energy, few banks and many high-return health-care and staples companies, which also explains the high price-to-book ratio. No source we found quantifies a sector-neutral comparison or a long history of the premium, so this is an open question for further work.

### Home bias and anchor owners

Swiss pension funds hold Swiss equities far above Switzerland's 2% weight in world markets. At the end of 2024 a typical fund held 10.5% of assets in Swiss equities and 20.9% in foreign equities <Cite id="cash-ppc" />. UBS's pension-fund data for Q4 2025 show 32% of equity holdings in Switzerland, and the five largest Swiss blue chips at about 16% of the average equity portfolio, against about 10% for the five largest US technology stocks <Cite id="ubs-pk-2026" />. Over May 2007 to December 2025 this home bias did not cost return, according to a UBS simulation <Cite id="ubs-pk-2026" />.

Foreign ownership of the blue chips is high, but current data are missing. The latest figure we found, from February 2019 and of unclear method, put US investors' share of SMI stocks at 40.6% and of Nestlé at 53% <Cite id="bilanz-2019" />. Anchor shareholders (section 5) remove a large part of several companies' shares from the free float.

### Median returns, lower drawdowns

Swiss equities returned 6.8% a year nominal and 4.6% real from 1900 to 2025, with nominal annual volatility of 18.5%; Swiss bonds returned 3.9% and 1.8% <Cite id="pictet-2026-pdf" />. Equities fell in 37 of those years, but Swiss bonds rose in 29 of them, and no 20-year holding period lost money <Cite id="pictet-2026-web" />. Across the countries in the Dimson-Marsh-Staunton (DMS) database, Switzerland's real return over 1900–2019 was exactly the median, below the US and UK and above Japan and Europe <Cite id="cs-giry-2020" />.

Swiss stocks fall less in global crashes. In 2007–09 MSCI Switzerland's largest fall was 52% in dollars, against 58% for MSCI World and 55% for MSCI USA <Cite id="msci-ch-usd" /> <Cite id="msci-usa" />. Over ten years to September 2026, its volatility in euros was 12.0% against 13.4% for the World and 13.3% for Europe, but in dollars the advantage disappears (14.8% against 14.9%) <Cite id="msci-ch-eur" /> <Cite id="msci-europe" /> <Cite id="msci-ch-usd" />. The defensive mix did not protect in the 2022 rate shock (−18.3% against −18.1% for the World, in dollars), when health care and staples de-rated <Cite id="msci-ch-usd" />. Switzerland lags when US technology leads, as in 2016, 2020, 2023 and 2024 (−2.0% against +18.7% in dollars), and over ten years to September 2026 it returned 9.0% a year in dollars against 12.8% for the World <Cite id="msci-ch-usd" />.

### Currency drives foreign returns

For foreign investors the franc has added to returns over the long run. Over 1900–2019 the franc appreciated 0.69% a year in real terms against the dollar, lifting Swiss equities' real return for a US investor to about 5.3% a year <Cite id="cs-giry-2020" />. In single years the effect is large: in 2015, when the SNB removed its floor, MSCI Switzerland returned 11.9% in euros but 0.4% in dollars, and in 2025 the SPI returned 17.8% in francs and 34.7% in dollars <Cite id="msci-ch-eur" /> <Cite id="msci-ch-usd" /> <Cite id="pictet-2026-web" />.

The forces that matter most for Swiss equities are external: US trade and drug-pricing policy, global health-care demand and the electrification investment cycle. Domestic policy matters mainly through UBS's capital, access to the EU and the franc. Several decisions fall due between November 2026 and 2029.

### US trade and drug pricing

US policy now works through prices more than tariffs. Under the December 2025 agreements, Roche's Genentech and Novartis offer some medicines at MFN prices through a government platform, Novartis committed to launch new drugs at comparable prices in high-income countries, and both reaffirmed US investment pledges of USD 50bn (Roche) and USD 23bn (Novartis) <Cite id="wh-mfn" /> <Cite id="bluewin-mfn" />. In return they are exempt from tariffs for three years; the Section 232 exemption runs to 20 January 2029 <Cite id="bluewin-mfn" /> <Cite id="ey-232" />. The deals' terms are largely confidential and mainly affect Medicaid and cash-pay channels, so their effect on net prices is unclear <Cite id="healthaffairs-mfn" />. In our view the long-term risk is a narrower US price premium and a shift of production from Switzerland to the US, while headquarters, research and intellectual property stay.

For other goods, the 12.5% Section 301 cap is below the November 2025 framework rate, but it rests on a unilateral US finding, and a second investigation into overcapacity is open <Cite id="seco-us-trade" />. Watches, machinery and medtech are the most exposed listed goods exporters (our view).

### EU relations after June vote

On 14 June 2026 voters rejected the "No to 10 million Switzerland" initiative, which would have capped the population, by 54.8% to 45.2%; that removed an immediate threat to free movement of people and the bilateral agreements with the EU <Cite id="wiki-ref-2026" />. The Bilaterals III package, signed in March 2026, was approved in broad terms by the Council of States in September, which also voted 23–17 for a mandatory referendum requiring a majority of voters and cantons <Cite id="fdfa-dispatch" /> <Cite id="lenews-eu" /> <Cite id="frontaliere-ref" />. The National Council takes it up in the winter session, and a popular vote before the autumn 2027 elections is possible <Cite id="lenews-eu" />. Switzerland has been fully associated with the EU's Horizon Europe research programme since 1 January 2025 <Cite id="snsf-horizon" />. Medtech, pharma, utilities and companies hiring across borders have most at stake (our view); the package does not restore stock-exchange equivalence.

### Franc strength, zero rates

The SNB has little room: its rate is at zero, it is reluctant to return to negative rates, and large interventions are politically sensitive while Switzerland sits on the US monitoring list <Cite id="snb-zus" /> <Cite id="swissinfo-treasury" />. Markets saw roughly even odds of a rate rise or a hold in December 2026 <Cite id="cnbc-snb" />. In our view the franc would absorb more of the next global risk-off episode than in 2011–15, when the SNB defended a floor. That favours companies with global pricing power and foreign cost bases, and hurts exporters with Swiss costs such as watchmakers and machinery mid-caps.

### Ageing and pension finance

The FSO's reference scenario has the population growing to about 10.5m by 2055, almost entirely through immigration, with the 65-and-over population up about 50% and the number of people aged 65 and over per 100 people of working age rising from 38 to at least 50 <Cite id="swissinfo-pop2055" />. Voters will decide on 29 November 2026 whether to raise the standard VAT rate from 8.1% to 8.5% from 2028 to fund the 13th AHV pension; it would raise about CHF 1.4–1.5bn a year against costs above CHF 4bn <Cite id="vatupdate-ahv" />. Voters rejected a reform of occupational pensions in September 2024 <Cite id="iamexpat-bvg" />. Health-insurance premiums rise about 5% in 2027 <Cite id="swissinfo-premiums" />. Ageing supports global demand for the listed health-care groups, while domestic cost control matters little for their worldwide revenue (our view).

### Innovation and patent cliffs

Switzerland ranked first in WIPO's Global Innovation Index again on 29 September 2026, but listed innovation sits mostly in pharma and industrial technology, not software <Cite id="wipo-gii-pr" />. Novartis faces a two-step patent cliff: in Q1 2026 Entresto sales fell 42%, Promacta 66% and Tasigna 59%, and Cosentyx, Kesimpta and Kisqali lose protection in the early 2030s <Cite id="novartis-q1-26" /> <Cite id="wkzo-novartis" />. Roche's 2026 loss of exclusivity is smaller, about CHF 0.6bn, but a US biosimilar of Xolair has launched, and Roche expects no Perjeta biosimilar before 2027 in the EU or 2028 in the US; Roche targets 20 new molecular entities by 2030 <Cite id="investing-roche-q3" /> <Cite id="investing-roche-h1" />. Both groups increasingly buy early assets from China: Roche's October 2026 deal with Hansoh is worth more than USD 15bn in potential payments <Cite id="morningstar-hansoh" />. Roche's obesity drug petrelintide, developed with Zealand Pharma, enters Phase 3 in H2 2026 <Cite id="biopharm-petrelintide" />.

### Sector cycles to watch

Four industry cycles drive Swiss earnings beyond pharma. Electrification: ABB's Q3 2026 Electrification orders rose about 70%, but its shares fell 5% on doubts about how long data-centre demand lasts <Cite id="swissinfo-abb-q3" />. Luxury: watch exports to China fell 14.2% in September 2026 while the US recovered, and Swatch's margin was 1.7% in H1 2026 <Cite id="fh-sep26" /> <Cite id="swatch-hy26" />. Wealth: global cross-border wealth grew 8.4% to USD 15.7trn in 2025, fastest in Hong Kong and Singapore <Cite id="bcg-wealth" />. Reinsurance: property-catastrophe rates fell about 16% in the 2026 renewals, the steepest fall since the late 1990s, with reinsurance capital at a record USD 800bn <Cite id="artemis-cat" />.

### Tax, climate and geopolitics

Switzerland applies the OECD minimum tax through a domestic top-up tax from 2024 and an income inclusion rule from 2025, has deferred the undertaxed-profits rule indefinitely, and accepted the OECD's "side-by-side" safe harbours in April 2026 <Cite id="ey-pillar2" /> <Cite id="kpmg-pillar2" />. In our view this narrows the tax advantage behind many head-office decisions without removing it. Voters rejected abolishing the issuance stamp duty and reforming withholding tax in 2022, so those capital-market frictions remain <Cite id="wiki-ref-2022" />. A climate law setting net zero by 2050 took effect on 1 January 2025 <Cite id="bafu-netzero" />. Switzerland adopts EU sanctions on Russia partly and with a delay: in May 2026 it took over only the listings of the EU's 20th package <Cite id="baker-sanctions" />. That keeps reputational risk alive for the mostly unlisted commodity traders and for banks. Parliament eased the War Materiel Act in December 2025, a referendum against it succeeded, and Swiss arms exports rose 43% in 2025 to CHF 948m <Cite id="swissinfo-arms-vote" /> <Cite id="swissinfo-arms-exports" />.

## Start with pharma, UBS, the franc

The Swiss market is best understood as a portfolio of global franchises with a Swiss wrapper. The links between economy, industries and listed companies run through a few channels, and they point to clear starting points for further research.

### What makes the market distinct

In our view seven features set Switzerland apart from other developed markets:

- **Extreme concentration** in three companies and two sectors, contained by index caps.
- **A defensive sector mix**, with health care and staples at about half the index and almost no technology or energy.
- **Global revenue and a hard currency**, so that the franc, not Swiss growth, drives reported earnings and foreign investors' returns.
- **High, stable payouts**, with dividends supplying about two-thirds of long-run returns.
- **Concentrated control**, through families, foundations, the state and dual share classes.
- **A disconnect from the domestic economy**: commodity trading, domestic banking, retail and the luxury watch brands with the most value are largely unlisted.
- **Policy risk concentrated in two places**: US trade and drug pricing for the largest weights, and Bern for UBS and EU access.

In our reading, pharma exports, the largest single driver of Swiss GDP growth and the trade surplus, correspond directly to the index's largest weights, so pharma data from customs (monthly) and from companies (quarterly) tell much of the same story. The wealth-management hub supports UBS, Julius Baer and Partners Group, but the cantonal banks and private partnerships that make up much of Swiss banking are not in the index. Head-office economics, built on tax, talent, low rates and stability, keep companies such as Nestlé, ABB, Holcim and Chubb domiciled in Switzerland even though they sell almost nothing at home. The franc and the SNB connect them all: a strong franc lowers reported earnings, raises foreign investors' returns and squeezes exporters with Swiss costs.

### Where to start

We suggest three starting points, without any view on the attractiveness of individual securities. First, the sectors: health care (Roche, Novartis, Lonza, Galderma, Sandoz) explains about 39% of the index and over 40% of goods exports, financials (UBS, Zurich, Swiss Re) another 20%, and Nestlé alone 14%. Second, the characteristics: currency translation, concentration and payout policy matter more for Swiss index returns than for most markets, so constant-currency analysis and dividend sustainability deserve early attention. Third, the data: FOCBS monthly trade statistics, the watch federation's monthly exports, SNB policy assessments and SIX index notices are free, timely and closely tied to the listed companies.

**Data used.** Research notes compiled on 27 October 2026 from official statistics (FSO, FOCBS, SECO, SNB, Eurostat, IMF, ECB, World Bank), exchange and index documents (SIX, MSCI, FTSE Russell, STOXX), iShares ETF holdings, company filings, industry associations, long-run return studies (Pictet; Dimson, Marsh and Staunton) and press reports.

The SMI close and year-to-date change are as of 26 October 2026; market values are aggregator figures of 26–27 October 2026; exchange rates are ECB reference rates of 26 October 2026. Company figures are the latest reported periods, stated in the text.

**Estimates and calculations.** Shares, ratios, regional splits, payout ratios and relative valuations marked "our calculation" were computed from the cited figures. Switzerland's weight in MSCI World and ACWI divides the MSCI Switzerland index value by the parent index value. Patria's stake in Helvetia Baloise is estimated from pre-merger holdings and the exchange ratio. Industry-to-sector mappings are approximate.

**Conflicts resolved.** Where sources disagreed we used the primary or more recent source: Roche's pool at 64.97% of voting bearer shares at end-2025 (the older 45% figure predates the 2021 cancellation of the shares bought back from Novartis); IMF GDP growth for the chart (FSO gives 1.0% rather than 1.1% for 2023); the FFA's 40.7% debt ratio in the text and IMF figures for peer comparison; FSO-based pharma growth contribution of about 40% alongside Interpharma's "almost half"; FH watch export totals as revised in October 2026; the Swiss Medtech 2026 study for the trade surplus (CHF 5.5bn rather than an earlier CHF 5.8bn); SBA's CHF 2,946bn of cross-border wealth for 2025 (BAK's CHF 2,427bn for 2024 uses a different series); Kühne+Nagel's annual-report turnover (CHF 24,476m, not the CHF 24.7bn on a newsroom page); and the Council of States' 29–16 vote on the 90% option (33–10 appears to be the final vote on the bill). For US tariffs we followed SECO and law-firm summaries of the Federal Register texts; the investment pledge is "over five years" per SECO and "by end-2028" in some press reports. The stamp tax is stated as total rates per trade, with the per-side split from the Stamp Duties Act.

**Secondary or unverified figures.** The 90% foreign share of SMI sales (press estimate without method); market values from Simply Wall St and companiesmarketcap (approximate; the latter has no timestamp); SMI year-end levels from a secondary compilation; the SMI record of August 2026 (Trading Economics); the 10-year bond yield after July 2025 (third-party quote); sovereign ratings and the US Treasury monitoring list (secondary compilations); the federal profit tax rate and debt-brake details (Wikipedia); the 2019 foreign-ownership figures (method unclear); the stamp tax per-side split; regional splits for Lonza, Sandoz, Lindt, SGS, Zurich's P&C profit and Partners Group's assets, which come from search extracts of company documents; and ownership descriptions of private companies (Rolex, Patek Philippe, Audemars Piguet, the trading houses, Migros and Coop), which are general knowledge.

**Limitations.** No SIX factsheet with official post-review index weights was accessible, and SIX no longer publishes ICB sector weights. Full 1900–2025 cross-country DMS returns are not public. Several 2026 events (the closing of ABB's Robotics sale, the UBS bill in the National Council, the AT1 appeal and the 29 November 2026 VAT vote) were unresolved at the cut-off.
