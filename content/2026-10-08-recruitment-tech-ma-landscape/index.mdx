## Bottom line: about 3x versus 7x

Recruitment tech is a slow-growth, AI-disrupted niche where scaled systems of record still clear at 6–7x revenue and point tools at about 3–4x. Retention, data ownership and AI-liability hygiene decide which side of that gap a company lands on.

- **Valuations have reset, but unevenly.** The median public software company trades at 4.2x next-twelve-months (NTM) revenue, about 80% below the February 2021 peak of 20.8x <Cite id="cj-2026-10-02" /> <Cite id="cj-2021-02-12" />. Companies growing above 22% still earn 19.4x; those growing below 15%, where most human capital management (HCM) vendors sit, get 3.4x.
- **Listed recruitment is the slowest-growing HR tech segment.** Public talent acquisition (TA) comparables, mostly job boards, marketplaces and screening firms, guide to 1.8% revenue growth for 2026, against 10.2% for payroll and benefits <Cite id="citizens-q2" />. The US hires rate is 3.3%, near decade lows <Cite id="jolts-aug" />.
- **Venture money has returned, but to a few names.** Work tech venture funding reached $6.24bn in 2025, 65% below 2021's $17.9bn, and 17 rounds above $100m took about 56% of it <Cite id="worktech-2025" />. Talent acquisition received $1.22bn <Cite id="worktech-bifurcation" />.
- **Suites are buying the agentic layer.** Workday spent about $2.2bn on Paradox and Sana, and its AI annual recurring revenue (ARR) is about $600m, up more than 200% <Cite id="wday-10q" /> <Cite id="fortune-sana" /> <Cite id="wday-call" />. LinkedIn's agentic hiring products passed a $450m run-rate in April 2026 <Cite id="sia-linkedin" />.
- **Systems of record clear at 6–7x revenue; recruiting assets at about 4x.** Dayforce sold at about 6.6x trailing revenue and Paycor at 6.1x, while Houlihan Lokey's 2026 AI-recruiting comparables sit at 4.0–4.3x <Cite id="hl-2025-review" /> <Cite id="hl-midyear" />.
- **The retention bar often quoted is top quartile, not table stakes.** Median private SaaS at $5–50M ARR runs 84–90% gross revenue retention (GRR) and 101–104% net revenue retention (NRR) <Cite id="highalpha-2025" /> <Cite id="devcorp-benchmarkit" />. "Rule of 60" is not a recognised standard; each 10 points of Rule of 40 adds about 1.0x EV/revenue <Cite id="aventis-rule40" />.
- **AI hiring regulation now has hard dates.** EU AI Act high-risk duties for recruitment AI apply from 2 December 2027, with fines up to €15m or 3% of turnover <Cite id="cooley-omnibus" /> <Cite id="aiact-art99" />. In Mobley v. Workday, claims against a vendor as the employer's agent are proceeding <Cite id="duanemorris-mobley" />.
- **For a $5–10M ARR company, the gap is about 3x versus 7x+ revenue.** Software deals of $20–50M revenue clear at about 3.0x <Cite id="aventis-multiples" />. Growth-led Rule of 40 above 40, NRR above 120% and a motivated strategic buyer move the outcome to the top tier (our judgment).

## Slow market, halved multiples

Recruitment technology is a $4–12bn software niche inside a roughly $46bn HR tech market. Its listed vendors guide to about 2% growth, not the 8% research firms forecast, and trade at about half the payroll multiple.

Gartner measures end-user spend and keeps raising its forecast: from more than $51bn by 2028, to more than $68bn by 2029, to more than $78bn by 2030 <Cite id="gartner-hcm-2024" /> <Cite id="gartner-hcm-2025" /> <Cite id="gartner-hcm-2026" />.

### Hiring stuck at decade lows

US hiring is near decade lows. In August 2026 job openings were 7.08m (4.3%), the hires rate 3.3% and the quits rate 1.9% <Cite id="jolts-aug" />. The Richmond Fed notes that only about 7% of monthly readings since 2000 had a lower hires rate than July's 3.2% <Cite id="richmond-fed" />.

Indeed's postings index stood at 101.0 on 30 June 2026, down 3.7% year on year, with software development at about 73 <Cite id="indeed-snapshot" />. US staffing revenue fell 14%, 12% and 3% across 2023–25, and Staffing Industry Analysts forecasts only 1% growth to $180.2bn in 2026 <Cite id="sia-staffing" />. The Cleveland Fed sees much of the low fluidity as structural, so a cyclical rebound would be partial <Cite id="cleveland-fed" />.

Seat-, requisition- and posting-priced revenue shrinks with hiring volume, which is why public TA comparables guide to 1.8% growth <Cite id="citizens-q2" />. The offset is an AI application flood: LinkedIn receives 11,000 applications a minute, up 45% <Cite id="eweek-linkedin" />, which raises demand for screening and verification.

2021-vintage marks are being written down. Forge data show 2021-vintage private shares trading at a median 59.1% discount to their last primary round <Cite id="tnw-pitchbook" />. SeekOut raised at more than $1.2bn on $25–50M ARR in 2022, then cut 30% of staff in 2024 <Cite id="tc-seekout" />.

## Agents pull hiring into the suites

Agentic AI is pulling the recruiting funnel into the suites. In one quarter, more than 30M candidates used Workday's TA agent and it scheduled more than 8M interviews <Cite id="wday-call" />. Point solutions built on a single funnel step are the main casualties, but the suites' own seat-based pricing is also at risk.

### Bundled steps lose pricing power

Four mid-funnel steps now ship natively in platforms: sourcing search, scheduling, first-round screening and interview notes. LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant went global in English in September 2025, and its agentic products passed a $450m run-rate by April 2026 <Cite id="linkedin-ha" /> <Cite id="sia-linkedin" />. Indeed's Sourcing Assistant searches 370M profiles, and Workable Agent reached general availability on 16 June 2026 <Cite id="indeed-sourcing" /> <Cite id="workable-ga" />.

Bundling is the clearest commoditisation signal. Oracle includes its HCM agents at no additional cost, Dayforce bundles job-description agents, and Workable gives 1,000 free agent credits per paid account <Cite id="oracle-hr-agents" /> <Cite id="outsail-dayforce" /> <Cite id="workable-ga" />. A point tool whose core is one of these steps faces price compression toward zero (our judgment).

Adoption still lags the headlines. Aptitude finds 69% of organisations use AI in TA but only 12% run agentic AI <Cite id="aptitude-agentic" />. Futurum says nearly 9 in 10 buyers source most applications from one suite, and over a third are cutting application count <Cite id="futurum-oracle" />.

Verification is the scarce layer. Gartner expects 1 in 4 candidate profiles to be fake by 2028, and 6% of candidates admit interview fraud <Cite id="gartner-candidates" />. Phenom justified its April 2026 Plum deal on deepfakes and AI-written résumés, and Deel bought Clarity for deepfake detection <Cite id="phenom-plum" /> <Cite id="frenchweb-deel" />.

The defensible layers are identity and liveness checks, validated skills assessment, explainable scoring and owned outcome data. Navagant reports that outcome data has become a diligence item rather than a marketing claim <Cite id="navagant-q3" />.

### Suites' seat pricing is exposed

Agents threaten the suites' own revenue model. HCM vendors bill per employee or per seat, so agents that cut recruiter and HR headcount stall that revenue. Aneel Bhusri, back as Workday CEO since 9 February 2026, framed AI as a bigger transformation than SaaS itself <Cite id="tc-wday-ceo" />.

Workday's answer is Flex Credits, a credit pool metered per completed agent action and priced against labour spend <Cite id="wday-flex" />. Only about 200 customers had signed by mid-2026, and management expects consumption revenue to lag by months <Cite id="wday-call" />. Gartner calls outcome-based pricing more buzz than reality, with only 19% of services buyers using it <Cite id="ciodive-pricing" />.

Pricing architecture shows up directly in retention. Usage-based SaaS posts a median NRR of 108%, against 95% for seat-based <Cite id="devcorp-benchmarkit" />. Juicebox prices agents as seats at $199 a month, Workable charges per candidate processed, and Paraform takes a share of first-year salary <Cite id="juicebox-pricing" /> <Cite id="workable-ga" /> <Cite id="axios-paraform" />.

The big suites have picked different routes. Workday buys agent infrastructure plus funnel capability, SAP bought a whole AI-native applicant tracking system (ATS), Oracle builds everything, and UKG made one frontline tuck-in. Mid-tier TA platforms buy assessment, interview intelligence and frontline conversational AI.

## Buyers price retention and risk

HR tech M&A runs at about 40 deals a quarter, and talent acquisition is the busiest sub-sector, with 132 of 402 deals since Q1 2024 <Cite id="navagant-q3" />. Price discovery is poor, buyers have changed what they measure, and AI liability now gates private equity (PE) investment committees.

### Volume holds; prices stay hidden

PitchBook data via Houlihan Lokey show HCM tech M&A at 329 deals in 2022, 175 in 2024 and 199 in 2025 <Cite id="hl-2025-review" />. Houlihan Lokey's prior deck counted 216 for 2024, so data vintages differ; we use the March 2026 deck <Cite id="hl-2024-review" />. Navagant shows 41 deals in Q1 2026 and 36 in Q2 <Cite id="navagant-q3" />.

Disclosed value is lumpy: more than $19.3bn in 2024, more than $16.2bn in 2025 and $12.1bn to 30 July 2026 <Cite id="hl-midyear" />. Houlihan Lokey describes 2026 as up about 49%, but the arithmetic does not hold: $12.1bn is about 75% of the 2025 total, and annualised it is about $20.7bn, or roughly 28% growth (our calculation).

Price discovery is thin. HrFlow found 19 HR tech deals in Q1 2026 and only one disclosed price <Cite id="hrflow-ma-q1" />. Across all software, H1 2026 deal count fell to 1,165, the lowest since 2021, and strategic buyers took 11 of 13 deals above $1bn in Q2 <Cite id="silverpeak-h1" />.

### Retention ranks ahead of growth

Buyers now rank retention and defensibility ahead of growth. Houlihan Lokey calls AI the primary investment lens for HCM platforms, with diligence on data ownership and agentic return on investment <Cite id="hl-2025-review" />. Vista Point reports buyers prioritising GRR in the 90s and treating the mid-to-high 70s as undermining any claim of entrenchment <Cite id="vistapoint" />.

**GRR above 93% and NRR of 110–120% is a fair premium bar, not a universal one.** High Alpha's upper quartile is 95% GRR and 110–115% NRR, while Benchmarkit's 75th-percentile GRR fell from 95% to 91% over 2022–25 <Cite id="highalpha-2025" /> <Cite id="devcorp-benchmarkit" />. The bar is unfair for SMB-heavy and seat-priced recruiting tools. A recruiting target below 100% NRR may sit at its segment norm, not below it; quote the benchmark that matches its contract value.

### "Rule of 60" is not a standard

No credible benchmark publisher uses a Rule of 50 or Rule of 60 as a formal bar. The working standard is the Rule of 40: each 10 points adds about 1.0x EV/revenue, and companies that pass trade at 4.8x against 2.7x for those that fail <Cite id="aventis-rule40" />.

Only 15% of public SaaS clears 40, with a median of 26% <Cite id="aventis-saas" />. Bessemer's growth-weighted Rule of X (growth times 2–3, plus free-cash-flow margin) explains 62% of multiple variance, against 50% for Rule of 40 <Cite id="bvp-rule-x" />. Meritech finds that at a similar Rule of 40, faster growers trade at 11.4x against 4.2x <Cite id="meritech-pulse" />.

Premium tiers look like this. Bessemer's top decile of public cloud scores about 48%, and Aventis's premium profile is 20%+ growth with a 25%+ free-cash-flow margin. Secondary data put private multiples at 7x for scores of 40–50, 9.5x for 50–60 and 12x+ above 60 <Cite id="saasrise-q1" />, which is the only sense in which "60" is meaningful.

Vanity metrics have lost currency. Investors now exclude pilots without renewal terms, usage without minimum commitments and services from ARR, and expect ARR reconciled to contracts, billing and cash <Cite id="burkland" />. Benchmarkit's median Rule of 40 rose from 15% to 25%, mostly through cost cuts, so buyers now split the score into its parts <Cite id="devcorp-benchmarkit" />.

### Three risks gate PE committees

PE committees price three distinct risks in recruitment tech: cyclical demand, AI substitution and regulatory liability.

On demand, TA's median 2026E EBITDA margin is 25.0%, against 36.5% for core HR, and the TA median multiple drifted from 2.0x 2026E revenue on 11 February to 1.7x on 5 August <Cite id="hl-2025-review" /> <Cite id="hl-midyear" />. On substitution, buyers ask whether an AI-native rival could rebuild the product in 12–24 months, and what the customer loses if it goes dark <Cite id="vistapoint" />. Products embedded in payments, billing or compliance pass; standalone sourcing tools often fail.

Litigation is the sharpest friction. In Mobley v. Workday, a nationwide age-discrimination collective was preliminarily certified in May 2025 and extended to HiredScore users <Cite id="clearinghouse-mobley" />. On 22 June 2026 the court let California FEHA claims proceed on the theory that Workday is directly liable as the employers' agent; it also kept an amended disability claim and dismissed a Title VII race claim <Cite id="duanemorris-mobley" />.

Kistler v. Eightfold, an FCRA class action filed in early 2026 and now in federal court in California, alleges the vendor compiled applicant data without the disclosures consumer-reporting law requires; that theory needs no proof of bias <Cite id="akingump-eightfold" />. By contrast, Harper v. Sirius XM, a claim over an iCIMS-based hiring process, ended in summary judgment for the employer on 30 September 2026 <Cite id="hrdive-siriusxm" />. The real risk is well-funded, vendor-targeted collectives (our judgment).

The EU delay is now law, not a proposal, so a buyer in late 2026 is acquiring a roughly 14-month compliance build <Cite id="lewissilkin-omnibus" />. Warranty insurers are adding AI-specific exclusions, so buyers fall back on AI representations, targeted indemnities, escrows and pre-closing remediation <Cite id="mayerbrown-pe" /> <Cite id="fasken-rwi" />. Colorado's new law voids indemnities that shift a party's own liability, which limits risk transfer to customers <Cite id="mcdermott-colorado" />.

The committee gate therefore has four parts: a feature-by-feature regulatory map, privileged adverse-impact testing, log retention to the strictest regime, and a review of customer liability terms. Privilege matters: in Mobley, internal bias-testing data stayed protected while EEO-1 data had to be produced <Cite id="gunderson-employment" />.

## Playbook: become a needed asset

A $5–10M ARR recruitment-tech company fetches about 3x revenue in a routine process, and 6x or more only from a buyer that needs it <Cite id="aventis-multiples" /> <Cite id="hrflow-ma-q1" />. The job is to become that asset and find that buyer before a suite ships the same feature for free.

### Motivated buyers pay 6x+

A motivated strategic has three things: a named roadmap gap, a customer base to sell into, and a deadline. Workday paid $1.06bn for Paradox to win frontline hiring, which Bersin notes is about 70% of jobs globally <Cite id="hrdive-paradox" />. Phenom bought two assessment firms in 10 weeks, citing Gartner's fake-candidate forecast <Cite id="phenom-plum" />.

Opportunistic buyers price off comparables or salvage. PE add-on buyers anchor on 3–4x medians, and AI labs buy teams, not products, as OpenAI did with Convogo <Cite id="tc-convogo" />. BOLD bought the CareerBuilder and Monster job boards out of Chapter 11 for $28.4m <Cite id="wiki-careerbuilder" />.

A boutique source puts strategic premiums at 25–30% above underlying multiples; treat it as directional <Cite id="windsor-drake" />. Navagant reports that proven AI-native assets are scarce and suites are paying rather than waiting <Cite id="navagant-q3" />. That scarcity is the founder's main lever.

Non-obvious buyers widen the field. Zoom bought BrightHire, Salesforce hired Moonhub's team, and Deel, Payoneer and Intuit bought HR or verification assets <Cite id="zoom-brighthire" /> <Cite id="tc-moonhub" /> <Cite id="frenchweb-deel" /> <Cite id="unleash-remote" />. Korn Ferry paid about $1.1bn for AMS, and Goldenhill expects many credible buyers to be cross-border <Cite id="kornferry-ams" /> <Cite id="goldenhill" />.

For acquirers, the lesson is to buy scarce layers, not commoditised steps: pay for verification, assessment and outcome data, value uncommitted usage as run-rate, and cost EU compliance into the model (our judgment). Upwork screened more than 100 targets and piloted two before buying, a useful template for de-risking <Cite id="tc-upwork" />.

### Checklist: product architecture

- Own an end-to-end workflow an agent can run, such as frontline or regulated-vertical hiring, not one step that suites now bundle free.
- Integrate deeply with Workday, SAP, Oracle, UKG and the major ATSs, and list agents in their marketplaces and agent registries.
- Stay model-agnostic, and review LLM provider terms on price changes, deprecation and usage limits.
- Build fraud defences into the core: identity and liveness checks, AI-use detection and bot filtering.
- Shift pricing from per-recruiter seats to platform fees plus committed usage or outcomes; usage-based NRR runs 13 points higher <Cite id="devcorp-benchmarkit" />.
- Show reasons and a human override on every recommendation, instead of opaque scores applied before human review.

### Checklist: data defensibility

- Capture outcome data (hire, tenure, performance) linked back to screening decisions, because buyers now diligence it.
- Document provenance for every candidate and training dataset; avoid scraped profiles and indefinite retention, which the UK ICO flagged <Cite id="ico-recruitment" />.
- Secure contract rights to use de-identified customer data for model improvement.
- Own validated assessments or skills signals that AI-written applications cannot fake.
- If you run a marketplace, quantify network effects, as Paraform does with 1,000+ hiring companies and $50M+ paid to recruiters <Cite id="pulse2-paraform" />.

### Checklist: financial health

- Hold GRR at or above 90%, and aim for 93%+ to read as top quartile; show cohort trends by segment.
- Reach NRR of 110%+, or 120%+ for premium multiples.
- Score 40+ on the Rule of 40, led by growth, and track Rule of X internally.
- Keep the burn multiple below 1.5x, ideally below 1x, and CAC payback at 14 months or less.
- Reach ARR per employee of $220k+, the top quartile at $5–20M ARR.
- Report gross margin with inference cost fully loaded: 75%+ for SaaS, 55–65% for AI-heavy products with a path upward.
- Build an ARR bridge tied to signed contracts, billing and cash, and show pilots and uncommitted usage separately as run-rate.

### Checklist: AI and regulation

- Map every feature against EU Annex III 4(a), NYC Local Law 144, Colorado SB 26-189, California FEHA and CCPA rules, and Illinois HB 3773.
- Run adverse-impact testing by age, race, sex and disability proxies under legal privilege, and keep NYC bias audits current.
- Retain logs to the strictest regime: six months in the EU, three years in Colorado, four years in California.
- Complete an EU AI Act provider gap analysis and a costed plan for quality management, documentation and conformity assessment before 2 December 2027.
- Rewrite customer contracts: liability caps, employer FCRA certifications and Colorado-compliant indemnities.
- Obtain written carrier confirmation of AI coverage across errors and omissions, cyber and employment practices liability.

### Checklist: deal process

- Build partnerships with likely buyers well before a process, and let them pilot the product.
- Prepare an ARR bridge, contract schedule, 12-month renewal calendar and contract-to-cash reconciliation.
- Demonstrate AI capability live, because overclaiming AI is now a diligence risk.
- Expect AI representations, a special indemnity or escrow and insurance exclusions; pre-size Mobley-type and FCRA exposure.
- Run a competitive process that includes cross-border and non-obvious buyers, from collaboration suites to staffing firms.
- Reset price expectations against 2021 marks: 2021-vintage private shares trade at a 59% median discount <Cite id="tnw-pitchbook" />.
