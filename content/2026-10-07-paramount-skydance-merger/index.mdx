# How the Ellisons won Paramount, then Warner Bros.

## Bottom line: a controller's conflict cost one year, the 2024 election cost another

The Ellison family needed two deals and two years to build what is now Skydance. Act I, buying control of Paramount Global from the Redstone family, took about 20 months from the first reported approach in December 2023 to the close on Aug. 7, 2025. It took that long for two separate reasons, one per year.

**The first year was a negotiation over a conflict of interest.** National Amusements (NAI), the Redstone holding company, cast 77.4% of Paramount's votes but owned 9.7% of its equity <Cite id="paramount-10q-q1-2023" />, and it needed cash. Whoever bought NAI got control without buying the company, so minority holders wanted a say. Skydance refused to let them vote, Shari Redstone ended talks on June 11, 2024, and she returned three weeks later for a higher price for NAI and stronger protection from lawsuits <Cite id="thr-talks-end-2024" /> <Cite id="fortune-revival-2024" />. The deal was signed on July 7, 2024.

**The second year was regulatory, and almost all of it was the FCC.** The antitrust waiting period expired on Aug. 19, 2024, a month after filing <Cite id="s4-2024" />. The transfer of CBS's 28 station licences then sat at the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) for 329 days <Cite id="rbr-fcc-filing-2024" /> <Cite id="fcc-approval-release-2025" />. Until the November 2024 election the docket was routine. After it, three political levers converged on the one approval still outstanding: a revived "news distortion" complaint over a *60 Minutes* edit, Mr Trump's lawsuit against CBS, and the new FCC chairman's demand that companies seeking approval end diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI) programmes <Cite id="thewrap-complaints-reinstated-2025" /> <Cite id="deadline-carr-dei-2025" />.

**It was finished in 23 days.** Paramount, still controlled by Ms Redstone, paid $16m to settle Mr Trump's suit on July 1, 2025 <Cite id="cnn-settlement-2025" />. On July 22 Skydance's general counsel sent FCC chairman Brendan Carr two letters committing to a CBS ombudsman, "viewpoint diversity" and no DEI <Cite id="variety-skydance-letter-2025" />. The FCC approved 2–1 on July 24 <Cite id="fcc-approval-release-2025" />. Mr Carr calls it a standard review; the dissenting commissioner accused Paramount of "cowardly capitulation" <Cite id="investing-carr-defends-2025" /> <Cite id="thr-gomez-dissent-2025" />.

**Act II was faster and costlier.** Paramount Skydance's $31-a-share cash purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD) closed on Oct. 6, 2026, 221 days after signing <Cite id="psky-8k-close-2026" />. No broadcast licences changed hands, so the FCC's only role was a staff-level ruling on Gulf investors (our reading), and the Justice Department closed its review without conditions <Cite id="cp24-fcc-ruling-2026" /> <Cite id="doj-wbd-statement-2026" />. The binding constraint was a 12-state lawsuit, settled with a five-year consent decree <Cite id="ap-states-settle-2026" />. The bill: about $80bn of debt, junk credit ratings and a share price of $9.51 on the first day as Skydance (NYSE: SKYD) <Cite id="variety-skydance-debt-2026" /> <Cite id="variety-skyd-debut-2026" />.

In both acts the federal gate opened once the Ellisons' interests and the administration's lined up; the enforceable conditions came from states, Brussels and London (our judgment). Still open: Delaware suits over the Redstone premium, a CBS News–CNN editorial-independence board due by April 4, 2027, and whether a company levered about 7.8 times can pay down debt on Fitch's schedule <Cite id="deadline-fitch-bb-2026" />.

## The sale took a year because Redstone's control was worth more than her stake

### National Amusements needed cash, and its vote was worth far more than its shares

NAI owned 77.4% of Paramount's voting Class A stock but only 9.7% of the combined Class A and B equity <Cite id="paramount-10q-q1-2023" />. That gap is the whole story of the sale: anyone who bought NAI got Paramount without buying it, which Deadline called the "trojan horse" route <Cite id="deadline-ellison-nai-2023" />.

NAI's need for cash predates the formal sale:

- **Dividend cut.** On May 4, 2023, Paramount cut its quarterly dividend from $0.24 to $0.05, its first cut since 2009, as streaming losses widened to $511m. The stock fell more than 25% <Cite id="cnbc-paramount-q1-2023" /> <Cite id="cnn-paramount-dividend-2023" />. The cut saved Paramount about $500m a year <Cite id="paramount-10k-2023" /> and reduced NAI's income from its stake (our inference).
- **Rescue capital.** In May 2023 NAI sold $125m of preferred equity to BDT & MSD Partners, the firm of Byron Trott and Michael Dell, and used it to repay loans secured by Paramount shares <Cite id="deadline-nai-bdt-2023" />. By July 2023 it was reported to be renegotiating debt with creditors, and in September it sold BDT another $25m of preferred stock <Cite id="defm14c-2025" />.
- **A ready market for control.** In 2023 BDT, which the filings describe as NAI's adviser, found parties interested in NAI-only deals worth about $2–3bn in cash to NAI's owners <Cite id="s4-2024" />.
- **Departing investors.** Berkshire Hathaway sold its whole Paramount stake at a loss; Warren Buffett said in May 2024 that it was "100% my decision" <Cite id="kontan-berkshire-2024" />.

The business behind the stake was shrinking. Revenue held between $25bn and $30bn a year from 2019 to 2024, but adjusted operating income before depreciation and amortisation (OIBDA) more than halved <Cite id="viacomcbs-fy2020-release" /> <Cite id="paramount-10k-2024" />.

The deal itself later showed up in the accounts. In the second quarter of 2024 Paramount wrote off all $5.98bn of goodwill in its cable networks unit, citing in part the company value implied by the Skydance transactions <Cite id="paramount-10q-q2-2024" />.

### A special committee fielded a dozen approaches while the chief executive was pushed out

Paramount's board formed a special committee on Jan. 2, 2024, at NAI's request, to weigh deals involving both NAI and Paramount. Its eight members hired Centerview Partners and Cravath, Swaine & Moore <Cite id="defm14c-2025" /> <Cite id="special-committee-release-2024" />. Skydance's opening bid followed on Jan. 8: merge Skydance into Paramount, invest cash, and buy control of NAI <Cite id="s4-2024" />.

The process ran on two tracks at once (our reading). Only a buyer of NAI could give Ms Redstone a cash exit at a control premium; whole-company bidders needed her consent, and she was reported to oppose private-equity buyers and a studio break-up <Cite id="thewrap-sale-process-2024" />.

- **Warner Bros. Discovery.** Bakish and WBD's David Zaslav first met in December 2023. The committee stopped sharing information in late February 2024 when no concrete offer arrived <Cite id="bnn-wbd-talks-2024" />.
- **Apollo.** It offered $11bn for the studios on March 6. Per TheWrap's reading of the filings, NAI would not have supported a sale at twice that, and Apollo tried seven times to get a foothold <Cite id="thewrap-sale-process-2024" />.
- **Skydance's price.** On March 13 Skydance narrowed its offer for Class B holders to $12–13 a share, with $1.5–2bn of new cash <Cite id="s4-2024" />.
- **Exclusivity.** On April 3 the committee granted Skydance 30 days of exclusive talks, after rejecting an all-cash Apollo offer reported at $26bn <Cite id="broadbandtv-exclusive-2024" />. On April 14 David Ellison complained that due diligence was slow and said Skydance would want more time <Cite id="s4a-2024" />.
- **Boardroom exits.** In late April three committee members and Ms Redstone's longtime lawyer said they would leave the board <Cite id="deadline-board-exits-2024" />. On April 29 Bob Bakish was replaced by an "Office of the CEO" of three division heads. The filing gave no reason; reporting tied the move to the merger talks <Cite id="paramount-8k-bakish-2024" /> <Cite id="newscaststudio-bakish-2024" />.
- **Sony and Apollo.** On May 2 they sent a non-binding $26bn all-cash letter, and Paramount closed 13% higher at $13.86 <Cite id="nbcnews-sony-apollo-2024" /> <Cite id="newscaststudio-sony-apollo-2024" />. TheWrap later reported terms of $28 per Class A and $17 per Class B share plus $1.8bn for NAI; CNBC at the time said NAI would get the same terms as other holders <Cite id="thewrap-sale-process-2024" /> <Cite id="nbcnews-sony-apollo-2024" />. Skydance's exclusivity lapsed on May 3 <Cite id="cnbc-exclusivity-lapse-2024" />.

### Talks collapsed on June 11 over who would carry the litigation risk

The deal broke on one protection for public holders: a majority-of-the-minority vote, which lets shareholders other than the controller approve or block a deal. Delaware courts give a controller deal far more deference when such a vote and an independent committee are both in place (our summary of the legal framework, not a sourced ruling).

- **May 29, 2024.** Skydance's lawyers said it would not accept the vote, offering a 30-day go-shop instead <Cite id="s4-2024" />.
- **June 1.** A Skydance draft dropped the vote and offered a 20-day go-shop <Cite id="s4-2024" />.
- **Overnight on June 11.** The committee's lawyers sent back a draft that restored the vote and removed the go-shop <Cite id="s4a-2025" />.
- **June 11.** As the committee met to vote, NAI said it could not reach "mutually acceptable terms" <Cite id="ap-redstone-ends-talks-2024" />. Paramount fell 7.9% <Cite id="yahoo-para-plummets-2024" /> and soon hit a record low of $9.83 <Cite id="thewrap-para-record-low-2024" />.

Ms Redstone gave no reason. Reports offered three:

- **Lawsuits.** She had tried to build an indemnity plan to shield herself from shareholder suits, was not satisfied, and backed the vote for the same reason; Skydance would not carry that risk <Cite id="cnn-why-redstone-2024" />.
- **Price.** The agreed terms would have paid $2.25bn for NAI, less than she expected because money had been moved to public holders <Cite id="cnn-why-redstone-2024" />.
- **Cold feet.** CNN reported that she was advised by Charles Phillips, a committee member it described as an Ellison foe <Cite id="cnn-why-redstone-2024" />.

Other buyers circled. A group led by producer Steven Paul and backed by John Paul DeJoria reportedly offered more than $2.25bn but less than $3bn for NAI <Cite id="thewrap-steven-paul-2024" />, and Barry Diller's IAC signed confidentiality agreements <Cite id="newscaststudio-diller-2024" />.

Skydance won anyway. The two sides re-engaged in late June, and on July 2 talks picked up again on a higher valuation for NAI and stronger indemnity for the Redstones' company against lawsuits <Cite id="fortune-revival-2024" />. Fortune reported $1.75bn for the Redstones <Cite id="fortune-revival-2024" />. Our reading: $2.25bn in June and $2.4bn at signing are prices for NAI as a whole, and $1.75bn is the family's equity after NAI's obligations. No filing we could read confirms that split.

The committee approved the deal unanimously on July 7 <Cite id="special-committee-release-2024" />. It contains no majority-of-the-minority vote; holders got a 45-day go-shop instead <Cite id="paramount-8k-terms-2024" />. NAI approved it by written consent, so public holders never voted <Cite id="defm14c-2025" />.

### The go-shop called more than 50 parties and drew one bid

The committee's advisers contacted more than 50 possible buyers before the go-shop ended on Aug. 21, 2024 <Cite id="paramount-8k-goshop-2024" />. One bid came:

- **$4.3bn, Aug. 20.** Edgar Bronfman Jr.'s group offered $1.75bn for NAI's equity and $1.5bn for Paramount's balance sheet, and would have covered the $400m break fee owed to Skydance. Class B holders would get no immediate cash <Cite id="axios-bronfman-bid-2024" />.
- **$6bn, Aug. 21.** The revised bid added $3.2bn that could repay debt or buy some Class B shares at $16. Backers included Fortress Investment Group and Jeff Ubben <Cite id="reuters-bronfman-6bn-2024" />. The committee extended the go-shop for Mr Bronfman alone to Sept. 5 <Cite id="paramount-8k-goshop-2024" />, and Skydance protested in writing <Cite id="variety-skydance-goshop-letter-2024" />.
- **Withdrawn, Aug. 26.** Mr Bronfman had raised closer to $5bn when his deadline was pulled forward a day <Cite id="cnbc-bronfman-drops-2024" /> <Cite id="paramount-8k-goshop-end-2024" />.

A later challenger, Project Rise Partners, claimed in October 2024 that the committee had wrongly ignored an $8.5bn offer <Cite id="axios-project-rise-2024" />. The go-shop was a weak test of price (our judgment). NAI was already committed to sell to the Ellisons, any rival needed NAI's cooperation, and a $400m fee stood in the way <Cite id="paramount-8k-terms-2024" />.

The Ellison family and RedBird Capital Partners committed about $8.4bn of equity. More of it went to public shareholders than to the Redstones, and in return the buyers got 100% of the voting stock <Cite id="paramount-skydance-release-2024" />.

- **Who paid.** About $6bn came from the Ellison family and about $2bn from RedBird, Gerry Cardinale's firm <Cite id="axios-ellison-6bn-2024" />.
- **Public holders.** Class A holders could take $23 in cash or 1.5333 new shares; Class B holders $15 or one new share <Cite id="paramount-8k-terms-2024" />. The $15 was a 27% premium to Class B's last close and the $23 a 12% premium for Class A <Cite id="insidearbitrage-skydance-2024" />. Class B cash was capped at about $4.3bn and prorated <Cite id="insidearbitrage-skydance-2024" />.
- **Mechanics.** The investors bought up to 400m new Class B shares at $15 for $6bn: $1.5bn for Paramount and up to $4.5bn to fund the cash elections <Cite id="paramount-8k-ex991-2024" />.
- **Skydance's price.** Skydance was valued at $4.75bn, paid in 317m new shares at $15 <Cite id="paramount-skydance-release-2024" />. Outside investors had valued it at $2.3bn in 2020 and at more than $4bn in 2022 <Cite id="businesswire-skydance-2020" /> <Cite id="deadline-skydance-kkr-2022" />.
- **Control.** The investor group ended with 100% of Class A, 69% of Class B and about 70% of all shares <Cite id="paramount-skydance-release-2024" />. David Ellison holds about 50% of the votes, Larry Ellison 27.5% and RedBird 22.5% <Cite id="thewrap-larry-6bn-2024" />. Forbes, reading the filings, says David Ellison has the votes but his father "controls the purse strings and owns the equity" <Cite id="forbes-ellison-deal-2025" />.
- **Tencent.** The Chinese group was an existing Skydance investor <Cite id="businesswire-skydance-2020" />. Skydance told the FCC it would own under 5% of the merged company, passively <Cite id="thewrap-ellison-carr-2025" />.
- **Protections.** Paramount would owe Skydance $400m if it took a better offer <Cite id="paramount-8k-terms-2024" />.

### Before the election, the docket was routine

- **Antitrust.** Skydance, Lawrence Ellison and a Redstone family trust filed under the Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) Act on July 19, 2024; the waiting period expired on Aug. 19 <Cite id="s4-2024" />. We found no second request.
- **FCC filing.** The parties filed on Aug. 29, 2024, for 28 CBS-owned stations and a few smaller licences; the FCC accepted the filing on Sept. 6 in docket MB 24-275 <Cite id="rbr-fcc-filing-2024" /> <Cite id="fcc-mb-24-275" />.
- **A self-inflicted reset.** On Oct. 30 the applicants amended the filing to show David Ellison as sole manager of the Ellison entities. On Nov. 15 the FCC restarted the pleading cycle: petitions by Dec. 16, oppositions by Jan. 2, 2025, replies by Jan. 13 <Cite id="fcc-da-24-1147" />.

**The news-distortion complaint.** On Oct. 16, 2024, CAR asked the FCC to investigate WCBS over how *60 Minutes* edited an interview with Kamala Harris <Cite id="tvtech-car-complaint-2024" />. As incoming chairman, Mr Carr told Fox News the complaint was "likely to arise" in the merger review <Cite id="imdb-carr-likely-arise-2024" />. The outgoing chair, Jessica Rosenworcel, dismissed it on Jan. 16, 2025, saying the FCC "should not be the president's speech police" <Cite id="npr-rosenworcel-dismissal-2025" />. Mr Carr reinstated it within days of taking over <Cite id="rbr-carr-reinstates-2025" /> <Cite id="thewrap-complaints-reinstated-2025" />.

- CBS gave the FCC the unedited transcript and video on Feb. 3, 2025 <Cite id="variety-cbs-transcript-2025" />. The FCC published them on Feb. 5. Commissioner Anna Gomez said they showed no rule violation <Cite id="cnn-fcc-transcript-2025" />.
- The FCC opened docket MB 25-73 for comment <Cite id="fcc-da-25-113" />. CBS asked it to dismiss the complaint as "blatant interference with free speech" <Cite id="variety-cbs-dismiss-2025" />.
- Two former chairs and three former commissioners from both parties warned that the FCC "seems poised to use the instant proceeding as additional leverage in the merger review process" <Cite id="protectdemocracy-former-chairs-2025" />.

**DEI.** In February 2025 Paramount's co-chief executives dropped numerical hiring goals and DEI pay factors <Cite id="variety-paramount-dei-2025" />. In March Mr Carr said "any businesses that are looking for FCC approval" should "get busy ending" DEI <Cite id="deadline-carr-dei-2025" />.

**Mr Trump's lawsuit.** He sued CBS on Oct. 31, 2024 for $10bn in the Amarillo division of the Northern District of Texas, whose only judge, Matthew Kacsmaryk, he appointed <Cite id="fortune-trump-suit-2024" />. First Amendment experts called it "frivolous and dangerous" <Cite id="cnn-trump-suit-2024" />. In February 2025 he raised the claim to $20bn, added a federal Lanham Act claim and named Paramount as a defendant <Cite id="variety-trump-20bn-2025" />. Mediation began in April <Cite id="usnews-mediation-2025" />. On April 13 he wrote that CBS should "pay a big price" <Cite id="fox5-trump-big-price-2025" />.

**CBS News.** Paramount installed former news president Susan Zirinsky to oversee standards in January 2025, and executives including Ms Redstone asked *60 Minutes* for a list of upcoming Trump stories <Cite id="semafor-redstone-60-minutes-2025" />. Bill Owens quit as executive producer on April 22, saying he would not be allowed to make "independent decisions" <Cite id="deadline-owens-2025" />. Wendy McMahon resigned as head of CBS News on May 19: "the company and I do not agree on the path forward" <Cite id="cnn-mcmahon-2025" />.

### The FCC's informal clock ran out in May, about ten weeks before approval

Mr Carr's line throughout was that the settlement talks had "nothing to do with" the FCC's work. In April 2025 he said the review was "somewhere in the 160s" of its informal 180-day clock <Cite id="imdb-carr-160s-2025" />, and in May he again called the two matters unrelated <Cite id="deadline-carr-may-2025" />.

- **The overrun.** Counted from the Nov. 15 re-notice, day 180 fell on about May 14, 2025. Approval came 71 days later (our estimate). The FCC has not explained the gap.
- **The contract.** The merger agreement's outside date of April 7, 2025 extended automatically, by 90 days at a time and at most twice, while only regulatory approval was missing <Cite id="deadline-extension-terms-2025" />.
- **The deadline.** The first extension started on April 7, the day mediation was reported to begin <Cite id="variety-90day-extension-2025" /> <Cite id="usnews-mediation-2025" />. The second and last started on July 7, giving the parties until early October <Cite id="deadline-second-extension-2025" />.
- **Who it pressed.** A further slip of about three months would have let either side walk away (our judgment). That deadline pressed on Paramount and Skydance, not on the FCC.

## Approval came 23 days after a $16m settlement and two days after a letter on CBS News

### Paramount, not Skydance, settled Trump's suit

The settlement was negotiated under Ms Redstone's board:

- **Price talks.** In May 2025 Paramount offered $15m; Mr Trump's side wanted more than $25m and an apology. On June 25 the mediator proposed $20m, including a $17m donation to his presidential foundation, legal fees and public-service announcements (PSAs) against antisemitism <Cite id="usnews-mediator-20m-2025" />.
- **Terms, July 1, 2025.** $16m including fees, allocated to Mr Trump's future presidential library, with no apology. *60 Minutes* will publish transcripts of interviews with presidential candidates. Paramount said a mediator proposed the terms and its board approved them <Cite id="cnn-settlement-2025" />.
- **Skydance's role.** Paramount had "full discretion" under the merger agreement to settle for up to $50m. It asked Skydance's consent only for the transcript term, and Skydance agreed <Cite id="skydance-senators-response-2025" />.
- **Ms Redstone.** She had recused herself but preferred a deal; she later called settling a "no-brainer" and said she was "blown away" it was only $16m <Cite id="variety-redstone-no-brainer-2025" />. Of *60 Minutes*' coverage she said: "Part of me thought, maybe Trump could accomplish what I never got done" <Cite id="lamag-redstone-2025" />.

The mediator has never been named. Mr Trump received the money and the suit was dismissed with prejudice on July 22 <Cite id="usnews-trump-received-2025" />.

### Skydance put an ombudsman and an end to DEI in writing

David Ellison met Mr Carr on July 15, 2025. Skydance's filing on the meeting promised "unbiased journalism" and editorial decisions reflecting "the varied ideological perspectives of American viewers" <Cite id="variety-ellison-carr-2025" />. On July 22 Skydance's general counsel, Stephanie Kyoko McKinnon, wrote to Mr Carr <Cite id="variety-skydance-letter-2025" /> <Cite id="cnn-skydance-letter-2025" />. ### The 2–1 vote left the news-distortion complaint open

The FCC approved the transfer on July 24, 2025 in order FCC 25-43. Mr Carr and Olivia Trusty, both Republicans, voted for it; Anna Gomez, a Democrat, dissented <Cite id="fcc-approval-release-2025" /> <Cite id="fcc-25-43" />.

- **Carr:** "Americans no longer trust the legacy national news media to report fully, accurately, and fairly. It is time for a change." <Cite id="cbsnews-fcc-approves-2025" />
- **Gomez:** the FCC had "used its vast power to pressure Paramount to broker a private legal settlement" and was "imposing never-before-seen controls over newsroom decisions" <Cite id="thr-gomez-dissent-2025" />.

The approval did not close the CBS news-distortion complaint <Cite id="reason-fcc-decision-2025" />. Mr Carr had earlier said "all options" remained open in that review <Cite id="reuters-carr-all-options-2025" />. The open docket keeps leverage over CBS after the close (our judgment). The companies set the closing date the next day <Cite id="paramount-closing-date-2025" />, and the deal closed on Aug. 7, with David Ellison as chairman and chief executive and Jeff Shell as president <Cite id="paramount-close-release-2025" /> <Cite id="variety-close-2025" />.

### Congress, shareholders and CBS's own staff protested, but none could stop the clock

- **Senators.** Elizabeth Warren, Bernie Sanders and Ron Wyden wrote to Ms Redstone on May 19, 2025 that concessions made "in a quid pro quo arrangement" might break bribery law <Cite id="warren-may-2025" />. Ms Warren called the settlement "bribery in plain sight" on July 2 <Cite id="variety-warren-bribery-2025" />, and the three wrote to David Ellison on July 21–22 <Cite id="wyden-july-2025" />.
- **Colbert.** On July 14 Stephen Colbert called the settlement "a big fat bribe" <Cite id="deadline-colbert-bribe-2025" />. On July 17 CBS said his show would end in May 2026 <Cite id="cnn-colbert-cancel-2025" />, and Mr Trump said: "I absolutely love that Colbert got fired" <Cite id="variety-trump-colbert-2025" />.
- **Delaware.** New York City pension funds sued on Feb. 4, 2025 to pause the deal over a rival offer <Cite id="thewrap-nyc-pension-2025" />. In early March Chancellor Kathaleen McCormick refused to block it, while calling it "a colorable claim that Redstone breached her fiduciary duties" <Cite id="bloomberglaw-mccormick-2025" />. On March 13 the funds dropped their injunction bid in exchange for disclosures, after Skydance alleged that Project Rise had misrepresented its financing <Cite id="bloomberglaw-off-fast-track-2025" /> <Cite id="variety-project-rise-fraud-2025" />.
- **A press-freedom group.** The Freedom of the Press Foundation, a shareholder, threatened a derivative suit if Paramount settled <Cite id="fpf-plan-to-sue-2025" />. We found no record that it filed one in 2025.

Most of the congressional investigation came after the close. Sen. Richard Blumenthal and Sen. Adam Schiff wrote to Mr Carr, and House Democrats Jamie Raskin and Frank Pallone opened a probe on Aug. 20, 2025 <Cite id="investing-carr-defends-2025" /> <Cite id="schiff-letter-2025" /> <Cite id="house-dems-aug-2025" />. None of this pushback had the legal power to delay the FCC (our judgment).

### The Ellisons' ties to Mr Trump are documented; a bargain is not

Larry Ellison, Oracle's co-founder and largest shareholder, supplied the family's money. His links to the President predate the deal and deepened during it:

- **2020.** He hosted a Trump fundraiser at his Rancho Mirage estate, with tickets up to $250,000 <Cite id="slate-ellison-trump-2025" /> <Cite id="revolvingdoor-ellison" />.
- **Jan. 21, 2025.** Six months before the FCC vote, he stood beside Mr Trump at the White House to launch Stargate, a $500bn AI-infrastructure venture in which Oracle builds data centres <Cite id="justsecurity-stargate-2025" />.
- **Jan. 22, 2026.** Oracle took 15% of TikTok's US joint venture, set up under a Trump executive order <Cite id="cnbc-tiktok-jv-2026" />.
- **February 2026.** He moved his main residence to Manalapan, Florida, about 20 minutes from Mar-a-Lago <Cite id="forbes-ellison-florida-2026" />.
- **June 2026.** The Wall Street Journal reported that he gave $45m to a pro-Trump nonprofit during the 2024 campaign. That was not public during the FCC review <Cite id="fortune-ellison-45m-2026" />.

None of this shows a bargain over Paramount, and no source we found documents one. It is the factual base on which critics build the conflict-of-interest case (our reading).

### What the record shows, and what is only alleged

- **Documented:** the sequence. Mediation began in April, the month Mr Carr put the review "somewhere in the 160s" of 180 days; approval came 23 days after the settlement and two days after the letters. Mr Carr had said the complaint was "likely to arise" in the review, and the letters address it <Cite id="imdb-carr-likely-arise-2024" /> <Cite id="variety-skydance-letter-2025" />.
- **Alleged:** that the settlement or a side deal was a price of approval, made by senators, House Democrats, Commissioner Gomez and former FCC officials <Cite id="warren-may-2025" /> <Cite id="house-dems-aug-2025" /> <Cite id="protectdemocracy-former-chairs-2025" />.
- **Denied:** Mr Carr said "the FCC ran a standard review process" <Cite id="investing-carr-defends-2025" />. Skydance says it complied with anti-bribery laws <Cite id="skydance-senators-response-2025" />. Paramount told House Democrats it had only "routine and customary interactions" with officials <Cite id="house-dems-stonewalling-2025" />.
- **Unresolved:** no committee, inspector general or court has made a finding. Democrats, in the minority, have produced letters, not subpoenas (our judgment).

### The hostile bid won on certainty, not on tendered shares

On Dec. 8 Paramount launched a hostile all-cash tender at $30 a share for the whole company, an enterprise value of $108.4bn <Cite id="aljazeera-hostile-bid-2025" />. Over the next twelve weeks it changed the terms, not the shareholders' minds:

- **A backstop doubted.** WBD's board rejected the tender on Dec. 17, saying Paramount had "consistently misled" holders about its family backstop <Cite id="cnbc-wbd-rejects-2025" />. Two days later only 397,252 shares had been tendered <Cite id="sec-tender-shares-2025" />.
- **Larry Ellison's guarantee.** On Dec. 22 Larry Ellison gave an irrevocable personal guarantee of $40.4bn of equity financing, and promised not to revoke the family trust while the deal was pending <Cite id="investing-ellison-guarantee-2025" />. WBD had argued that the trust's backing alone was not enough, "despite the trust holding a majority of the assets of Larry Ellison" <Cite id="variety-ellison-guarantee-2025" />.
- **Courts and proxies.** On Jan. 12, 2026 Paramount sued WBD in Delaware for disclosures and threatened a proxy fight <Cite id="kvia-paramount-sues-2026" />. On Jan. 15 a vice chancellor refused to fast-track the suit <Cite id="thr-zurn-denies-2026" />.
- **Netflix goes all-cash.** On Jan. 20 Netflix switched to all-cash at $27.75 <Cite id="wbd-8k-netflix-cash-2026" />. Peak tenders reached 168.5m shares, about 7% of WBD by our estimate <Cite id="thewrap-tender-extension-2026" />.
- **Paying the exit costs.** On Feb. 10 Paramount added a $0.25-a-quarter ticking fee and offered to pay Netflix's $2.8bn break fee <Cite id="semafor-ticking-fee-2026" />.
- **The winning bid.** On Feb. 24 it raised to $31 in cash, moved the ticking fee forward to start after Sept. 30, 2026, and offered a $7bn reverse termination fee if regulators blocked the deal <Cite id="cnbc-31-bid-2026" />.
- **Netflix declines.** WBD's board called the offer superior on Feb. 26. Netflix declined to match the same day: the deal was "a 'nice to have' at the right price, not a 'must have' at any price" <Cite id="pbs-netflix-walks-2026" />.
- **Signing.** On Feb. 27 WBD ended the Netflix agreement, Paramount paid the $2.8bn fee, and the two boards signed <Cite id="wbd-8k-signing-2026" /> <Cite id="wbd-10q-q1-2026" />.

Mr Trump commented at each turn. He said a Netflix deal "could be a problem" <Cite id="abc13-trump-netflix-2025" /> and on Dec. 10 that it was "imperative that CNN be sold" <Cite id="cnn-trump-cnn-sold-2025" />. On Feb. 21, three days before the $31 bid, he demanded that Netflix fire board member Susan Rice "or pay the consequences" <Cite id="yahoo-trump-rice-2026" />. The Wall Street Journal reported that David Ellison told White House officials he would make "sweeping changes" at CNN <Cite id="politicalwire-cnn-changes-2025" />, and that Larry Ellison called Mr Trump to argue that a Netflix deal "would hurt competition" <Cite id="yahoo-wsj-ellison-2025" />. Those reports are anonymously sourced, and Paramount denies making any promise about CNN: "No commitments from either David or Larry Ellison have been made to any government body … regarding the future of CNN" <Cite id="thewrap-cnn-denial-2026" />.

Certainty and risk transfer won the bidding, not WBD shareholders (our judgment). Few holders tendered and the Delaware suit stalled. What moved WBD's board was the guarantee, the offer to pay Netflix's fee, and a $7bn penalty that put regulatory risk on Paramount.

### Gulf money and a record junk-bond sale paid for it

The $31 offer valued WBD at about $110bn including debt and $81bn of equity <Cite id="nbcnews-wbd-signs-2026" />. Roughly half was paid with new equity and half with new debt (our estimate, from the figures below).

- **Gulf equity.** About $24bn came from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF), Qatar's QIA and Abu Dhabi's L'imad. They gave up board seats and votes, which Paramount said kept the deal outside review by CFIUS, the US foreign-investment committee <Cite id="thr-gulf-funds-2025" /> <Cite id="thewrap-gulf-confirm-2026" />.
- **Ellison equity.** The Lawrence J. Ellison Revocable Trust committed up to $46.72bn of new equity, and RedBird's fund $250m <Cite id="psky-prer14a-2026" />. RedBird added $4bn at the close, $6bn across both deals <Cite id="thr-redbird-4bn-2026" />.
- **Whose money.** Larry Ellison owns about 41% of Oracle. His net worth reached $393bn on Sept. 10, 2025, after a $101bn one-day gain on Oracle shares <Cite id="axios-ellison-richest-2025" />, so his guarantee ultimately rests on Oracle stock; our report on Oracle's Project Jupiter covers the pressures on it: <PublicationRef id="2026-09-26-oracle-project-jupiter-force-majeure" />.

**Justice Department.** It issued a second request on Dec. 23, 2025; the waiting period expired on Feb. 19, 2026 <Cite id="thewrap-hsr-wbd-2026" />. A week earlier, antitrust chief Gail Slater had been forced out after clashing with the attorney general and the White House <Cite id="kvia-slater-fired-2026" />. On June 12 the department closed its investigation without conditions, after reviewing more than 2m documents; it found no likely harm in streaming, linear TV or film <Cite id="doj-wbd-statement-2026" />.

**FCC.** WBD holds no broadcast licences, so no licence transfer was needed (our inference from the record). The question was whether Gulf money could stand behind CBS's licences.

- **The petition.** Paramount applied on April 24, 2026 for foreign ownership of about 49.5% <Cite id="thr-fcc-petition-2026" />. Senators asked for a CFIUS review, warning of "unprecedented influence" by foreign governments <Cite id="warren-cfius-2026" />.
- **The ruling.** On Sept. 17 the FCC's Media Bureau, acting on delegated authority and without a Commission vote, allowed up to 100% indirect, non-voting foreign equity <Cite id="cp24-fcc-ruling-2026" /> <Cite id="thedesk-fcc-bureau-2026" />.
- **The dissent.** Commissioner Gomez said the FCC had let "some of the most repressive governments in the world indirectly control nearly all of a combined Paramount-Warner Bros." <Cite id="cp24-fcc-ruling-2026" />

**Europe and Britain.**

- **EU.** The European Commission cleared the deal on July 22. Paramount must leave United International Pictures (UIP), its European film-distribution venture with Universal, within 13 months, and may not make a new distribution arrangement with Universal in Europe for 10 years <Cite id="paramount-ec-release-2026" />.
- **UK.** The Competition and Markets Authority cleared it in August <Cite id="deadline-cma-2026" />. Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy decided not to intervene after obtaining binding commitments, including editorial independence for Channel 5 News from CBS News and CNN International <Cite id="newscaststudio-uk-2026" />.

**The states.**

- **Suits.** Twelve states led by California sued on July 13 under the Clayton Act, alleging harm in cinema distribution and basic cable <Cite id="lasvegassun-states-sue-2026" />. The Writers Guild sued the next day <Cite id="ktar-wga-suit-2026" />.
- **Freeze.** On July 20 a federal judge in Northern California froze the deal for 14 days <Cite id="kob-tro-2026" />. On July 24 Paramount agreed not to close until five days after a trial ruling or June 1, 2027, whichever came first, with trial set for March 2027 <Cite id="thewrap-standstill-2026" />.
- **Counter-suit.** The Republican attorneys general of Iowa and Montana asked the Supreme Court to declare the deal lawful, arguing that the 12 states had "effectively vetoed" a deal approved by 68 jurisdictions <Cite id="scotus-iowa-v-arizona-2026" /> <Cite id="thewrap-iowa-montana-2026" />.
- **Settlement.** The states settled on Sept. 21 <Cite id="ap-states-settle-2026" />, and the judge approved the consent decree on Sept. 30 <Cite id="cp24-decree-approved-2026" />.
- **Decree terms, for five years:** at least 30 films a year in years one and two and 32 in years three to five; a 45-day cinema window; at least $300m a year of extra US production ($1.5bn in total); separate carriage negotiations for each company's basic cable channels; worker protections; no divestitures. A News Editorial Independence Board for CBS News and CNN must be set up within 180 days <Cite id="ap-states-settle-2026" /> <Cite id="ca-oag-settlement-2026" />.
- **The writers.** The Writers Guild settled for $17.5m to its health plan and a five-year ban on writer layoffs at CBS News <Cite id="thewrap-wga-settles-2026" />. Critics called the decree weak <Cite id="prospect-bonta-2026" />.

The deal closed on Oct. 6, 2026. The company is now Skydance, listed on the NYSE as SKYD, with Mr Ellison as chairman and chief executive and Mr Kreiz as co-chief executive <Cite id="psky-8k-close-2026" /> <Cite id="axios-kreiz-2026" />.

- **The fee.** The ticking fee accrued at $0.00277778 a share a day after Sept. 30, about 1.7 cents in all (our arithmetic; payment not confirmed) <Cite id="psky-8k-close-2026" />.
- **CNN.** Mark Thompson stays as CNN's chief <Cite id="axios-skydance-leadership-2026" />, and Mr Ellison told staff CNN would have "complete editorial independence" <Cite id="cnn-ellison-town-hall-2026" />.
- **Trump.** After the close he called the Ellisons "terrific people" and said "I'm glad they let it go" <Cite id="thewrap-trump-applauds-2026" />. In April, while the deal awaited clearance, David Ellison had hosted a private dinner honouring him, at which Mr Trump predicted "fantastic changes" <Cite id="jewishinsider-dinner-2026" />.
- **Gifts.** ProPublica reported that Paramount gave FCC officials tickets to the December 2025 Kennedy Center Honors, and that Mr Carr sat in a private box with David Ellison. Ethics complaints followed <Cite id="propublica-gala-2026" />.
- **A derivative suit.** A shareholder suit backed by the Freedom of the Press Foundation alleges the Ellisons offered Mr Trump "illegal private benefits", including changes at CNN. Paramount says it complied with the law <Cite id="fpf-derivative-2026" /> <Cite id="newscaststudio-derivative-2026" />. These are allegations.

### Why Act II closed in 221 days, not 396

- **No licence transfer.** Act I needed the FCC to transfer CBS's licences, which gave the agency a decision to withhold. Act II needed only a foreign-ownership ruling, which the Media Bureau issued itself <Cite id="cp24-fcc-ruling-2026" />.
- **Federal agencies did not condition the deal.** The Justice Department closed its review 105 days after signing with no remedy <Cite id="doj-wbd-statement-2026" />.
- **Paramount priced its own delay.** The ticking fee from Sept. 30 and the $7bn reverse fee made a long trial costly for Paramount <Cite id="cnbc-31-bid-2026" />. Settling on behavioural terms within ten weeks was cheaper than waiting for a March 2027 trial (our judgment).
- **The politics were aligned from the start (our judgment).** The administration's demands on CBS had been met in Act I, and Mr Trump publicly favoured the Ellisons in Act II.

What the parallels show (our judgment):

- **Duration was ordinary.** Act I's 396 days sits near the 421-day median of the seven earlier deals. The story is what the review extracted, not how long it took.
- **The FCC needs a licence transfer to have leverage.** Of these deals, only those that moved broadcast licences gave the FCC a decision to hold. AT&T, Disney and Discovery did not need one.
- **Conditions have moved from competition to content.** Comcast's conditions were about rivals' access to programming. Skydance's were about newsroom bias and hiring policy.
- **States now set the real terms.** In the two most recent FCC-touching deals, Nexstar–Tegna and Paramount–WBD, the FCC acted at staff level, and state attorneys general imposed the only US constraints.

- **Delaware Chancery.** Three shareholder class actions over the Act I sale process are pending, and the court wants them coordinated. ## Sources and method

The data cut-off is Oct. 7, 2026, the day after the WBD close. Every figure carries its source where it appears; full details are in the references.

**How the sources were read.** Page fetching was blocked in the research environment, so every source, including SEC filings, FCC documents and court records, was read through search-result excerpts rather than in full. Key primary documents were therefore not read in full: FCC order 25-43 and its ordering clauses, the "Background of the Transactions" section of the S-4 and the DEFM14C information statement, and Skydance's letters to Chairman Carr of July 22, 2025. Paywalled outlets (Bloomberg, the Wall Street Journal, Puck, the New York Times) were seen only through headlines, syndicated copies or second-hand accounts; the references flag these and lower-quality aggregators.

**Conflicts in the notes.** Where first-pass notes and later verification notes differed, we used the later ones. That applies to the Paramount Class B last close ($11.04, not $16.91), the 14-day restraining order in Act II, the EU condition (a Universal distribution venture, not NBCUniversal), and the dates of the FCC filing and complaint reinstatement.

**Estimates and judgment calls**

- **Day counts** are computed from the dates shown. The FCC's 180-day clock is our inference, counted from the Nov. 15, 2024 re-notice; the 71-day overrun depends on that choice.
- **The $1.75bn Redstone payout** is a press figure. Our reading that $2.25bn and $2.4bn are prices for NAI as a whole is an inference.
- **Share prices** are unadjusted closes from aggregators where no exchange data was available. The June 2024 record-low date is approximate; the $20.86 high is probably intraday.
- **Paramount Global's 2023 and 2024 adjusted OIBDA and 2024 debt** are approximate, derived from reported changes or rounded figures.
- **The Oct. 13, 2025 bid** ($23.50) is inferred from a "$1.50 higher" description.
- **Act II equity of about half the financing** rests on low-tier and tertiary sources for the $47bn figure. The Ellison family's actual cash at the close is not disclosed in anything we read.
- **The debt package** figures are preliminary press reports.
- **Comparable-deal durations** use announcement days known only to the month for four deals.
- **Scenario odds, thresholds and the catalyst dates marked approx.** are our judgment.

**People.** Private individuals who appear in the record, including individual plaintiffs and petitioners and a private litigant in a suit against Jeff Shell, are described by role, not named.
